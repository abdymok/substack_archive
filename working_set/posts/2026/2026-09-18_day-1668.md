I’ve been collecting assets around the war and writing about the politics that have grown up around it.

Not quite an archive. Not quite a chronology.

More like a wall.

67 assets, pulled from twelve years and 1,702 posts. Each one is a door back to the day, the argument, the event it came from. Some people will treat it as a wall. Some will walk through. Hit Recompose and it rearranges itself — the old Blogger flipcard, brought back and let loose on a war.

[Image]

This is not a methodology. There will be no framework, no journey, no visual-language ecosystem, and no map of the mind. There is a wall. It has pictures on it. Press the button.

**[Enter ukraine@war →](https://abdymok.github.io/mural/?utm_source=substack&utm_medium=post&utm_campaign=ukraine_at_war_wall)**

Substack builds a branded cover for every post — the picture, the Day number, the subtitle. That card goes out to [x.com/dronedept](https://x.com/dronedept) first. The wall is where it ends up.

This is one collection. There are 24 more in the archive, and 27 assets still waiting to be placed. The wall isn’t finished.

Together they’re less a war archive than an ordinary life with one running through it the whole time — poppies and a cat on a windowsill, then a night in the snow, then riot police. Underneath all of it, the same question, again and again: how does Ukraine survive?

That’s not rhetorical. This is the bloodiest mess Europe has seen since the Second World War, and I’ve been filing from inside it, most mornings, for twelve years.

I don’t want to explain the rest in advance. Play with it.

It’ll keep changing. I’ll come back to it.
