Four men and one woman have been describing a giraffe called Ukraine, and every one of them, in his or her own register, has started to suspect the same uncomfortable thing: it isn’t a giraffe. It’s some large but unspecified number of weasels, standing on each other’s shoulders, wearing a leaky coat.

An American sees the silhouette from across the room and likes what he sees — a long, elegant neck of momentum, arithmetic finally bending the right way, a deal within reach if Trump would just close it. He’s not wrong about the shape. He’s simply never been close enough to notice the coat is buttoned oddly, or that the tail moves independently of everything above it.

The émigré theorist has his hand on the flank and feels it breathing in two rhythms at once, which he takes, correctly, as evidence of something structural rather than something simply tired. He’s right that both nations have built entire cosmologies of dignity around a war that no longer has much to do with its own stated purposes. But a diagnosis of the coat’s psychology is not the same as counting what’s underneath it.

The colonel has his hand on an old wound, and twenty-five years inside the coat means that when he says the plan for war reached the desk in October and needed only a signature that never came, he isn’t guessing at the shape of the animal — he watched a specific weasel decline to move. His register is grief wearing the costume of fury. He’s the only one of us who’s met some of the weasels personally, which makes him precise about the wound and unreliable about the whole.

We’re in there too, holding up the year’s costume against the year’s actual weasel — the Crimea Platform against the disbanded intelligence unit, “nine-megawatt victories” against a 0% interception rate on August the 5th. We don’t accuse the coat of conspiracy. We just note that it keeps confessing, one seam at a time.

And then there’s [Inna](https://zn.ua/ukr/POLITICS/virodzhennja-vladi-jak-zelenskij-dosjah-svojeji-steli-a-sistema-derzhupravlinnja-dna.html), who writes: not a giraffe, never was one, a monarchy of convenience stacked to shoulder height and buttoned tight, and the man at the top of the coat has mistaken loyalty for the ability to actually be a giraffe — has installed weasel after weasel this August alone, each one competent at being a weasel and catastrophically miscast as a neck[1](#footnote-1).

None of us are wrong about our patch of fur. What we share, oddly, is that four of us have stopped being surprised by the confession. Some of this — the appointments, the paralysis, the unsigned defense plan — was always going to be revealed as weasels regardless of who invaded. The war didn’t stack the coat. It just made the thing finally trip in public.

\*\*\*

There's a sixth voice worth adding, though he'd resist standing in the lineup at all — [Roman Bezsmertny](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IDEPp1_dIw8), who read Inna's piece the night before the vote that didn't happen, and who wants to correct our giraffe metaphor at its foundation. He'd say: stop looking for the weasels' names. There is no coat holding a shape, however leaky — there's only the accumulation, decision after decision, of what he calls "the logic of errors," each one making the next one worse, with no structure underneath to blame or fix. Not a conspiracy. Not even, really, a monarchy. Just improvisation with nothing under it, which he insists is the more frightening diagnosis, since a coat full of weasels can at least be unbuttoned. He offers a memory as proof it's been unbuttoned before: two officials, once, standing near him, one telling the other to just work together already, so many problems to solve — and the other answering, essentially, *take the shit off my shoulder first and we'll talk*. The purge happened the next day. Work began. His point isn't nostalgia for that moment, it's that the mechanism existed, will could produce it, and its total absence now is the actual diagnosis — not any single weasel's name. Which is also why he'd push back on the personnel obsession that Inna, and the rest of us, keep circling — the ex-defense minister candidate, he says, was never really about the man himself. By the time the ministry vote failed twice, the name had already become an icon, small-i, a shape the public assigned meaning to independent of the man wearing it.
