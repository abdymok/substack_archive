America is preparing a special holiday.

Tuesday, the President of the United States announced, will be Power Plant Day. Also Bridge Day. All in one. A twofer. There will be nothing like it, he promised — and one believes him, because there has genuinely never been anything quite like this: a sitting American president posting in the early morning hours, signing off with *Praise be to Allah*, and threatening to destroy the civilian infrastructure of a country of ninety million people, the way one might text an ex-girlfriend at 2 a.m., except the ex-girlfriend has no electricity afterward, and the children in the neonatal ward are on backup generators that run for six hours.

> Tuesday will be Power Plant Day, and Bridge Day, all wrapped up in one, in Iran. There will be nothing like it!!! Open the Fuckin’ Strait, you crazy bastards, or you’ll be living in Hell - JUST WATCH! Praise be to Allah.
> — @realDonaldTrump, Truth Social, Apr 05 2026, 8:03 AM ET

One recalls, not so long ago — three years, give or take an eternity — when General Mark Milley, Chairman of the Joint Chiefs of Staff of the United States Armed Forces, stood before the cameras and said of Russia: *indiscriminate and deliberate attacks on civilian targets is a war crime in the international rules of war.* ([Ukraine Defense Contact Group press conference](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=w1lOq6VjDwM), October 2022.)

Russia had been hitting power stations. Bridges. The things that keep the lights on and the old women warm and the dialysis machines cycling. The general was grave. The world nodded. Evil was being named.

We wrote about this as it unfolded — [Day 1005](https://abdymok.substack.com/p/day-1005?utm_source=publication-search), [Day 1006](https://abdymok.substack.com/p/day-1006?utm_source=publication-search), [Day 1017](https://abdymok.substack.com/p/day-1017?utm_source=publication-search), [Day 1149](https://abdymok.substack.com/p/day-1149) — when the pattern was still being explained, when the words still felt like they might matter if repeated often enough and with sufficient clarity.

That was then.

Now the President of the United States would like to do Power Plant Day.

The magnitude of what is being casually proposed deserves the kind of protracted, nauseating reckoning that the human mind resists, because it was not designed to hold it comfortably. We are not talking about a military strike on a radar installation. We are talking about the deliberate erasure of the infrastructure that keeps hospitals running, that pumps water to apartment buildings, that allows a city of millions to remain a city rather than a slow emergency.

People will die — not soldiers, mostly. The elderly man whose oxygen concentrator stops. The premature infant. The woman on the fourteenth floor who cannot carry water up the stairs. The ones who depend on a machine that depends on electricity that depends on the continued existence of a power plant that the President has decided to call a military target because the negotiators on the other side wanted to meet next Tuesday instead of next hour.

And here is the part that should make the room spin: the officers who would carry out these orders — whether they have been sufficiently hollowed out of conscience, whether the steady removal of anyone who raised an ethical concern over the past fourteen months has produced the desired result — this is no longer a theoretical question posed by historians imagining dark futures. It is an operational question.

The institution that was supposed to say no has been carefully, methodically prepared to say yes. This is what the firings were for. This is what the purges were for. History, which has seen this before, is not surprised. History is just tired.

There is a Fox News correspondent named Trey — we have met him before, and again, and again — going back at least to February 20, 2022, when he was standing in a field somewhere in eastern Ukraine, narrating the opening phase of a war he did not understand:

[Video: https://youtu.be/Z5AZ6ilujZE]

He has not changed.

On Sunday morning, Trey called the President and learned that Power Plant Day is coming, that the Kurds may or may not have kept some guns intended for Iranian protesters, and that there is a good chance a deal gets done by tomorrow. He reported all of this as though it were fascinating rather than terrible. There were, he noted, fifteen points. The President sounded optimistic. Trey sounded optimistic. The anchor said: incredible reporting.

Ninety million Iranians were not consulted.

There are moments in history when language begins to fail — not because the events are complex, but because they are simple in a way that resists interpretation. A thing is said plainly. A threat is issued plainly. The machinery that would carry it out stands ready.

No metaphor is required. No parable will improve it.

A day has been named.

Tuesday will come.

The spring in Tehran is said to be beautiful.

*Praise be to Allah*, the President wrote.

One assumes He is taking it under advisement. One assumes He has seen this before. One assumes, even now, He is not entirely sure what happens next.
