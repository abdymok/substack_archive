I realized I was 100 days off on several of last week’s posts—dating them in reverse instead of looking forward. Let me set the record straight: 100 days from now, it will be Day 1549, May 13, 2026. The weather will be warm and sunny, and we’ll be up to all sorts of mischief.

Today’s temperature? About the same as [Anadyr, Chukotka](https://www.bbc.com/weather/2127202)—a lovely Russian city in the Far East where I watched entrepreneurs hawk *limbs* of various forest creatures on winter streets in the early ‘90s. Actual moose and reindeer limbs. The acting mayor then was a Kherson native. Magadan wasn’t warm either. But things thawed in Khabarovsk and Sakhalin.

The coldest place I’ve ever been? Probably Chara in Yakutia. Maybe Chita. Irkutsk, Novosibirsk? I *know* cold. After getting frostbite in Syktyvkar, I finally learned to dress appropriately.

On the Ukraine front, the situation looks almost hopeless. Trump has teamed up with Putin to drag out negotiations to end the war. Talk about talks about more talks. This has been going on for more than one year.

Trump’s special envoys held “productive and constructive” meetings with Putin’s man in Florida Dmitriev over the weekend—because apparently Florida is now the epicenter of Eastern European diplomacy.

Meanwhile, Ukrainian and Russian negotiators might shuffle off to Abu Dhabi for yet another round of talks, though Z himself admitted he’s still waiting to hear from Washington about “further meetings.”

What has all this hot air produced? Fawning reviews of the documentary “Melania.”

Emergency power cuts swept across Ukrainian cities Saturday due to what officials called a “technical malfunction”—funny how these malfunctions keep happening after weeks of Russian strikes systematically destroying Ukraine’s energy grid. Moscow graciously paused bombing Kyiv for a few days at Trump’s “personal request,” then immediately pivoted to targeting logistics networks instead. Temperatures are forecast to hit -30°C in some areas this week.

Bloomberg [reports](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-02-01/zelenskiy-says-no-confirmation-grid-outage-was-a-cyberattack) that insider sources see "little chance of a breakthrough" in ongoing peace talks. Russian and Ukrainian military delegations have been discussing technical details of ceasefire implementation, but territorial issues require "leadership-level decisions"—which is diplobabble for "we're going nowhere." Putin apparently considers Ukraine's cession of all of Donetsk and Luhansk oblasts a "concession" on his part, since Russia illegally annexed the entirety of four oblasts in 2022 despite never actually controlling them. The Kremlin rejected Ukraine's December 2025 proposal for a demilitarized zone in Donetsk region and is now positioning its maximalist territorial demands as moderate compromise.
