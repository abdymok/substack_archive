There is a kind of promotion that is also a verdict. The man who fails upward has been judged, just not as he fears. The system has looked at him and said: yes. This one. He will do what is necessary and remember it differently afterward. A rare talent. Not to be wasted.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Someone baked paska in Kyiv on Sunday. Two paskas. Tulips — pink and yellow. A cat asleep in the sun. In the window, a small wooden tag: Paris. Why Paris, nobody explained.

Z spoke from Saint Sophia Cathedral. He spoke of children’s laughter and the first peaceful Easter that was surely coming. It was moving. The cathedral stands. The children laugh. The paskas were baked.

Also that morning: four hundred drones. And in a courtroom that receives less attention than the cathedral, a man named [Roman Chervinsky](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aKpGNiP7yp0&t=1s) prepared for another session of a trial now in its third year. The charges are fabricated. The people who fabricated them have been promoted. This is not a paradox. It is the system describing itself.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

A retired American general said true things after visiting. Ukrainian forces have made greater incremental gains than the Russians over the past two months. Ukraine’s cruise missile now flies farther and carries a heavier payload than America’s. He was witnessing extraordinary innovation — a smaller force outthinking a larger one. He was right to be astonished.

He did not say why the innovation was necessary.

A country does not become ingenious because it wishes to. The soldiers are extraordinary the way a man pushed from a building is extraordinary for learning, on the way down, to fall correctly. The falling is real. The ingenuity is real. Who did the pushing is a different question. It is not asked at security forums. It was not asked when [Z presented the hardware](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6Br_kdXR-sk&t=11s) yesterday, in what is its own kind of liturgy.

In Belgrade, Oleksandr Lytvynenko is settling in.

He was Ukraine’s chief of foreign intelligence from 2021 to 2024. By presidential decree, he is now ambassador to Serbia. Yesterday he gave his [first interview from Belgrade](https://interfax.com.ua/news/interview/1158835.html). It is a pleasant document.

He discusses Serbian bookshops: forty shelves of English titles for every one in Russian. Encouraging. The Orthodox connection. A future free trade zone. A direct train from Kyiv to Belgrade — someday, once Belgrade–Budapest is sorted. He notes that more than a hundred thousand Russians now live in Serbia, against two to three thousand Ukrainians. Worth noting.

The interview does not mention 2021 to 2024.

During that period, on detailed and sourced accounts that have not been refuted, Lytvynenko disbanded the directorate within foreign intelligence tasked with monitoring Russia. August 2021. Completed by December. His own officers were documenting invasion corridors, force concentrations, timelines. He reported to the president that no invasion was coming. The president said so publicly. The invasion came.

Lytvynenko was then appointed to lead the body whose archives contain the most complete record of what was known, and when.

He is now in Belgrade. The bookshops are well stocked. The train may run someday.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

There is a word for the confession that protects. It is not confession. It is something older, more precise. Perhaps we lost it. Perhaps we never had it.

The man now heading the President’s Office [said recently](https://www.ukrinform.ua/rubric-ato/4111141-budanov-bez-ludej-vijni-ne-vigrautsa-droni-ne-zroblat-ce-za-nas.html) that draft evasion is a terrible problem. That Ukraine has made heroes of both the soldier who bleeds and the man who films himself evading the recruiter. If everyone evades, Ukraine will not remain on the political map. He asked the question and answered it: no, it will not.

This was reported as honesty.

One pauses here and asks a simple question: what have these two men — the one discussing Belgrade bookshops, the one naming the mobilization crisis — actually done to earn the platforms from which they now speak? The answer, if one consults the record rather than the title, is clarifying. The platform is not the reward for the achievement. The platform is the achievement. This is how the system pays its debts.

This is the same man who, in 2020, denied on the record that the Wagner operation had taken place — a claim demolished fifteen months later by hundreds of documents. The same man who, the morning Navalny died, told the public the opposition leader had not been poisoned. The same man whose directorate prepared daily intelligence briefings that should have named invasion routes. Did not name them.

When this man names the crack, he is not outside the wall. He is the wall. The alarm is raised so it can substitute for repair. The crack is acknowledged so it need not be fixed. This is not honesty. It is honesty’s uniform, worn by someone else.

The divining rod and the icon are held by the same hand. The hand now sits at the head of the President’s Office.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

In the courtroom, the trial continues.

Chervinsky’s account — of who disbanded what, who reported what to whom, who was promoted, whose names were passed to foreign prosecutors through a man later designated by the U.S. Treasury as a Russian agent — is public record. It has not been answered. It will not be. Answering would require the system to look at itself from outside itself. It was built to prevent this.

The general admires the soldiers. He is right. The cathedral held the ceremony. The ceremony was not false. The ambassador discusses bookshops. The bookshops are real. The official names the crisis. The crisis is real.

Everything is real. That is the difficulty.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

The tulips are still on the table. The cat likes to nibble on them. The small wooden tag still says Paris, in a city where four hundred drones came one morning, and a man goes to court for the third year for the crime of having been right.

The tomb was sealed by someone. It is worth knowing who. And where they are now. And what pleasant interviews they are giving. And what they choose not to mention.
