Russia is attacking Kyiv at the moment, so I will be brief. The text and images in this post may appear disjointed, perhaps bizarre.

As the rockets and drones and bombs came whistling in this morning I learned there exists a fortuneteller in Kyiv, a woman of fifty-one years, called Veronika Anikiyevich, though she prefers the title “astrology consultant”—which possesses a certain dignity that “fortuneteller” lacks, the way a man might insist he is a “beverage historian” rather than merely a drunkard. Her contact list presents her more frankly as “Veronika Fenshui ([Feng shui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feng_shui)?) Office” and “Veronika Anikiyevich/Fortuneteller Yermak.” She runs a Telegram channel called “Lunar Hours” and, since 2024, operates as a registered sole proprietor offering “other individual services”—a classification so vague it could encompass anything from tarot readings to the rewriting of history itself.

Her Russian father, named Fedor Anikiyevich, once headed some provincial political organization in a town called Henichsk, and one suspects the apple did not fall far from the tree, though it rolled in a different direction entirely. He is a Russian citizen who, judging by recent photographs, still resides there, posing with Russian flags and St. George ribbons.

This Veronika possessed a most curious talent: she could read the heavens like other people read newspapers. Her educational credentials—financial analysis and management from Kherson State Technical University—suggest she was once ambitious in more conventional ways. But apparently, a certain gentleman named Andriy Yermak—Z’s righthand man, the second most important politician in the country—would send her the birth dates of people he wished to appoint to important positions, much as a housewife might send a servant to market with a list of vegetables, asking only that they be fresh and properly portioned by the zodiac.

Yesterday prosecutors claimed—and here we must pause, as one does when hearing such things—that Yermak appointed a prosecutor general, a health minister, a deputy chief of the presidential office, and the head of state administration, all according to this woman’s celestial calculations. He would send her birthdates and ask for advice, the way medieval kings consulted astrologers before battle. One imagines the conversation: “But surely,” says the rational man, “you cannot run a government by the stars?” To which the fortuneteller presumably replied: “Why not? You are running it by something.”

When journalists from [Radio Free Europe](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ng2x2eXg89o&t=943s) came to ask her about this arrangement, she hung up the telephone. She called the investigators “scoundrels” and the journalists “jackals” on social media, suggesting the entire affair was a “Trump-Putin special operation for Ukraine’s capitulation”—which suggests she understood perfectly well what was being said about her, and preferred not to elaborate. The rational world has its limits; beyond them lies only silence and contempt for those who ask impertinent questions.
