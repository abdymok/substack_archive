Fedorov’s [exit briefing](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=g-7UA-eDDuo) names everyone except the one person who matters.

Asked how many foreign governments called about his dismissal, he doesn’t answer. He tells a story instead: Pistorius as a new Churchill, Karp calling with urgency, Fedorov politely deferring. Whether it happened that way is beside the point. The names do the boasting. A phone call stands in for an answer.

Twenty minutes later, same move with sharper edges. Who bought Trukha? Who’s behind the Telegram pile-on? Thrown into the room, never resolved. He built that ecosystem himself in 2019. Telegram was a campaign weapon before it turned on him. He noticed it only when it became personal.

Outside, near Franko, people did what they do here when institutions don’t hold: they showed up. But read the signs closely and the target isn’t Fedorov’s reinstatement — it’s commander-in-chief Syrsky. The last cardboard protest was aimed at a policy nobody could put a face to. This one has a face, and it isn’t the man who lost his job. It’s the general who kept his.

Fedorov himself was a reshuffle accident — one draw in a chain no one planned. The same lottery removed Zaluzhny, with no protest at all. Good draw, bad draw. Same dice.

[Image]

Fifty-five minutes of questions, and no one asked the one that mattered: who is Vova.

Not an abstraction. A man who, with friends, has been building a mansion complex outside Kyiv — before the invasion, during it, after it. The referent behind last year’s “golden toilet mafia.” Others get named to their faces. Vova gets the unwritten rule: investigate the court, never the king, especially while the house is still going up.

Call it what [Day 1546](https://abdymok.substack.com/p/day-1546-c5b) called it: two wars running in parallel, sometimes in the same room. Abroad, this is filed as institutional churn — a reformer, a general, a system finding its footing. The version outsiders need.

The mechanism is simpler. A minister, an apartment, a stranger who turns out to own half the defense economy, dinner over veal, no minutes taken. Different address, same blueprint. The mansion outside Kyiv and the apartment on Hrushevskoho are the same structure at different heights.

Small footnote, same calendar: [Halushchenko moved into the Justice Ministry — the seat that decides who gets prosecuted — on July 17, 2025. Not because of scandal. He was already in the chair months before anyone was raided](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Galushchenko).

The forecasting question didn’t stay rhetorical for long.

Last night Vova named Yevhen Khmara, acting head of the Security Service, acting defense minister. Not a politician. The justification was his record — long-range strikes, Alpha, High-T. Then the phrase that matters: control over the internal situation. Control is the point. Internal is the tell.

Parliament will confirm him later, after “procedures.” In the meantime, “acting” governs. A familiar maneuver — widely understood, rarely stopped.

By morning Andy [runs the reshuffle](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/07/16/world/europe/ukraine-military-defense-minister-drones.html), quotes the line: the situation is what it is, someone has to choose. What it doesn’t run is why the choice sits where it does, or what “internal intelligence” means in practice — the Security Service, and within it, Alpha. Corruption appears once, sourced to Fedorov, as contractor complaints. It isn’t followed up the org chart to the mansion complex outside Kyiv.

So the next minister wasn’t drawn.

He was installed. Same evening. From the one institution built to watch everyone else.
