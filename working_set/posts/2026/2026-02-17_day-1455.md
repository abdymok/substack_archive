*On Napoleon cakes, fitness-studio snipers and a capitulation with custard on top.*

[Image]

*Photo c/o abdymok (February 17, 2026)*

On Valentine’s Day morning in Kyiv, the mother of a woman known online as KonMari baked two Napoleon cakes. The first, full-sized, was for us. The second — smaller, a gesture of winter warmth toward a guest — was for a young man with nice muscles who was rumored to be a sniper with the Armed Forces of Ukraine, apparently toning those muscles at her downtown fitness studio. It should be noted that she baked both cakes with equal care, because that is the kind of woman she is, and also because a Napoleon cake, if it is worth making at all, is worth making properly, regardless of the recipient’s relationship to the truth.

The extended family had been laughing about this young man for months, and for two reasons, not one. The first: snipers do not spend their Valentine’s Day mornings in downtown Kyiv working on their physique. The second, more fundamental, and this is the kind of thing that separates those who understand war from those who merely discuss it at fitness studios: an actual Ukrainian army sniper would never, under any circumstances, tell a fitness instructor that he is a Ukrainian army sniper. If the young man with the nice muscles had somehow learned that people were calling him a sniper, he would have switched fitness studios immediately or killed the rumor on the spot — because in this war, operational security is not a bureaucratic courtesy but the precise distance between a man and his grave. The fact that the rumor circulated freely, warmly, cheerfully, all the way to a Valentine’s Day Napoleon cake, tells you everything about its relationship to reality. The family’s actual breadwinner — an officer who has been fighting on the zero line in eastern Ukraine since early 2022 — has never discussed what he does with anyone at a fitness studio. He did not receive a Napoleon cake, or a Valentine’s Day card, or indeed any of the pleasant accessories of a Kyiv winter Sunday. He received the zero line, which is its own kind of education.

This small domestic scene — the two cakes, the young man with the muscles, the officer who is elsewhere — contains, if one looks at it carefully and without sentimentality, everything that is wrong with how this war is being discussed, managed, and now supposedly ended.

✦

Standing beside Viktor Orbán in Budapest — and one must pause here to appreciate the choice of companion, a man whose relationship to Ukrainian sovereignty resembles the young man’s relationship to military service — Secretary [Rubio framed American abdication as American leadership](https://www.state.gov/releases/office-of-the-spokesperson/2026/02/secretary-of-state-marco-rubioand-hungarian-prime-minister-viktor-orban-at-a-joint-press-availability).

> *“We’re the only nation on Earth that apparently can get both sides to the table to talk,” he declared, with the quiet satisfaction of a man who has confused the act of pushing someone toward a door with the act of opening it.*

The United States did not bring Russia to the table. It handed Russia the table, expressed admiration for the craftsmanship, and walked away from ours.

Trump had promised, more than eighty times on the campaign trail, to end the war in a single day. We are now deep into year four, which suggests either that the promise was somewhat optimistic or that the single day in question has not yet arrived. The [Kiel Institute data](https://www.ifw-kiel.de/topics/war-against-ukraine/ukraine-support-tracker/) is unsparing on the consequences: American withdrawal meant total military aid to Ukraine in 2025 ran 13 percent below the 2022–2024 average. Europe heroically surged 67 percent to compensate, because Europe, whatever its many faults, was paying attention. But the gap could not be fully closed. People died into that gap. Real soldiers, not fitness-studio snipers.

Which brings us to [Trump’s Air Force One remarks](https://rollcall.com/factbase/trump/transcript/donald-trump-press-gaggle-air-force-one-february-16-2026/), delivered the day after Valentine’s Day with the breezy authority of a man who has never personally experienced the zero line.

> *“Ukraine better come to the table fast, it’s all I’m telling you,” he said — about the victim, it must be noted, not the aggressor, a distinction that apparently no longer commands the attention it once did in Washington.*

Meanwhile [Rubio expressed bewilderment](https://www.state.gov/releases/office-of-the-spokesperson/2026/02/secretary-of-state-marco-rubioand-hungarian-prime-minister-viktor-orban-at-a-joint-press-availability) that anyone would “criticize a President trying to end a war,” as though the criticism were of the goal rather than the method — as though a doctor who prescribed the wrong medicine to the wrong patient deserved applause simply for having entered the room. The criticism is of the method: cut support to the victim, legitimize the aggressor’s territorial gains, conduct the diplomacy from the capital of NATO’s most enthusiastic Kremlin apologist, and present the resulting document as a peace agreement.

Some European analysts, surveying this situation with the philosophical detachment available only to those not currently living in Kyiv, counsel patience. Outlast Trump, they suggest, the way one outlasts a pandemic — wait for immunity to develop, return to normal, open the bars again, order the pumpkin latte. It is a tempting prescription, and it has the considerable advantage of requiring nothing. But the old normal, one suspects, is not waiting. The retreat from Atlantic solidarity may be less Trump’s personal eccentricity than a genuine shift in American mass consciousness — a shift that subsequent administrations may soften, as a good custard softens a brittle pastry layer, but cannot reverse. On this diagnosis, the analysts are probably correct. On the therapy — patience, watchful waiting, the strategic doing of nothing — they are as mistaken as a doctor who charges sixty sheep per day while sleeping under a walnut tree.

✦

The Napoleon cake, by the way, tastes better on day two. The cream penetrates the layers overnight, softening what was brittle into something that holds together under its own weight. It is patient work, but the patience must be preceded by the work — someone must have rolled the layers thin, baked them one by one, prepared the custard, assembled the whole careful edifice. It does not improve on its own simply by being left alone.

There were two cakes on Valentine’s Day morning. One for us — full-sized, the real thing, made with butter and time and the knowledge that it would be eaten by people who would appreciate it. One smaller one for the young man who was rumored to be a sniper and who, if he ever heard that rumor, certainly took no steps to discourage it. [Witkoff and Kushner flew to Geneva](https://rollcall.com/factbase/trump/transcript/donald-trump-press-gaggle-air-force-one-february-16-2026/) the following day, carrying terms that nobody on the zero line had been consulted about, which is perhaps appropriate, since the zero line is not currently accessible by private jet. And in a Carmelite monastery in Budapest, [Rubio assured Orbán](https://www.state.gov/releases/office-of-the-spokesperson/2026/02/secretary-of-state-marco-rubioand-hungarian-prime-minister-viktor-orban-at-a-joint-press-availability) that this — all of this — is what ending a war looks like.

*It is a capitulation with custard on top. One cake for those doing the fighting. A smaller, more decorative one for those who merely claim to — and who, if they ever suspected people had noticed the difference, would have long since found somewhere else to be.*

**Letters to the Editor** *Foreign Affairs*

**The Border They Cannot See**

Slezkine and Shifrinson have written [a careful article](https://archive.ph/dlzn1) about a country that does not exist.

Their argument is internally coherent. De jure recognition of Russia’s territorial gains would clarify tripwires, attract investment, ease EU accession, and reduce escalation risk. The logic is sound. The premise is not.

The premise is Ukraine as rational unitary actor — a government capable of correctly assessing strategic tradeoffs, negotiating binding commitments, and administering a credible postwar state behind a newly recognised line. This Ukraine appears regularly in [Foreign Affairs](https://www.foreignaffairs.com/russia/price-peace-ukraine). It does not appear in Kyiv.

Consider the evidentiary record the authors decline to consult. On January 19, 2022 — thirty-six days before the full-scale invasion, after explicit warnings from the CIA, MI6, and Ukrainian military intelligence had been delivered repeatedly — President Z told the nation to plan its Easter celebrations, its May holidays, its spring barbecues. A leader who could not process unambiguous invasion intelligence at thirty-six days’ remove is not a leader who will correctly model the consequences of de jure border recognition or credibly commit to anything Brussels or Moscow requires.

The morning after Z’s Munich performance, Ukraine’s National Anti-Corruption Bureau detained the former Energy Minister at the border. On a train. The formal suspicion notice — including a schematic of the financial architecture — is [publicly available](https://nabu.gov.ua/news/sprava-midas-pidozriuyet-sia-eksministr-energetyky/) and worth reading carefully.

> In February 2021, a fund was registered on Anguilla by members of a criminal organisation operating inside the energy ministry, headed by a professional money launderer from the Seychelles. Shell companies in the Marshall Islands named the minister’s ex-wife and four children as beneficiaries. Over \$112 million in cash from criminal activity in the energy sector was laundered through three Swiss banks. The children’s tuition at prestigious Swiss schools was paid from these accounts.

The fund on Anguilla—located approximately 9,000 kilometers from Kyiv—was registered thirteen months *before* the invasion — while the West was already funnelling support premised on Ukraine being a reforming democracy worth defending.

The authors argue that legal certainty over borders would attract the private investment essential for reconstruction. But the uncertainty deterring capital is not primarily about where the eastern border sits. It is about whether the minister overseeing the energy sector your capital would flow into was simultaneously running Marshall Islands shells into Swiss accounts through a man called Rocket. The border is not the variable. The Professor is the variable.

A correct prescription administered to a patient whose institutions have been redirected into loyalty networks does not produce the expected outcome. Slezkine and Shifrinson have identified the right border. They have simply written the wrong country into the treaty.
