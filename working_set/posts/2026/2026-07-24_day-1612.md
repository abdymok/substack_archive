**Yesterday I wrote about Laura Loomer.**

*The hot-girls-wear-sunglasses-and-eat-shashlyk part of today’s post follows. The* [story](https://puck.news/laura-loomers-stunning-reversal-on-ukraine/?utm_source=chatgpt.com) *has, predictably, been covered by* [The Financial Times](https://archive.ph/QYzhM?utm_source=chatgpt.com) *as well, although* [Caolan](https://www.facebook.com/CaolanRobertson/?utm_source=chatgpt.com) *hasn't touched it yet.*

Today, I [read](https://x.com/petereharrell/status/2080365980841038103) that her conversion to the Ukrainian cause is “a powerful reminder of the importance of being open to engaging with people you might profoundly disagree with today.”

This is probably true.

It is also exactly the sort of thing I would now like to ask the [archive](https://notebooklm.google.com/notebook/0c30d4e5-3bba-4d3c-a488-01ee2444e3bd) about.

What did I write about Laura before she came around on Ukraine?

The answer, as it turns out, is nothing.

I had never written about Laura Loomer.

But I had written quite a lot about people who changed their minds—and about the difference between a change of position and a change of heart.

The archive reminded me that I had described *ukraine@war* as containing “wrong calls, dead ends, and positions later revised,” arguing that this is the only form of **epistemic accountability available to a writer**: leaving the record of what you once believed where somebody can find it later.

I contrasted this with institutional analysts who “systematically convert prior errors into subsequent authority.”

Ask the archive about the author of the *Financial Times* piece about Loomer.

It will bring him up.

Not because I wrote about Laura.

I didn’t.

But because I wrote about Miller’s earlier reporting, his later admissions about “the signs we missed,” and what I called his “autohagiography.”

The archive, in other words, had been thinking about Laura Loomer before I had ever heard of Laura Loomer.

Or rather: it had been thinking about the problem she now represents.

**When somebody changes a position, what exactly has changed?**

The conclusion?

The evidence?

The people they trust?

The political incentives?

**KonMari will sort everything out, eventually.**

Will Z visit America next week? I don’t know. After chatting up Laura, anything is possible.
