Pots of gold and the ends of rainbows have traditionally been regarded as imaginary destinations, suitable for children, poets, and other persons not yet initiated into the practical affairs of life. The modern investment community has improved upon this arrangement. It has discovered that a rainbow need not terminate in folklore. Given a sufficient quantity of Russian cruise missiles, Ukrainian drone sorties, Pentagon procurement officers, and speculative capital, the phenomenon may be made to terminate quite satisfactorily in a brokerage account. The old Irish peasant looked for his treasure at the end of a rainbow arching over a green field. His latter-day successor looks for it at the end of a rainbow arching over a smoking oil depot somewhere beyond Rostov.

The circumstance is not without its instructive features. The drone, having first converted fuel depots, command posts, and assorted military assets into debris, proceeds through the accustomed channels of modern finance to convert debris into revenue, revenue into earnings expectations, and earnings expectations into capital gains. The resulting increase in shareholder wealth is then received as evidence of industrial foresight and technological progress. By this ingenious mechanism the pecuniary culture succeeds in extracting a pot of gold from the end of a rainbow illuminated by distant fires, while preserving the agreeable conviction that the transaction is not merely profitable but morally elevating. Those who arrive first at the treasure naturally attribute their success to superior judgment and, with becoming magnanimity, invite the public to admire the view.
