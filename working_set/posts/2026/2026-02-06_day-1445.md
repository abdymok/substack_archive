Running water, electricity and heat at the apartment again this morning. I played around with the Xaomi hot water pump on Wi-Fi and listened to a podcast with Anne and Preet, titled *America is in Rupture Under Trump*[2](#footnote-2)**.**

Near the end, Anne said she has “a lot of sympathy with the people of the 1990s, because they had experiences that led them to believe that economic ties would lead to better political ties, and they thought that if you connected the world economically you would have a more peaceful world because no one would want to go to war with their trading partners… That was kind of the dominant theory of the 1990s”—globalization as a pathway to democracy.

She’s right to defend that generation’s reasoning, even if the outcome disappointed. The premise wasn’t naive: interdependence *does* raise the cost of conflict. What failed wasn’t the theory but the execution—the refusal to enforce conditionality, the tolerance for kleptocracy dressed as market reform, the unwillingness to defend the rules that made openness possible.

Now an American president has finished what autocrats started. Anne apologizes—as an American?—for the economic chaos Trump has unleashed on Europe and the world. But the damage isn’t just economic uncertainty. Trump has **obliterated** the foundational principles of the postwar order: Most Favoured Nation status, the rules-based trading system, international economic law, the very idea that agreements matter. What took eighty years to build—imperfectly, yes, but deliberately—has been gutted in eight years of contempt for institutions, allies, and the notion that open societies require defense, not just declaration.

The 1990s generation believed connectivity would make war costly. They were right. What they underestimated was how profitable dismantling that connectivity could be—for strongmen, for opportunists, for anyone who confused the *benefits* of the open system with permission to loot it. Trump didn’t inherit a failed experiment. He **destroyed a functioning one**, and what comes next won’t be a more peaceful multipolar world. It will be a return to the logic of blocs, spheres, and the kind of transactional brutality that globalization was supposed to make obsolete.

The principles of open society haven’t failed. They’ve been **abandoned**—by those who benefited most from them.
