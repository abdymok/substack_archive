*In the old parable, a group of blind men come upon an elephant, and each, touching only the part within reach, describes it faithfully. One feels the trunk and reports a snake. One feels the leg and reports a tree. One feels the ear and reports a fan. Each description is accurate. None of them is the elephant.*

It is a delicate craft, narrating a small nation’s war against a large one, and like all delicate crafts it rewards specialization[1](#footnote-1). The specialist learns early which parts of the animal are within reach, and reports those faithfully, at length, with real eloquence — and learns, just as early, which parts to leave for someone else’s hands. A man who stayed in his capital while missiles fell — that is the trunk, and a fine trunk it is, worth describing twice. A man’s approval rating in the weeks before that, hovering near the floor, for reasons having nothing to do with the missiles — that is somewhere further back, past the shoulder, and nobody’s hand happens to wander there.

One of our blind men is, at least, honest about his method. Before he even begins, he announces it: he is “*quite deliberately bracketing*“ the ethics and the law of the thing, he says, and will speak only of power. A reasonable enough discipline, and an admirable bit of housekeeping — name the part you’re not touching, so no one mistakes the trunk for the whole animal. He does this for the war he is angriest about. For the other war, the one he loves, no such announcement is made. The bracketing happens anyway — governance, patronage, the small mountain of birthdays and helicopters — but silently, as if the category itself had never occurred to him to name, let alone set aside. One omission is a method. The other is just an absence, dressed, by its tailoring, to look like one.

Our visiting historians, lovely people, deeply read, weep real tears for Mariupol and mean every one of them, arrive with frameworks sharpened on other people’s corruption — they can tell you, unprompted, how patronage calcifies into governance, how a circle of old friends becomes a budget line, how proximity launders itself into plausible ignorance over enough birthdays in enough mountains. They have written whole books describing exactly this part of exactly this animal, in other countries.

They simply find, this once, that their hands have wandered to the ear.

This is not dishonesty, properly speaking. Dishonesty would require noticing there’s a hindquarters at all. What we have instead is a kind of professional hygiene: a man stands in a dark room with an enormous animal and reports, with complete sincerity, the only shape his palm has found.

And it is not only the historians in the room. The official-turned-podcaster is there too, and he reaches for his own favorite part — the ingenuity, the feedback loops, the basements full of soldering irons — and tells, with visible pride, of the *“Ukraine technology sprint”* his own people fought the Pentagon for, the \$1.5 billion they got into the country’s drone industry “by the end of ‘24,” seed money for a harvest, he says, beyond anything they could have imagined. All true. All the trunk. What goes unmentioned, by a man holding the very part of the animal closest to it, is which hands that seed money eventually passed through, and which of those hands now have a missile named, with the specific cruelty of the accurate, after a friend.

So the helicopter that flew friends to a birthday in November before the invasion does not make it into the podcast. Neither does the warehouse of vests cleared by “one phone call,” nor the missile the soldiers call by that friend’s name. What makes it into the podcast is not merely freedom *from* but freedom *to* — all true, all moving, all of it the trunk, none of it the elephant.

The animal, for its part, never asked to be described in parts. It simply stood there, whole, in the dark, while careful men took turns explaining it to each other.

**Addendum: A Man Who Has Already Touched the Whole Animal**

There is, it turns out, a man who does not need to feel around in the dark, because he used to run the department that turned the lights on for a living.

Colonel Roman Chervinsky spent years as head of the SBU’s counterintelligence directorate — the man who pulled Tsemakh out of Donetsk for the Hague, who built the operation against Wagner’s mercenaries in Belarus, who is named in Western reporting as the coordinator on the ground for Nord Stream. He has, in other words, held the trunk, the leg, the ear, and a few parts the parable doesn’t have words for. He is currently on trial himself, on charges he calls retaliation for refusing to stay quiet about what he saw — which means he has, in the most literal sense available to a human being, skin in this particular game, and his account should be weighed as such: not a disinterested seminar paper, but the testimony of a man currently being prosecuted by the system he is describing.

What he describes is not a missing trunk or a misplaced ear. It’s the suggestion that someone has been quietly walking the elephant out of the building since 2019 — that the corruption wasn’t a wartime lapse but the founding architecture, that the Wagner operation collapsed because someone with access to the timeline made sure it would, that a President’s Office could, while citizens were memorizing air-raid schedules, still find the staff and the patience to build four houses outside Kyiv. He goes further than the documents do, further than any court has ruled — he names the thing directly, treason, agentry, intention — and that further step is his own, not yet anyone’s verdict. But the documented scaffolding underneath it — the Dynasty case, the Tsemakh irregularities, the Wagner sabotage allegations, the elder Yermak’s reported trips east — is not something a credentialed visitor would need a source for. It’s been reported, for years, in the same country the seminar is praising for its candor.

None of this makes the rabbit’s old description of the trunk untrue. It makes it a description of one part, offered by men who had the choice of which part to touch, given by a man who has touched all of it and is currently paying for having said so out loud[2](#footnote-2).
