The philosopher Daniel Dennett once proposed [a test for reality](https://ruccs.rutgers.edu/images/personal-zenon-pylyshyn/class-info/FP2012/FP2012_readings/Dennett_RealPatterns.pdf) that has the charm of a joke.

A checkerboard can be written down quickly. A field of flax, seen from above: hundreds of small blue and violet flowers, each slightly different, scattered without order, light catching some and losing others, and a dark human shadow cutting through the middle, takes longer to encrypt. There is nothing to say about it except itself.

From this he concluded: a pattern is real if it allows you to say less.

This is a comforting idea. It suggests the world, at least in places, can be shortened.

-

On April 18, a man with a gun killed seven people in Kyiv. Two police officers arrived and left.

The next day, there was a statement. It contained the usual things: procedures, reviews, the future tense.

A day later, the language improved. The officers had fled. There would be accountability.

Nothing essential had changed. The sentences had.

-

It is tempting to explain such shifts. One can speak of pressure, of advisors, of timing. These explanations are available in abundance, like confetti.

But there is a simpler approach, which requires a certain restraint.

Dennett suggested that, when faced with a system one does not understand, it is often useful to pretend it is rational. Not wise, not good—only consistent. Then one asks what it would want, and watches.

-

Z is a man who speaks well when speaking costs nothing.

This is not a rare talent.

He also prefers silence when speech would require him to choose between two things that both have consequences. This, too, is not rare, though it is less often noticed.

Between these two observations, a certain economy appears.

One begins to suspect that many details are unnecessary.

-

There was a [conversation with Amanpour](https://www.facebook.com/watch/?v=4509715932594959) about peace. It went well. It could hardly do otherwise.

[Video: https://youtu.be/biAKEJXBXRQ]

There were later remarks about American behavior, sharper than before. They appeared at a moment when silence had begun to look like a decision rather than an absence.

Again, the sentences improved.

-

If one wished to be unkind, one could say that the man develops convictions precisely at the point where they become inexpensive.

But this would be to say too much, and also too early.

It is better to wait.

-

Elsewhere, other men speak.

Donald Tusk and Emmanuel Macron meet and discuss unity. The word appears several times. It does not seem to burden them.

[Video: https://youtu.be/foyf6P9Vabk]

At the same time, there are calculations. Not hidden exactly, but not emphasized. What is given, what is received, what is conditional.

The conversation proceeds without difficulty.

Empires, when they divide, usually do so more plainly. There is a certain relief in that.

-

A pattern, if it is real, should reduce effort.

In this case, one can ignore almost everything: the topic, the tone, the declared principles. A single question remains, modest but persistent:

what does this cost?

From it, much follows.

-

There is something faintly embarrassing about a person who can be understood in this way. Not because he is worse than others, but because he is clearer.

Clarity, in such matters, has a way of shifting from virtue to symptom.

One begins to wonder whether the man is leading events, or whether events have found in him their most economical expression.

-

Difficulties arise when the arithmetic stops working—when what is advantageous and what is necessary no longer coincide.

At such moments, patterns of this kind do not break. They pause.

This is also a form of consistency.

-

A real pattern allows you to say less.

It also leaves you with less to hope for.
