As on Day 1, on the Ukrainian jacket, it’s all in your face, famous tongues just a-snakin’ away, badass chains nestled in luxurious chest hair. But on the Russian jacket, lots of bullshit, and all that supermacho chest growth continues to recede.

The fried eggs and wine diet has obviously not worked for Z, and it was a bit rich for him to [lecture European leaders](https://www.president.gov.ua/news/zvernennya-prezidenta-do-uchasnikiv-specialnogo-zasidannya-v-102517) about anything yesterday at Davos.

Z closed his performance by insisting “Groundhog Day must end.” He’s right, but not in the way he thinks.

The real loop isn’t Europe’s—it’s Z’s. Another forum, another speech, another round of lecturing others about failures that mirror his own. Europe lacks courage? Z ignored explicit, detailed warnings and failed to prepare for invasion. Europe can’t act independently? Z admits “no security guarantees work without the United States.” Europe suffers from corrupt leadership? Almost every top official Z appointed—from SBU to Energy to Justice to Border Service—faces embezzlement charges.

The man who couldn’t defend his own coastline offers to patrol Greenland’s waters. The president who presided over strategic autophage—the state literally consuming itself through corruption while fighting for survival—lectures Europe on institutional development. The leader who botched conscription, approved faulty fortifications, and funneled defense contracts to cronies demands others demonstrate competence.

Z wants Europe to seize Russian assets while his officials seized everything not bolted down. He wants Europe to act on Iran while ignoring warnings about his own country. He wants a Special Tribunal for Russian aggression while neutering Ukraine’s anti-corruption prosecutors. He questions whether NATO would defend Lithuania while desperately seeking membership in an alliance he suggests is worthless.

This isn’t analysis—it’s performance art. Z emerged from a sham democracy where legitimacy comes from popular uprisings, not institutional competence, where reality isn’t expressed in rational categories, where sovereignty means theatrical defiance rather than state capacity. He never prepared to govern, never developed policy expertise, never built functional institutions. Why would he start now?

The French historian Ernest Renan said a nation is a daily plebiscite. Ukrainians vote daily by risking their lives. Their heroism is real and profound. But they are poorly led by a man who confuses his presence with governance, his eloquence with strategy, his moral clarity with institutional development.

Churchill understood the difference between sovereignty and subjectiveness. Z cosplays Churchill while exemplifying the opposite: not fortitude sending eloquence into battle, but eloquence substituting for strategy.

Europe has real failures—frozen assets, phantom tribunals, inadequate defense spending. But lectures from a leader whose top lieutenants loot during wartime, whose military interference costs lives, whose pre-war incompetence enabled catastrophe? That’s not critique. That’s projection from the stage of a primitive post-Soviet feudal autocracy.

Groundhog Day must indeed end. Perhaps Z could start by looking in the mirror—assuming he’d recognize what he sees after years of performing someone else.

**Slava Ukraini**—to the people paying the price for this magnificent resistance to reason.
