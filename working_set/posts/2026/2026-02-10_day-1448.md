On my Iphone as I write is a screenshot from [Vatican News](http://vaticannews.va). It shows Pope Leo XIV’s “solidarity amid Ukraine’s freezing winter”—three lorries carrying 80 electricity generators, along with medicine and food supplies, from Rome to Ukraine.

[Image]

*The Pope’s solidarity amid Ukraine’s freezing winter . Pope Leo has arranged for the delivery of 80 electricity generators, along with medicine and food supplies, to Ukraine ( Vatican News , February 10, 2026)*

Eighty generators. For a country of 37 million people. That’s one generator per 462,500 people.

The photograph also shows the Dicastery for the Service of Charity preparing “thousands of antibiotics, anti-inflammatories, antihypertensive medicines and various food supplies” for distribution through parish networks. An outpouring of generosity made possible, Cardinal Krajewski emphasizes—expressing thanks on behalf of the Pope—by “all people of goodwill who continue to support those who suffer.”

People of goodwill sending generators and sleep aids (”melatonin is especially in demand as it helps people sleep amid ongoing fear and constant stress”) while the people responsible for infrastructure send information pamphlets on energy efficiency.

This reminds me of walking around with the chief urologist at a dilapidated state hospital outside Bishkek in the early 1990s. The wards were full of men who had just been operated on for [Benign Prostatic Hyperplasia](https://www.mayoclinic.org/diseases-conditions/benign-prostatic-hyperplasia/symptoms-causes/syc-20370087). MSD’s wonder drug [Proscar](https://mozdocs.kiev.ua/likiview.php?id=12539)—which reduces prostate volume, improves urine flow, and can prevent the need for surgery—would have been invaluable to these men. Six months earlier. Before they needed the knife.

But there we were, discussing pharmaceutical donations for post-operative patients. Pills that prevent surgery, arriving after the surgery has already happened.

The Pope responding to appeals from Ukrainian bishops “aware of the suffering caused by the war.” Night-time temperatures falling to minus 25 degrees Celsius. People forced to leave their homes for heated shelters where, thanks to the generators, they can receive a cup of tea.

At least His Holiness acknowledges the actual problem: people need electricity and heat, not tips on how to conserve power.

Though one suspects even he might consider addressing the alleged corruption at Energoatom before blessing another shipment.

Meanwhile, here in Kyiv, winter is over—so Ukraine has decided to dole out heat packages (пакунки тепла) to immiserated citizens.

The kits include warm blankets, plaids, thermal blankets, energy-saving lamps, hygiene products, information materials on energy efficiency.

No, thank you.

Prime Minister Svyrydenko announced that 30,000 people have already received packages “in areas where the situation during frosts was most difficult.” Past tense. The frosts *were* difficult. They happened. We are now distributing comfort items.

President Z, in Monday’s meeting: “It’s important that people from each such building receive all necessary support.” Support. Not heat. Support.

Then: “The heat package program in Kyiv will also be expanded. In the near future, the program will reach a distribution volume of 40,000 such packages to people, with a significant portion specifically in Kyiv.”

Naftogaz, Energoatom, Ukrenergos, Ukrhydroenergo, and Lisy Ukrainy (Forests of Ukraine) have collectively pledged 70,000 additional packages. The initiative has “expanded.”

The heating has not.

[Image]

*Energoatom spends UAH 150 million on heat packages*

Near future. After the worst has passed. After winter has broken.

This is crisis management calibrated not to the thermometer but to the news cycle. Scale up aid distribution precisely when the emergency is ending, ensuring maximum coverage of government action with minimum pressure to solve the underlying problem.

Absent from the packages: any timeline for heat restoration. Any acknowledgment of the procurement failures, the infrastructure corruption, the systematic looting that left Ukraine’s energy system so vulnerable that Russian strikes could reduce the capital to celebrating blanket distribution.

The packages contain “information materials on energy efficiency.” Tips on staying warm. How to conserve power. Make do with less.

Lady bountifuls of state enterprise, distributing wool socks to the deserving poor. Thermal underwear for pensioners. Candles for the disabled.

All very humane. All very photogenic. All very beside the point.

The point being: [we celebrated an extra nine megawatts yesterday](https://abdymok.substack.com/p/day-1447). Today we’re celebrating thermoses and papal generators. Tomorrow perhaps we’ll celebrate the energy efficiency pamphlets.

Blankets that keep you warm, arriving after winter ends. Pills that prevent surgery, arriving after the operation. Generators for a crisis, arriving as temperatures rise.

[Image]

But at least the vulnerable categories—properly registered, properly documented, properly grateful—will have a pair of new socks for the spring.
