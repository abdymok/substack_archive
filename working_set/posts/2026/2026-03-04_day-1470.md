If you’re as exhausted as we are by the metastasizing genre of strategy document — those careful monuments to institutional self-regard, produced with the confidence of men who have confused the writing of plans with the execution of them, and whose principal function is to provide, in the event of catastrophe, a paper trail demonstrating that someone, somewhere, said the right words before the bombs fell — then the [National Security Strategy](https://www.whitehouse.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/National-Security-Strategy.pdf) and [National Defense Strategy](https://media.defense.gov/2026/Jan/27/2003398550/-1/-1/1/2026-NATIONAL-DEFENSE-STRATEGY.PDF), published in November 2025 and January 2026 respectively, will not disappoint.

Key message: No more interventionism. No more endless wars. No more regime change.

Six weeks later, Iran.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Yesterday in the Senate, Tim Kaine read the NDS back to one of its authors with the patience of a man holding a receipt. If you arrest one foreign leader and assassinate another, is that not regime change? US Deputy Secretary of War Elbridge Colby — whom we have pillaged repeatedly — would not say. This is strategy’s first and most reliable instinct when pressed by reality: renegotiate the definitions. When language softens, missions expand. When missions expand, the documents are archived — diet plans tucked beneath a takeout menu.

[Video: https://youtu.be/N4j8Bb6mQq0]

Mark Warner, fresh from classified briefings, reported no imminent threat from Iran. Only a succession of rationales: nuclear sites, then missiles, then naval assets, then regime change, now recast as necessity. A war of choice, he called it. Someone chose. It was not Congress.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

[Iran’s foreign minister spoke to Al Jazeera](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TAUOeoLqzUE) and did something rarer than outrage: he drafted the afterword before the chapter had closed. The assassination unprecedented. A violation of international law. Dangerous. The Supreme Leader not merely a head of state but a transnational religious figure for tens of millions beyond Iran’s borders — damage measured carefully, almost tenderly. Americans, he added, deserve better.

Inside the condemnation was architecture. America entered this war on behalf of Israel, he implied. The blood is on Israeli hands. If this is not groundwork for future reconciliation — we were never truly at war, we were maneuvered — then what is it? A new leader will be elected. Everything constitutional. Everything orderly. The exit drafted before the entrance seals.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

We have seen this structure before. In July 2025, we examined Colby’s wager that Moscow could be peeled from Beijing — a clean pivot to Asia, Europe demoted to distraction — a theory that presumed a Putin shopping for alignment[1](#footnote-1). The Putin who exists shops for survival. Moscow sank deeper into Beijing’s orbit. China acquired a dependent, not a weapon.

The flaw, in Iran as in Russia, is identical: abstraction over agency. Pivot to Asia, wage war in the Middle East. Avoid regime change, remove regimes. The theory is always cleaner than the autocrat.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Separately, Trump referred to Z yesterday as [P.T. Barnum](https://truthsocial.com/@realDonaldTrump/116165640188665672) — for the second time. Barnum was a 19th-century circus pioneer and professional hoaxer; the reference requires a specific cultural literacy that Kyiv does not possess, which may be the point. Trump has previously called Z “the greatest salesman on Earth.” The escalation in contempt tracks the escalation in strategic irrelevance. Ukraine was always the thing Colby’s Asia pivot needed to leave behind. It is being left behind. The salesman has become the clown.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Iran can be degraded militarily. That is not the question. The question is whether a republic that generates polarization without algorithms and wages war without authorization can sustain the discipline great-power competition requires.

Colby wanted to husband resources. The resources are definitely not in Ukraine.

The strategies promised limits. The limits reside in the archive, filed under lessons learned, beside the previous lessons learned, beside the ones before those.

The current catastrophe will produce more documents. They will be very clear. They will promise restraint.
