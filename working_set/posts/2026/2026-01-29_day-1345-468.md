One war, begun in 2014 and massively escalated in 2022, still confounds Marco Rubio, who at the two hours and forty-second minute of his testimony before the [US Senate Foreign Affairs Committee](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=OkfwTrcNYsI&t=7187s) yesterday described the situation as “very fluid.”

There is one issue remaining: “The territorial claim on Donetsk,” Rubio said, adding the situation is “so fluid it could change in 72 hours.”

Look, we’ve all experienced fluid situations. Coffee spills. Market volatility. The gelatinous state of Trump’s promises. When someone describes an active genocide as “fluid,” they’re either catastrophically unqualified or practicing a very specific art form called **diplomatic cowardice**.

What’s fluid, apparently, is Trump’s willingness to demand Ukraine withdraw from all of Donetsk region as a precondition for “security guarantees.” [Reuters](http://reuters.com) and [The Financial Times](http://ft.com) confirmed what was an open secret: Trump’s “deal” mirrors Putin’s demands, a framework likely sketched in earlier understanding between the two leaders who understand each other “at half a word.”

*“Round and round and round we go.”*

> There’s a small legal complication. Since 2014, the United States has enacted multiple binding federal laws—the [Support for the Sovereignty, Integrity, Democracy, and Economic Stability of Ukraine Act of 2014](https://www.congress.gov/bill/113th-congress/house-bill/4152) and the [Ukraine Freedom Support Act of 2014](https://www.congress.gov/bill/113th-congress/house-bill/5859)—that explicitly affirm Ukraine’s sovereignty within its internationally recognized borders. U.S. policy recognizing Ukraine’s territorial integrity is embedded in [federal law](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=granuleid:USC-prelim-title22-section8921&num=0&edition=prelim). The U.S. executive branch cannot legally recognize Russian claims to occupied Ukrainian territory without violating existing U.S. law.

One might think this matters. But we're watching the fragility of our institutions in real time. Even knowing the playbook of a demagogue doesn't mean we can successfully battle it. The gains we thought were permanent—rule of law, human rights, territorial sovereignty—turn out to be profoundly vulnerable when there are perceptions (real or manufactured) of scarcity, intergroup competition, or predation.

*“When will it end?”*

Rubio said earlier he’s in constant touch with Trump’s special envoys—including his son-in-law and golf partner—tasked with ending what the [Center for Strategic and International Studies](http://csis.org) estimates has killed two million people, mostly Russian soldiers[1](#footnote-1).

Recall that Trump pledged at least 83 times to end the Russia-Ukraine war within 24 hours of returning to the White House. Day 1435 suggests the timeline was optimistic.

*“But I can see no end.”*

Don’t call genocide fluid. Don’t pretend laws don’t matter. Don’t expect 72 hours to change what 1,435 days couldn’t.

*“Round and round and round we go / When will it end?”*

Stay un-gaslit. Stay furious. Watch out for falling icicles!
