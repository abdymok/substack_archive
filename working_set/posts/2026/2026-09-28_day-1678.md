There is something almost old-fashioned about the Russian drone attack on the Inkom building in Kyiv on Friday.

Not the drone itself. The arrangement.

According to [Serhiy Beskrestnov, known as Flash](https://t.me/serhii_flash/7775?utm_source=chatgpt.com), the Russians sent two jet-powered drones into Kyiv. One was a Geran-5, the attacking vehicle. The other was a Geran-4, flying at around three kilometres over the target. It acted as a MESH radio relay for the Geran-5 while simultaneously filming the attack and transmitting the pictures back. Afterward, Flash says, the Geran-4 flew beyond Kyiv and attacked a logistics facility.

At first this was one man’s reconstruction of what had happened.

Now the Russians appear to be providing their own visual corroboration.

The Varyag Telegram channel has published a 2½-minute video showing strike sequences against a series of Ukrainian data-center and network facilities, including the Inkom/Datagroup site. The video identifies eleven targets, among them De Novo, PrivatBank, Kyivstar, United DC, Cosmonova, G50/NEWTELCO, Inter Cars, BeMobile, Datagroup, Parkovy and Volia.

I have been keeping the data-center part of this story in a [running chronology](https://abdymok.github.io/40/articles/data-center-damage.html?utm_source=chatgpt.com). It began simply as a record of attacks on Kyiv’s data centers and network infrastructure. But as the pattern has become clearer, it has become difficult to look at those attacks in isolation.

The Varyag video does not, by itself, prove every detail of Flash’s reconstruction, particularly the relay function of the Geran-4. But it does something unusual. The attackers themselves are publishing pictures of the attacks that Flash was trying to explain.

The video shows the strike being filmed from the air. Flash’s account provides the explanation of how he believes that was done. Taken together, they describe a rather different kind of drone operation.

This is not entirely new.

For roughly the past month, the same family of jet-powered Gerans has been appearing around rather different kinds of targets. Trains have been among them, including trains carrying Western visitors leaving Ukraine for Europe. There have also been attacks on the SBU director’s office and other targets whose identities are less clear.

On September 4, Russian forces struck the SBU headquarters in Kyiv. Ukrainian officials said the office of SBU head Oleksandr Poklad was specifically targeted. Flash said the drone was remotely controlled and that a MESH-network repeater was involved.

The significance of the Inkom attack is therefore not simply that another building was hit.

It is the division of labour.

One drone carries the weapon.

The other brings the eyes — and, apparently, the telephone.

Flash has been warning about this development for weeks. He has described Russian use of jet-powered Gerans with MESH networking and argued that the technology is allowing operators to maintain control over drones at increasing distances.

And then there are the targets that become personal by accident.

The building hit in Pechersk on Friday was not some abstract piece of Kyiv geography. I lived there for four years. It was part of the ordinary map of my life, until suddenly it became part of another map.

There is a peculiar moment in this war when geography turns into literature all by itself.

For the last 72 hours, Kyiv has spent **almost 36 hours under air alert**.

So perhaps this is the part worth remembering.

The new danger is not simply that the drones are faster.

It is that they have begun to work in pairs.

One carries the bomb.

The other brings a friend.
