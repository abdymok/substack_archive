That it took almost the entire month of June 2026 to legalize (wash, clean) a mere UAH 150 million (\$3.4 million) through state-owned banks to bail out Ukraine’s ex-Energy and ex-Justice Minister Herman Halushchenko is a testament to the incompetence of everyone involved.

Halushchenko, we recall, was remanded to custody on Feb. 17, 2026, for his alleged involvement in a \$100 million kickback scheme involving state-run Energoatom. He was detained after attempting to flee the country on Feb. 15.

Details of the botched operation emerged during a three-hour bail hearing for Sense Bank officials at the High Anti-Corruption Court of Ukraine (HACC) on August 25.

[Video: https://youtu.be/WHmb9DQQHDI]

What emerges from the hearing is less a sophisticated financial operation than a bureaucratic relay race in which nobody quite knew who was carrying the baton, where the finish line was, or why the President’s Office was involved in the first place.

The money, according to evidence presented in court, passed through a succession of state banks, accounts and intermediaries before the full UAH 150 million reached the HACC bail account.

Iryna Mudra, Deputy Head of the President’s Office, who allegedly managed the operation, was led out of the courtroom yesterday in handcuffs. HACC remanded her to custody for 60 days, with an alternative of UAH 20 million bail.

There was, of course, a simpler way to organize the operation.

A competent team would have started with a small meeting: We have UAH 150 million. Herman needs UAH 150 million. Here is Herman. Here is the money. Here is the bail account.

Then everybody goes home.

Nobody calls the President’s Office. Nobody involves the chairman of a state-owned bank, the chairman of its supervisory board, various trusted people and trusted people of trusted people. Nobody asks half the Ukrainian financial system to participate in what is supposed to be a clandestine operation.

Most importantly, professionals understand the first rule of clandestine operations: the fewer people who know, the better.

Instead, the operation acquired the organizational elegance of a Soviet housing cooperative. There were meetings, introductions, intermediaries, phone calls, instructions, objections and reconsiderations — and, apparently, considerable anxiety about how to move UAH 150 million without making it look as though someone was moving UAH 150 million.

Investigators say preparations ran from June 9 to June 22, followed by the actual posting of the bail between June 22 and June 29.

This is what happens when people who have spent their careers arranging things in offices suddenly discover that money has to move in the real world.

In a normal criminal organization, somebody would have said: “Stop. This is becoming complicated.”

Here, apparently, the answer to every complication was to add another person.

And so UAH 150 million went on a grand tour of Ukrainian finance before arriving where it was supposed to go in the first place. It was not so much money laundering as money sightseeing.

The remarkable thing is not that the operation was discovered. The remarkable thing is that it worked at all.

The money was posted. Halushchenko was released. And then the people who allegedly organized the operation spent three hours explaining it to a court.

There are many ways to describe this. “State capture” is one. “Financial crime” is another. But perhaps the simplest is this: they needed almost an entire month to move UAH 150 million from Point A to Point B — and somehow managed to leave fingerprints at every stop along the way.

Z says Ukraine needs to find \$120 billion a year to keep fighting. His own office, judging by this week’s testimony, needed a month, half the banking system, and a small army of intermediaries to move \$3,400,000 without getting caught. Draw your own conclusions about the other \$119,996,600,000.
