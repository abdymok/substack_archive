When two distinguished analysts arrive independently at identical conclusions, the reader is entitled to experience a certain relief. The matter appears settled. [Fareed Zakaria](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kdtfJra3K6M), from his studio, and [Anne Applebaum](https://www.theatlantic.com/ideas/2026/06/ukraine-war-momentum-shift/687444/), from her van, have both examined the war and reached the same verdict: momentum has shifted, drones are working, Europe has stepped up, Ukraine is not losing.

Most of this is true. The drones are working. Momentum has shifted. These developments are visible even from Kyiv, where some residents receive updates during the night.

What attracts attention is not what the two analyses contain but what they leave out. The omissions are remarkably similar. One hesitates to call this coordination. It is something more dependable.

Ukrainian resilience is real. Russian brutality is real. These facts present no difficulty. What presents difficulty is information suggesting that Z and several people around him have done rather well during the prolonged defense of the country they are defending.

The same difficulty appears in the treatment of Z’s recent open letter to Putin. Abroad it was received as a peace initiative. Closer to the scene, many regarded it as a carefully arranged provocation. The difference is less important than the fact that only one interpretation seems admissible.

Meanwhile, Putin’s system resembles a bicycle. It remains upright while moving and becomes unstable when stationary. This does not mean it is approaching collapse. It means only that stopping may be more dangerous than continuing.

For Europe, this creates an unusual strategic problem. Defeating Russia is one thing. Managing a Russia that cannot win and cannot stop is another. The regime’s remaining escalatory options — pressure on the Baltics, nuclear use, full mobilization of the economy — appear less decisive the more closely they are examined. Yet a government may continue for a long time on options that are self-destructive. History contains several examples.

These matters are absent from both the van piece and the studio piece. Not because they are secret. Not because they are unknown. They simply belong to a different category of information.

The young civil servant Applebaum quotes, grieving the loss of a flight to Barcelona, reads Ukrainian. She reads Russian as well.

She is familiar with the missing category.
