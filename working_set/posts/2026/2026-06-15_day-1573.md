There is something clarifying about the image. The president of the United States, white golf cap lettered USA, peers through a chain-link fence at sweaty men grappling on the White House lawn. This is the UFC. This is diplomacy. This is the arrangement.

Or consider another image. The same cap. The same fence. Behind it, Kyiv at dawn: apartment blocks, treelines, a construction crane, a sky the color of a bruise. The smoke is there if you know where to look.

When the United States was a young and culturally peripheral republic, Europe was producing people who worried about whether freedom could survive if detached from moral duty. They gathered in places like Jena and treated the question with great seriousness. The individual mattered, certainly. But so did obligation. So did other people. So did the larger world into which one happened to be born. They were mocked as dreamers, and perhaps they were.

What would they make of this morning?

[Image]

*Firefighters work at a site of the Dormition Cathedral of the Kyiv Pechersk Lavra monastery, which burns after it was hit during Russian missile and drone strikes, amid Russia’s attack on Ukraine, in Kyiv, Ukraine June 15, 2026. ( Ukrainian Culture Ministry/Handout/Reuters)*

This was the second strike on the Lavra since the full-scale invasion began. The first, in January, damaged structures near the Far Caves and the Church of the Conception of St. Anne. The Lavra is a UNESCO World Heritage site. It is also, apparently, a target.

[Image]

The State Service for Ethnopolitics and Freedom of Conscience issued a statement. Moscow’s invaders, it said, had spent centuries trying to erase the monastery from the face of the earth. Their descendants had shown their true face once again. For Russian terrorists, nothing is sacred: not human life, not faith, not temples, not history.

In Kharkiv, five State Emergency Service rescuers were killed in a double-tap strike. The second missile arrived when they reached the scene of the first. At least five more were wounded. In Kyiv, by morning, four people were dead and twenty-three wounded, among them a child and a pregnant woman. The attack was still ongoing.

It was, by coincidence or design, the president’s [80th birthday](https://edition.cnn.com/2026/06/14/europe/kyiv-pechersk-lavra-monastery-fire-russian-attack-intl-hnk). He spent part of it on the phone with Putin, who called to congratulate him. The conversation lasted just under an hour. Trump called for an end to the war. Putin suggested that if Z wanted a meeting, he should come to Moscow. They agreed that Witkoff and Kushner would visit Russia again soon. Later, Trump spoke with Z as well. “We discussed many key things,” Z said. “Peace, surely, was among them.” They agreed to talk more at the G7 in France. In Kyiv, the attack was still ongoing.

The thinkers in Jena worried about what would happen when everything became instrumental — when value was measured only by utility, when spectacle displaced meaning, when freedom detached itself from obligation. They did not imagine drones. They did not imagine social media. They certainly did not imagine cage fights on the White House lawn. But they would recognize the tendency.

A thousand-year-old monastery burns in one country. In another, the cameras pan toward the entertainment.

The man in the golf cap did not comment on the Lavra. He was watching the fight.

What has the world come to. It is not really a question. It is the sort of sentence one says aloud in an empty room, in a city where the air-raid alert has become ambient, where a thousand-year-old altar burns at one in the morning, and where the leader of the free world is cageside.

Two centuries later, the world’s most powerful democracy appears to have settled on a different answer to the question the Jena people were asking. Freedom without obligation. Spectacle without meaning. The self without the world.

They were not wrong to worry.
