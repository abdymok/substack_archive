Ruslan Lobanov has museum editions of *[Wartime Sketches](https://www.ruslanlobanov.com/%D0%BA%D0%BE%D0%BF%D0%B8%D1%8F-not-black-and-white-cinema-book?lightbox=dataItem-lhytsvei)* left. Three, he says. Selected, a new large-format book printed in Verona, is also out.

Both are about visualizing Ukraine’s victory.

We wrote about *Wartime Sketches* in January 2024, when it first appeared[1](#footnote-1). The book is about optimism. The photographs of fully naked women cannot be called excessively provocative, which is itself a kind of argument. A concept addressing the war directly would not have worked. We live in the modern world. Yes, Russian invaders are murdering people and dropping bombs in eastern Ukraine. *Wartime Sketches* makes no attempt to illustrate bloody disaster. It illustrates something else, which is harder.

The cover of *Selected* shows a woman pouring Tears of Russian Fears champagne into glasses at the celebration of Putin’s demise. I have not seen the prints inside. Yet. I suspect they are not about bloody disaster.

The news, meanwhile, arranges itself, accordingly.

Belarusian signal repeaters along the Ukrainian border reportedly went dark on June 22. Z had told Lukashenko to disable them by June 26 or Ukraine would strike the equipment. Lukashenko, characteristically, neither complied nor refused. The repeaters simply stopped. Whether they were dismantled or merely paused is, at this point, a Belarusian state secret, which is to say a Russian one.

The Kremlin is pressuring Lukashenko to let Russian forces launch drones from Belarusian territory and extend the frontline westward. A former Russian intelligence officer told the Wall Street Journal that Moscow is threatening to cut financial support if Minsk does not cooperate. Lukashenko has been resisting this particular demand since 2022. He continues to resist it. This is, by the standards of the neighborhood, statesmanship.

Medvedev dismissed Z’s legitimacy. Lavrov said Russia will not freeze the current frontline. Ryabkov invoked the Alaska Summit. Russian officials have been invoking the Alaska Summit regularly, citing an agreement that no one confirmed and no public statement followed. The Kremlin alleges agreement in order to assert that the US is not abiding by it. This is the negotiating equivalent of a receipt from a restaurant that does not exist.

In Moscow, gasoline is running short. The city removed permit requirements for fuel tankers to keep supply moving. Azimuth Airlines says Russia has reached a critical aviation fuel situation. Russia is negotiating with Kazakhstan for 50,000 tons of AI-92. Ukraine’s strike campaign against Russian refineries since March has been producing these results at a pace that is no longer being called surprising.

A hill that is not a hill is a tell. The Irish equivalent of a crannog.

People build a village. It burns down. Another village is built. That one burns too. Centuries pass. The same patch of ground accumulates houses, ovens, coins, bones, ash, broken cups, children’s toys. Eventually there is enough debris to create a rise in the landscape, and someone, seeing only the gentle slope, mistakes it for nature.

We know better.

Roman Chervinsky has appeared more than 230 times across over 100 posts in this archive. He arrived first as an arrested counterintelligence officer, then as Wagnergate’s inconvenient witness, then as an increasingly lonely custodian of institutional memory. Read chronologically, his appearances form less a biography than a stratigraphic column of the Ukrainian state’s arguments with itself.

His latest interviews are interesting not because they contain spectacular accusations — Ukraine has never lacked those — but because they attempt to connect the layers[2](#footnote-2). Oman ceases to be an isolated curiosity and becomes a foundation trench. Prisoner exchanges stop being humanitarian episodes and become methods of advancement. Tsemakh explains Wagnergate. Wagnergate explains the distrust inside the security services. The ignored invasion warnings explain February 24. The houses outside Kyiv cease to be vulgar corruption stories and become artifacts from the same settlement.

One need not accept every conclusion to appreciate the excavation.

[Image]

An archaeologist is allowed hypotheses. He may say this shard probably belonged to this pot, this wall probably supported this roof, this destruction layer probably corresponds to this invasion. Other archaeologists may disagree. What matters is that the pieces are finally being laid side by side instead of catalogued in separate drawers.

The alternative is journalism by surface inspection.

One writes about a mansion without asking who appointed the owner. One writes about a prisoner exchange without asking why the same negotiators always prosper. One writes about intelligence failures without asking who promoted the men who failed. Each article is accurate. None requires remembering the article before it.

The tell grows taller.
