The electricity went off at 0300. The mother of the woman claiming to be [KonMarie](https://konmari.com/?srsltid=AfmBOoqVMqMBXia7zvpDLpuWWF3LgDNYB3aGs7qDE52AQl8HucYS_XSF) wrapped up porridge in a blanket for when I got up at 0500. So yesterday’s oats would at least be warm.

That’s the actual story of Kyiv’s freeze—practical, unglamorous, the kind of detail that doesn’t make it into atmospheric retrospectives about “defiant ingenuity.” But it’s what happens when someone steals from the defense budget and calls it innovation.

After accompanying police on welfare checks in neighborhoods on the left (eastern) bank of the Dnipro. [The New York Times](http://nyt.com) ran a print story about our adventure, with weeks-old video, headlining the suffering of residents freezing in their apartments[1](#footnote-1). The convoy of suffering tableaux was accompanied by a [105-second video](https://www.nytimes.com/video/world/europe/100000010689521/how-ukrainians-are-coping-without-heat.html?searchResultPosition=3) of Andrew — NYT’s bureau chief— saying that he “really has a sense of Kyiv unravelling.”

Meanwhile, Simon dropped dropped his Atlantic [tech-optimism fantasy](https://archive.ph/qwOZx#selection-611.0-617.68) about laser cannons. Both stories arrived precisely on February 10, 2026, when investigation would be awkward and atmosphere would be safe[2](#footnote-2). Access for deference.

What else happened February 10th? US Ambassador to NATO Matthew Whitaker—former Iowa toilet-scam lawyer turned Trump loyalist—declared peace in Ukraine "as close as it's ever been." "I think we should continue to pray for peace," Whitaker said, "but at the same time, make sure Ukraine continues to have … all they need to defend themselves."

Whitaker is America's Pavlo Yelizarov, the protagonist in Shuster's fantasy: unqualified, inexperienced, appointed for loyalty not competence, now managing something important. The parallel is perfect. Unfortunately, the journalism covering both the cold snap and Yelizarov's innovations is identically worthless.

### The Evasion Pattern

Kramer opens with Svitlana Zinovieva watching a Russian missile hit her heating plant. “It was like fireworks. But I knew it would soon be cold.” Beautiful. Haunting. Completely beside the point.

What Kramer doesn’t give you: Wine bottles in tents aren’t defiant ingenuity—they’re evidence someone stole the heating plant protection and building insulation budget.

Instead: outdoor rave, people in penguin suits, Zinovieva crossing fingers while watching her smokestack for steam. The kind of atmospheric drivel that makes editors weep and readers feel informed while learning nothing. Kramer gets his sense of Kyiv “unravelling” without explaining why the unravelling was profitable for specific people with names and bank accounts.

### The Tech Theater

Shuster transforms Z’s catastrophic decision to put a TV producer in charge of air defense into startup hero story. Pavlo Yelizarov: former political talk show producer, “same comedy circuit as Z,” spearfishing enthusiast. Now he runs Ukraine’s air defenses.

This is the same patronage pattern that put Maksym Bakhmatov (TV producer, Kvartal 95 circuit) in charge of Darnytsia district. Year four of infrastructure war, and critical positions are staffed like a comedy troupe reunion. Loyalty over competence, entertainment connections over expertise, accountability flowing only upward to Presidential Office.

Shuster doesn’t investigate this. He tours drone factories, watches laser demos, learns about 3D-printed interceptors nicknamed “Pisun” (dick). He writes: “Ukrainian technology has caught up faster than expected.” The kind of breathless innovation coverage that reads like a TechCrunch press release crossed with war tourism.

### The Access Economy

Shuster maintains Kyiv access. The Atlantic wants exclusive interviews, factory tours, commander sit-downs. Z’s Presidential Office controls that access like the velvet rope at [Tootsie Club](https://en.tootsie.kiev.ua/).

The transaction: access for deference. Ask hard questions about Operation Midas, Kvartal 95 cronyism, how UAH billions were misspent? Your factory tour invite gets revoked. Next access request gets slow-walked by the doucherockets in Z’s PR machine who’ve apparently learned media manipulation from watching Fox News Chief Correspondent Trey Yingst balance water bottles on door handles and prepare his mind through cold plunges.

### What Gets Protected

Both filed February 10th when winter broke. Crisis retrospective arriving precisely when investigation is safe because urgency has passed.

The story isn’t P1-Sun interceptors or penguin-suited ravers.

It’s why TV producers run air defense and district heating during year four of infrastructure war. It’s why no foreign flak will write those sentences. Instead we get piffle.

Kramer gets his sense of Kyiv unravelling. Shuster gets his laser demos. Whitaker gets his prayer-for-peace soundbite.

The winter *was* cold. It is over. It's so warm today I'm wearing sneakers. The oatmeal wrapped in a blanket is room temperature now. And nobody's going to explain why it needed wrapping in the first place.
