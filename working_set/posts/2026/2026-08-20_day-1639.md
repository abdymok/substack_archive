*The man at a wooden table.*

Fedorov is one of the weasels in Ukraine’s giraffe problem [we wrote about yesterday](https://abdymok.substack.com/p/day-1638).

Before the weasel taxonomy, though, the video itself. The Guardian says Fedorov has “nimbly positioned himself to challenge Z” once elections happen ([Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2026/aug/19/mykhailo-fedorov-zelenskyy-loyalist-opponent-ukraine), Aug 19, 2026). The FT calls it an “explosive nine-minute video” in which Fedorov accused Z of systemic failures and abuse of martial-law powers ([FT](https://www.ft.com/content/5799e5fa-c836-443b-935d-eb9fb58681b6?syn-25a6b1a6=1), Aug 19, 2026).

Nimble and explosive — nobody at either paper seems to have watched it as a video rather than mined it for adjectives. Nine minutes is a long time to hold a camera on a man with, by his own account, zero interest in office. Somebody storyboarded those nine minutes. The jacket, the table, the lighting, the missing flag — all of it chosen. Before what Fedorov said, let’s look at how he was styled while saying it.

Let us talk about the outfit first, because Ukrainian political theater always begins in the wardrobe department. They did not put Fedorov in a suit — a suit would have made him an ordinary politician, God forbid. They did not put him in a uniform either, because after Z wore his little green t-shirt into the ground, the public has developed an allergy to costume militarism. So they landed on black utility wear, somewhere between civilian clothing and a work jacket. No accident in a single stitch. This is premium simplicity. This is a man who works.

What is the stupid viewer meant to understand? That Misha is not a bureaucrat but a Western-style effective manager. While the old guard steals in expensive suits, this young man in his black uniform is coding our victory with his bare hands, and he doesn’t even want money for it — only results. Very touching. Also very cheap, at four thousand views and eleven thousand likes — which tells you how many people watched this civic epiphany versus how many just liked the thumbnail.

Now the frame, because someone got paid to think about the frame. No flag. No coat of arms. No ministry, no institutional furniture of any kind — every trace of the state sanded off. Sit him next to a flag in the Ministry of Defense and the message is: an official reports on his work. Strip that away and the message becomes: institutions are secondary, there is only me, you, and the problem of the state. The stupid viewer is meant to feel addressed one-on-one, late at night, by a fellow lonely warrior fighting the same faceless “system” as the rest of us — a system whose actual name, Volodymyr Oleksandrovych, Misha never quite manages to say out loud.

The table is wood, not glass — wood gives you the warmth of soil, of a man with taste who happens to work through the night. The lighting is half-dark, intimate: nothing to fear here, everything under control. The body: hands resting symmetrically, no wide gestures, camera at eye level. Around him, chaos, death, corruption. And he alone, apparently, knows what he is doing.

A small masterpiece of contemporary Ukrainian PR, then — the smart adult alone with the viewer, built entirely out of absence: no flag, no ministry, no uncomfortable surnames. Just Misha, his wooden table, and eleven thousand people who liked a photograph without asking who else was sitting in it.

But the stupid domestic viewer was never really the audience. Strip the state furniture, ditch the uniform, sit a telegenic disruptor at a bare wooden table denouncing the legacy system standing between his country and the future — that’s not a broadcast to the kitchens of Kyiv. That’s a casting reel for two men, several time zones away, fluent in exactly this dialect of founder-messiah minimalism. One sells governments software that promises to see through their own bureaucracies. The other sells the idea that one obsessive enough individual can out-engineer any institution. Misha 3.0 isn’t addressing Ukraine. He’s auditioning for Karp and Musk, in their own visual language — black utility wear, unadorned desk, the calm stare of a man who’s identified the problem and just needs the system out of his way.

Funny, incidentally, that NABU dropped its own trailer the same week, for an entirely different case, and called it Operation Forrest Gump. The name was supposed to mock a mid-level idiot who registered stolen money in his own kids’ names. But the real joke of Forrest Gump was never the box of chocolates. It was that the man wanders through every major event of an era — present for all of it, implicated in none of it — and by the closing credits he is somehow still innocent. Run that against Misha’s résumé instead of the courier’s: drone czar, digital minister, deputy PM, defense minister, present at every checkpoint this war’s corruption story has passed through.
