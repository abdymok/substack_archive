It is Monday. Z is somewhere in Yerevan gladhanding foreign leaders, collecting loans, outlining priorities. Forty-eight heads of state. Canada attending for the first time. The headline writes itself.

This week two other stories will land in the Western press simultaneously: Moscow celebrating May 9th with the usual pageantry, and Ukraine marking May 8th ([Day of Remembrance and Reconcilliation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victory_Day_over_Nazism_in_World_War_II)) alongside Europe and the United States. Editors will have their hands full with the symmetry.

Z’s mess at home will probably wait.

Nine suspects charged. Luxury estates in Kozyn. A fence between Mindich’s property and “Vova’s.” A defense minister allegedly asking a fugitive businessman whether a procurement decision “will suit us.” Andriy Yermak—referred to as the Surgeon—still uncharged, still present. BBC Ukrainian covered it, kind of. Ukrainska Pravda covered it. The Kyiv Independent covered it, very carefully.

Foreign flaks have not.

What the recordings document is not corruption in the primitive sense but an operating system. Reporting it in Ukrainian, for Ukrainians, is one thing. Reporting it in English, for the audiences whose governments are financing this war, is another.

A story has been invested in.

The shortcut works, until it becomes the road[1](#footnote-1).
