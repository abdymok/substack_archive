A country does not become real because its president embodies it. And it does not become unreal because he does not.

That’s my beef with historian Tim Snyder’s [meditation](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TKwfEs71VQI&t=2062s) over our current situation.

Snyder’s argument is that Z represents something Putin cannot accept: the idea that Ukraine is a real country, with its own history, interests and values. In Snyder’s telling, Z becomes almost a personification of Ukrainian sovereignty.

But there is an awkward question here.

Does Z actually deserve to represent all that?

[Image]

Snyder is asking what Z represents. The timeline asks what Z did.

Those are not necessarily the same thing.

And then there is the phrase “Putin hates Z.”

Perhaps. But turning the war into a story about Putin and Z — two men who dislike each other — is precisely the framing Trump uses repeatedly. It makes a war against Ukraine sound like a personal quarrel that might be settled if only the two principals could get along.

But Ukraine is not Z.

And Z is not Ukraine.

That distinction seems rather important to me.

A country can be real while its president is disappointing, foolish, compromised or simply wrong.

Indeed, perhaps that is the point.

The country does not need its president to embody it.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

There is a joke here that has been running longer than I would like. In January 2024, I wrote that Kyiv was “not a punching bag.”¹ Three months later we were “sitting ducks.”² By November, I had upgraded the zoology: “Target practice,” with the memorable diagnosis that Ukraine had become drone bait.³ In January of this year, I wrote about unmentioned urbicide, the crime under the Geneva Conventions, the Hague Convention, and the Rome Statute, and contrasted the physical reality of winter missile strikes on Kyiv with the absence of the word from the geopolitical yap sessions at Davos.⁴ Then, in July, Kyiv became a clay pigeon again for modified S-400s and ballistic missiles.⁵ In August, the ducks were still sitting.⁶ In September, after another exhausting procession of air alerts, we wrote: “more target practice. And we’re the targets.”⁷

[Image]

Perhaps “sitting duck” has outlived its usefulness. A duck at least has somewhere to go. The metaphors have stopped keeping pace with the news. We wait for Crotale NG VT-1 interceptors while ballistic missiles and jet-propelled drones practice their aim, and on Monday they hit the Academy of Sciences.

The same afternoon, a Russian drone also smashed into a hospital across the street from Ukraine’s Constitutional Court.
