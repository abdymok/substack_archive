Ukraine will not *cede the Donbas region to Russia*.

> Since 2014, the United States has enacted multiple **binding federal laws**, passed by Congress and signed by the President, that explicitly affirm **Ukraine’s sovereignty and territorial integrity within its internationally recognized borders**. These include the *Support for the Sovereignty, Integrity, Democracy, and Economic Stability of Ukraine Act of 2014* and the *Ukraine Freedom Support Act of 2014*, as well as later statutes that codify sanctions and policy toward Russia’s aggression. U.S. policy recognizing Ukraine’s territorial integrity is also **embedded in federal law** (U.S. Code). As a result, the U.S. executive branch **cannot legally recognize** Russian claims to occupied Ukrainian territory without violating existing U.S. law.

*Cautionary Tale from Odesa*

Look, we’ve all been there. The alarm goes off. You think, “Five more minutes won’t hurt.” But here’s a pro tip: if you’re in a war zone and the alarm is an air raid siren, those five minutes absolutely *will* hurt.

Our sleeping beauty special correspondent, Askold (*Nom de guerre*: The Turtle, for his confabulatory reporting about the Melnychenko recordings[1](#footnote-1)), demonstrating the kind of commitment to unconscious reverie that would impress even the most dedicated teenager, managed to remain unconscious through multiple warnings that explosive death was literally flying toward him. Admirable dedication to rest, truly.

In Ukraine when sirens designed specifically to wake you up before you become part of the debris field start wailing, perhaps consider relocating. Your sleep schedule will recover. Your ceiling, as demonstrated at the [Hotel Alarus](https://www.tripadvisor.com/Hotel_Review-g295368-d12713777-Reviews-Hotel_Alarus_Luxe-Odesa_Odesa_Oblast.html) at approximately 2:20am, may not.
