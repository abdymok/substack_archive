A pack of Det-A types with heavily tattooed legs at 6:30 a.m. Running off too many Mohitas at [Tootsie Club](https://uk.tootsie.kiev.ua/)? Not happy being photographed?

Z’s [pep talk last night](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CRf6isky1eI) ran the usual three minutes: New York next week, meetings with partners, negotiations, energy, food security, weapons, new support packages, air defense in particular. The Anti-Ballistic Coalition is expanding — Europe’s strongest manufacturers are already inside something called FREYJA, a name that suggests someone in the president’s office still has a Norse mythology app open. He thanked parliament for voting through criminal liability for scam call centers, a law he’d just signed, and noted, almost as an aside, that asset declarations are coming back for law enforcement and security personnel who aren’t at the front — enough, he said, to actually monitor their lifestyles. State-owned enterprises are getting overhauled too, on the model of Naftogaz and Ukrnafta, whatever that model turns out to mean once the cabinet writes it down.

It is, item for item, a perfectly reasonable three minutes. It is also worth noticing what work each sentence is doing. The call-center law exists because a sitting prosecutor general was just fired for protecting the call centers. The asset-declaration reform exists because two of the country’s own intelligence services shot at each other in a residential district two weeks ago over the custody of one militia commander, and because a deputy head of the Presidential Office was running a raider ring out of government offices under an operation named, with the investigators’ own gallows humor, for the goddess of justice. Every “important issue” in this speech is a policy response to a scandal Z has never once been asked to explain on camera — Carthage, Midas, Femida, Forrest Gump, a naming convention that started in classical antiquity and gave up somewhere around Robert Zemeckis.

That is the trick of it. He can announce the fix all day. What he cannot do, and what nobody currently interviewing him seems inclined to make him do, is sit still for the question underneath the fix: how did the state get to a place where its own law enforcement needed its assets re-monitored, where its spy agencies needed to be told not to shoot each other, where the fired defense minister has better things to say about governance from a YouTube studio than the sitting president says from his own office. Adrienne Arsenault didn’t ask it in Calgary, or Banff, wherever it was. CBS didn’t ask it either. Maybe New York will ask it next week.

There is a wiretap transcript with a diminutive in it that nobody has the nerve to read aloud. Are you Vova? Nobody asks, so nobody answers. Z narrates the repair work as though the building fell down on its own. The government keeps being run by people who have never had to say what Ukraine is — only, this week, what it isn't.
