> Historians’ conversation, being unfettered and life-affirming, will usually cause other diners to fall silent, ashamed of their bourgeois values and enchanted by the exuberance of the ‘credentialed’. A kindly act is to draw them into your group by waving or going over to their tables, kneeling down before them and begging forgiveness in extravagant language. Try to sing the theme tunes from obscure Cossack ballads, quote from Ivan Ilyin, and – of course – lots and lots of regional accents and silly voices.
> — “The Historiography of Lunch” ([Day 934](https://abdymok.substack.com/p/day-934))

It’s churlish to dwell on all that can go wrong with the professional historian. True, some aren’t completely annoying to be in the same room with, and some do [good work](https://web.archive.org/web/20011214052756/http://wwics.si.edu/kennan/), har har[1](#footnote-1). And you have to admire the tenacity to remain in a profession wherein rejection and slamming criticism are just the beginning. Gaping insecurity, white-hot neediness, gelatin bravado – there are worse characteristics to have.

All of which are savagely thrashed in a most welcome 4-year old post of “Cheap Caviar Comes With the Job,” written by the alter ego of someone resembling [Nigel Planer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nigel_Planer), whom you might recall as Neil the hippy on [The Young Ones](https://theyoungones.fandom.com/wiki/The_Young_Ones_Wiki).

> “It is relevant that he \[Z\] stayed and it’s relevant that the Ukrainian government stayed. But like that raises the final thing I want to talk about which is why it was so obvious to him that he should stay. And I think this has to do with Ukrainian civil society... He says I stayed because I knew it’s what the people expected of me. So like he sidelines the question of like are you so courageous and he says no I’m not so courageous. I stayed because that’s what people expected me to do.” — Timothy

Z’s self-deflation — “I’m not so courageous, I stayed because that’s what people expected me to do” — is the most interesting detail in the passage, and it’s the one that cuts against the heroic-communicator narrative that Snyder himself had just been building. It also sits in some tension with our treatment of Z — documenting governance failures. In February 2022 the same figure is doing something that Snyder, twenty years into studying Ukrainian civil society, describes as the inevitable expression of what Ukrainian horizontal networks of trust produce. The courage wasn’t personal — it was structural. Which is, interestingly, not so far from the feudal-autocracy thesis: the same structure that enables extraction also, under maximum pressure, produces a particular kind of accountability to popular expectation.

But I digress. The point here is that neither [Timothy nor Michael](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NPPDzVIKb3c) avail themselves of the opportunity to at least mention the historical fact that Z’s popularity in early 2022 was near zero, and that he and his top managers were building mansions in Kozyn instead of heeding months of detailed warnings that Russia intended to decapitate them.

A venue I neglected to mention in [yesterday’s survey](https://abdymok.substack.com/p/day-1576) of Kyiv’s rooms: Doors (Двери), on Reitarska — the same Reitarska Street where, in October 2003, a Land Cruiser carrying Volodymyr Kysil blew up opposite No. 13, six people inside, the Gongadze case visibly eating its own witnesses. Doors was running at full capacity that autumn. It was always a rough crowd, in the specific sense that rough and credentialed were the *same* crowd: the room had a talent for producing evenings where a foreign emissary, deeply confused about the country he had just arrived to save, could be debriefed at length before anyone told him what year it was. I hosted a number of those sessions. The Orange Revolution, which set the stage for the Revolution of Dignity, which set the stage for everything else, was already audible in the walls if you knew what feedback sounds like before it peaks.

The venue is now called Zig Zag. [We noted its existence in September 2024.](https://abdymok.substack.com/p/day-943?utm_source=publication-search) I avoid it on principle. The door, as you can see, is currently closed.

The event has been extensively memed.

[Image]

The text reads “ми спалимо вас на%уй!” — “we’ll burn you the f\*\*k up!” — in the aggressive red Soviet-style lettering that Ukrainian meme culture has been cheerfully repurposing since 2022.

The refinery is so thoroughly on fire it has achieved the energy of a lit cigarette carelessly flicked, or possibly that the whole operation was that casual. Either way it has the specific Ukrainian wartime meme energy of treating catastrophic violence as an occasion for a cheerful selfie in a flower crown. Russia’s official response, so far, has been restrained and formulaic, with officials promising to continue bombing us into oblivion.

Wonderful.
