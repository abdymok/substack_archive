I learned a lot about Google Analytics, GitHub and how HTML works, or doesn’t work. I think we ironed out most of the glitches on Monday morning.

The bulk of the work was done over the weekend, when Russia was trying to kill us with jet-powered drones.

This morning the page in front of me looks like this.

Malevich. A cat. Kyiv metro. The 41st square and pliers.

It all makes perfect sense.

Or at least it did after coffee.

I was thinking about Bakhtin on my walk yesterday morning.

Three words came to mind:

*dialogue*, *carnival*, *chronotope*.

The dialogue is today.

Z and Trump meet in New York. They will presumably talk about the war, Russia, Ukraine, oil, peace, whatever is currently occupying the diplomatic universe.

They may even agree about something.

But I have a feeling they will mostly be talking past one another.

That is dialogue, too.

The carnival is everything swirling around them.

And the chronotope is the stubborn part: time and place. Geography that refuses to move simply because history has become bored with it.

Brett McGurk is writing about Europe preparing for the possibility that Russia’s war against Ukraine could widen into a broader confrontation, including the Suwałki Gap and the possibility of Russian testing of NATO. [McGurk on Europe preparing for a wider conflict](https://edition.cnn.com/2026/09/21/politics/nato-prepares-russia-widen-ukraine-war-mcgurk)

Vladimir Pastukhov, writing yesterday, makes a related argument in a very different register: the war is fundamentally a war Russia is waging against Europe, with Ukraine as the immediate battlefield. [Pastukhov’s strategic picture of the war](https://vladimirbpastukhov.substack.com/p/putin-strategic-picture-of-the-war?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

And Shaun Walker has spent much of this year going back through the warnings that preceded the full-scale invasion — who knew what, when they knew it and why so many people failed to believe them. [Shaun Walker on the warnings before the war](https://www.theguardian.com/world/ng-interactive/2026/feb/20/a-war-foretold-cia-mi6-putin-ukraine-plans-russia?utm_source=chatgpt.com).

Three different people. Three different registers.

More or less the same geography.

I was at Teufelsberg 35 years ago. People were worrying about the Suwałki Gap then.

The Gap hasn’t closed. It’s as wide as ever.

That’s the chronotope.

Everything else is carnivalesque.

Which brings me to Oleshky.

Here’s where Oleshky is, so you can find it on a map.

[Image]

*Map c/o DeepState (September 22, 2026)*

Before the full-scale invasion, about 24,000 people lived there. Ukrainian officials now put the number at about 2,000, including roughly 50 children. There is no electricity or gas. Food and medicine have been severely restricted, and evacuation has not happened.

**People have been eating purslane.**

Anyone who has seen The Road will understand the atmosphere.

But here is the thing that bothers me.

Oleshky is not some isolated dot of misery that appeared last week because the United Nations General Assembly opened.

Ukrainian officials were raising the situation months ago. There were evacuation discussions in May. The UN human-rights monitoring mission reported in June that no food deliveries had reached Oleshky since May 26. Now the issue is being taken to the General Assembly.

Good.

About time.

But the people eating purslane did not suddenly become hungry because the General Assembly opened.

And Oleshky is not the only place on the map.

Kreminna. Debaltseve. Soledar. Popasna. Marinka. Krasnohorivka. Novohrodivka. Selydove. Ukrainsk. Vuhledar. Kurakhove. Polohy. Vasylivka. Kamianka-Dniprova. Hola Prystan. Kakhovka.

Cities.

Not villages on some abstract military map.

Cities with populations in the tens of thousands before the war. Places with schools and pharmacies and apartment blocks and bus stations and people who knew where to buy decent bread.

Some have been occupied for years. Some were occupied much more recently.

Their stories are not identical. Oleshky’s present catastrophe should not be flattened into a generic list.

But the map does something useful.

It restores the scale.

Oleshky is not the story.

Oleshky is a window.

And perhaps that is why the sudden attention to it feels so strange. The horror is real. The international attention is necessary. But the chronology is real too.

It is a chronicle of impotence.

JD Vance says the war will eventually be resolved.

> “Just a question of time.”

I suppose that is one way of looking at time.

Bakhtin might have had another.

Somewhere between Bakhtin and the Gap, I started thinking about complexity.

Vitebsk. The old journal. The carnivalesque.

Then Chara.

I went there in 1993, by helicopter from Chita, then the capital of Chitinskaya Oblast.

Chara is still there. It just isn’t in Chitinskaya Oblast anymore. Today it’s in Zabaykalsky Krai, whose capital is still Chita.

Same place. Different name.

I was leaving Moscow for Minsk.

Lukashenko had just been elected.

What a drag.

Thirty-two years later, Trump wants to buy Belarusian potash for the United States at a price he says would be substantially lower than what the U.S. currently pays Canada.

Last week Belarus released 25 political prisoners in exchange for U.S. sanctions relief.

And yesterday Trump put the potash version of the arrangement on Truth Social:

> “The United States is working on a massive Deal with respect to the purchase of Potash from Belarus. The pricing would be for substantially less than we are currently paying to Canada, very good news for our Farmers and Ranchers.”

Belarusian President Alexander Lukashenko said yesterday that Belarus has little potash available for the United States this year because its 2026 output is already under contract.

The carnival continues.

The places remain where they were.

And then there is diesel.

Trump: stop hitting the refineries.

Fire Point: we’re hitting the refineries.

Moscow refinery: actually stopped.

The SBU said Ukrainian forces struck the Moscow refinery on September 20. Fire Point said its FP-1 drones were involved; Reuters reported that both primary crude-distillation units caught fire and that repairs could take several weeks.

According to the Financial Times, “diesel” came up repeatedly in Trump’s call with Z on Sunday: “diesel, diesel ... diesel.”

Then Trump posted his own diagnosis on Truth Social:

“Russia has unfortunately lost control of its Diesel Oil Industry due to its War with Ukraine.”

So there we are.

Fire Point is hitting Russian oil infrastructure.

Trump is worrying about diesel.

And the Moscow refinery actually stopped.

Russia is losing refining capacity.

America is looking for cheaper Belarusian potash.

Lukashenko is releasing prisoners in exchange for sanctions relief.

Z is meeting Trump in New York.

Oleshky is eating purslane.

And somewhere in the background the Suwałki Gap is sitting exactly where it was 35 years ago.

Dialogue. Carnival. Chronotope.[1](#footnote-1)
