So, yesterday’s attack complicated an already bad situation. CHP-5, the central heating and electricity plant, isn’t pumping out enough heat and electricity to provide for Kyiv districts on the western side (Right Bank) of the Dnipro. This has led to unscheduled emergency blackouts, even for critical infrastructure, closure of supermarkets, suspension of electrified bus/trolley service, et cetera = misery for hundreds of thousands, what we whined about on Day 1416[1](#footnote-1).

[Image]

*Freezing our way to victory! (Photo c/o abdymok , made to look colder c/o ai. January 13, 2026)*

And I didn’t even mention the situation on the Left Bank, which is even worse. **The January 9th strikes knocked out power to 70% of Kyiv, with Moscow’s stated aim to “disconnect the city” entirely according to Ukrenergo.** Some neighborhoods on have been without reliable electricity for days now—emergency services have graciously set up warming tents with hot food stations, because apparently we’ve time-traveled back to the siege warfare era. Z calls them “Points of Invincibility”—complete with hot drinks, charging stations, and a warm welcome. Nothing says invincibility quite like huddling in a tent for basic human warmth. **In December, Kyiv residents averaged a delightful 9.5 hours without electricity per day.**

[Image]

*Photo c/o abdymok (January 12, 2026)*

**The timing is exquisite: daytime temperatures have dropped to -12°C, and as low as -19°C at night.** As Peter notes in his [Atlantic Council](https://www.atlanticcouncil.org/) piece (presumably written from his own igloo), **the bombing campaign was deliberately timed to coincide with the coldest period in over a year, with temps plummeting to minus fifteen Celsius**[2](#footnote-2)**.** “They deliberately waited for freezing weather to make things worse,” Z observed. Strategic genius, really. People are sleeping in thermal underwear, ski clothes, and multiple layers, huddling together in the same bed like some dystopian camping trip nobody signed up for. Without electricity, there’s no water (pumps don’t work), no cooking (electric stoves), no heat in many buildings. Elevators are dead, trapping elderly residents in their apartments. **Some villages on the outskirts have been without power for four days straight and have blocked roads in protest.**

[Image]

*Combined Heat and Power Plant \#5 (Photo c/o abdymok , January 12, 2026)*

It’s warmer if you keep moving, but being in perpetual motion burns a lot of calories and people get tired. **Over 700,000 consumers lost power in the past week before it was gradually restored.** The attacks on January 9th were described as “one of the most massive attacks on the capital’s energy infrastructure,” with Deputy Energy Minister Mykola Kolisnyk helpfully noting that **Russia is “going all in” to destroy Ukraine’s power system, launching attacks just five days apart.** The intention is crystal clear—as one resident cheerfully noted, “they’re trying to freeze us.” At least they waited for the cold snap.

**The buzz of generators has become the background noise of winter**—that is, for those lucky enough to afford them. Portable gas stoves are the hot new accessory. Mayor Klitschko has already urged residents who can leave to do so, presumably to less-affected areas where “power and heating are more readily available,” which is a delightfully optimistic way of describing “anywhere else.” Peter notes fears are mounting over a possible winter exodus to neighboring EU countries, which may be exactly what Putin has in mind. **A recent UN probe concluded Moscow’s actions amount to crimes against humanity: “murder and forcible transfer of population.”** But sure, let’s keep calling it a “special military operation.”

The last message as of this writing from [DTEK](https://t.me/dtek_ua/3740), our beloved monopoly energy supplier:

> *“The damage is serious, so emergency blackouts continue throughout Kyiv, as well as in the Brovary and Boryspil districts of the region. ‼️Remember, during emergency blackouts, schedules don’t apply. It’s hard for us and we’re very tired. But the work doesn’t stop for a minute. 629 DTEK crews are working around the clock. Thank you to everyone who supports energy workers in these difficult times. The lights are holding!”*

Well, “holding” is certainly one way to describe it. As building manager Oleksandr Matienko put it with admirable clarity: **“They are trying to kill us.”**

But as another resident told [The Guardian](http://theguardian.com), this just makes us tougher and more determined[3](#footnote-3). War amplifies who you are, and we’ve seen massive volunteering to help neighbors. So here we are, in our longjohns, being amplified.
