There is a sophisticated form of cruelty that flourishes far from war.

It does not bomb apartment buildings or leave craters in playgrounds. It sits at a respectable desk, reviews the available suffering, and explains to people living under missile attacks that their survival is evidence of sound strategic thinking.

This week, Ross Douthat offered [a polished example](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/05/05/opinion/trump-ukraine-iran.html).

[Image]

*c/o The New York Time s, May 5, 2026*

His argument, more or less, is that Donald Trump’s Ukraine policy has succeeded because America spends less, Europe spends more, and Ukraine continues fighting. Iran, meanwhile, is where things have become messy.

From a great distance, this probably looks very elegant.

One imagines arrows moving across maps. Budgets being rebalanced. Someone using the phrase “security architecture” over a good lunch.

The only inconvenience in this theory is Ukrainians themselves.

They keep appearing in hospitals without limbs, at funerals, in dark apartments during blackouts, in towns that no longer exist.

Still, even this can be absorbed into the framework. Their endurance becomes “resilience.” Their dead become proof that America has recalibrated successfully.

This is one of modern commentary’s more impressive tricks: converting another country’s catastrophe into evidence of responsible imperial bookkeeping.

We remain stubbornly unimpressed.

Missiles continue arriving without consulting columnists. Widows show little interest in burden-sharing debates. Children pulled from rubble rarely pause to admire geopolitical efficiency.

Analysis has its place. Countries have interests. Resources are finite.

But a person watching a city burn should be cautious before congratulating himself for discovering efficiency in the flames.
