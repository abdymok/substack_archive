**\[4:25\] — Conversation between Mindich and Umerov: cabinet personnel**

*The host notes this is July 8, 2025, before the cabinet reshuffle on July 17 and the NABU/SAPO raid on July 22. Umerov is recounting a conversation with the president and relaying it to Mindich.*

**Umerov:** Who do you see for that position? Okay. Why? It’s distrust.

*(Recounting the president’s words)* No, he’s loyal. What do you think?

**Mindich:** It’s an illusion. Well, what can you say, yes, but I’m not touching my own ideas.

**Umerov:** Look at the cabinet — it’s nothing. You ask for money, you can’t solve it.

**Mindich:** This is going to be a disaster. He’s not our man, not loyal to anything. He has his own interests. He’s friends with you-know-who, and everything works for him.

**Umerov:** Mindich, you understand — he’ll come for your seat tomorrow. What happens then? A person who won’t make any decisions with such a gap. It’ll just be bad for the country.

**Umerov:** I understand.

**Mindich:** Let him put Misha in.

**Umerov:** I understand, but he asked.

**Mindich:** No, Misha was pushing so hard — it’s more profitable to put him as First Deputy Prime Minister.

**Umerov:** Who?

**Mindich:** Misha.

**Umerov:** Yeah.

**\[6:28\] — On another candidate (likely Klymenko, then being discussed for Defense Minister)**

**Umerov:** Why didn’t you want this one?

**Mindich:** I said it would be... well, I don’t know why he didn’t want it. I just said that this...

**Umerov:** Is no better than Shmyhal.

**Mindich:** Okay, let’s go with him then. The principle is — whether it’s Shmyhal or this one — if the goal is to wreck things overnight, they can do it. They’re two geniuses, possibly geniuses.

**Mindich:** Neither of them — only you.

**\[7:47\] — On the ambassador to the United States**

*The host notes that at this time it was widely discussed that Oksana Markarova was leaving her post as ambassador to the US, and Umerov himself was considered the frontrunner for the replacement — right up until the last moment.*

**Umerov:** He says: “There are no people.” I say: and then he says: “There are a couple of candidates here, but mostly it’s Shmyhal talking.” I say: “But he’s not yours.” He says: “Well, who can do it?” I say: “Well, look for yourselves — if the decision is already made, no questions. I’m kind of a team player in this. If it’s needed, it’s needed. What are the tasks there?” He says: “It’s just a shame — nobody can get in.” Four people. Herman is good, Mindich is good.

**Umerov:** He’s the closest, in my understanding — mentally the closest.

**Mindich:** Yes, he’s understandable.

**Umerov:** Because he’s just like that right away. Second — this one, whoever he has there — Stefanyshyna.

**Mindich:** Well that’s Khomerov. She won’t be able to.

**Umerov:** She has a suspicion against her.

**Umerov:** Already, Mindich, I think. No, well that’s just basically a smokescreen.

**Umerov:** I’m saying the last mile — he says it won’t work, I say I hear you. And there was some third person too.

**Mindich:** There were three. Always you, Shmyhal, back and forth, and Yermak.

*The host notes that it was later reported in the media — including, he believes, NV — that Herman Halushchenko was genuinely being considered for the ambassador post to the US at that time.*

**\[11:52\] — On Yermak’s control and the ambassador role**

**Mindich:** He put you here from the start — and for him you’re comfortable. He wants to control this space 100%. Whatever happens there.

*The host notes the context here is likely Andriy Yermak placing a loyal person.*

**Umerov:** No extra moves, no extra information. That’s it. For a long time.

**Mindich:** We’ll take it back soon.

**Umerov:** Yeah, in a year. Fine. Do you need to negotiate or not — with serious people?

**Mindich:** Mm-hmm.

**Umerov:** Yes. In what capacity?

**Mindich:** Ambassador to America... or like advisor to the president, assistant to the president, or what?

**Umerov:** Something unclear.

**Mindich:** Well, in about 3 months, 2–3 months.

**Umerov:** The doctor is handling it.

**Mindich:** Yes, it takes a long time over there — bureaucracy, agrément. Then you sit and wait a month while he has to shake your hand. A month. Who will be meeting with the Russians? That’s the question.

**Mindich:** But the final decision came on Friday, Saturday — because before that he was still thinking.

**Mindich:** On Friday? No, on Friday still — Saturday. Around the weekend. Saturday evening, Friday. Andrey started knocking everyone else out.

**Umerov:** Why not you?

**\[17:00\] — “Project 23”**

*The host notes that “Project 23” has been a mysterious topic discussed by several investigative journalists — the hypothesis being that it was either a slush fund or a financing mechanism for various expenditures. He also shows a clip of Tsukerman visibly becoming nervous when asked about it by journalist Mykhailo Tkach.*

**Umerov:** One question about 23.

**Mindich:** Yes.

**Umerov:** Look — it’s more convenient for me there at two times or three.

**Mindich:** Two times.

**Umerov:** But remember, back then you said we don’t need Rheinmetall.

**Mindich:** And there’s nobody else.

**Umerov:** Right.

**Mindich:** They want Ukrainians to be involved.

**Umerov:** Yes.

**Mindich:** I told him, he says: “Well, make the decision.” He says: “You make the decision.”

**Umerov:** Maybe — look, there’s one option here.

**Mindich:** There’s one problem here.

**Umerov:** What is it?

**Mindich:** What happens when he gets in?

**Umerov:** He’ll say: “It’s me.”

**Mindich:** Well, of course.

**Umerov:** There’s this option. *(enters a code)* Is it registered to you, or better as a business — a joint venture. But you represent the interests.

**Mindich:** Won’t work.

**Umerov:** Won’t work. He’s scared.

**Mindich:** You know how much money he has?

**Umerov:** How much do you think he has?

**Umerov:** 200, at least.

**Mindich:** Cash.

**Umerov:** Cash — not counting apartments, cars, houses. You need that for...

**Mindich:** Where does it come from?

**Umerov:** From everywhere. Three from here, five from there. He has basically no expenses. He doesn’t bear any costs. He pays his driver 300 dollars — and even that, probably on the state’s tab.
