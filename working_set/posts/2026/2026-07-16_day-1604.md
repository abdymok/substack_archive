The Telegram message from Ukraine’s former defense minister [reads](https://t.me/zedigital/6882):

**What didn’t work out:**

1.  Completing the organizational transformation of the Ministry of Defense according to NATO standards and common sense. The new structure was launched, many people were let go, many processes were begun. But we needed to be more decisive in dismissing people who were slowing down the changes.

2.  Moving absolutely all procurement to a tender system.

3.  Building a culture of accountability for decisions made. I believe that it is precisely the heroic Ukrainian people who will shape this institutional culture.

Thank you to everyone, men and women alike, who is defending Ukraine and working toward victory.

Thank you to my entire team for their effective 24/7 service.
I especially thank my family for their patience.

Thank you all for your support!

I will continue working toward the mission I came to the Ministry of Defense with in the first place — to defeat the enemy through asymmetry, speed of innovation, and organizational strength.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Read plainly, it is a confession. Three admissions of insufficient decisiveness, from the man readers of [The Economist](https://archive.ph/tjTtC) would have named the most decisive person in the building. Which tells you less about Fedorov’s nerve than how little of the political apparatus around him was built to reward using it.

Z’s replacement is [Ihor Klymenko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ihor_Klymenko), the nation’s top cop, a man whose formative organizational experience appears to have been perfecting hospital corners in a barracks somewhere in Zhytomyr after the Soviet Union imploded. I hadn’t clocked that he’s still young enough for that biography to be true.

As we tried to argue [yesterday](https://abdymok.substack.com/p/day-1603), none of this changes very much. The ministry gets a new occupant. The building keeps its architecture. The Rada will presumably wave it all through today, hours after this goes out, in whatever theatrical register these confirmations get performed in — and none of the spectacle will touch the arithmetic underneath it.

Which brings us back to the general we left last night weighing his own alleged haul in kilograms. Nearly three years after Yuriy Shchyhol’s office was cleared out over sixty-two million hryvnia in software procurement — a case adjacent to, but distinct from, the drone-pricing scandal that also carries his name — the file has produced no verdict. It has produced seven separate attempts to open a preparatory hearing, and, this week, a finding from the High Anti-Corruption Court that the very technical documents prosecutors called improperly classified were in fact legitimately secret all along, which is not the sort of ruling that strengthens a corruption case. Meanwhile the general who was found, at his arrest, sitting on a small currency exhibition of euros and dollars is back in uniform, brigadier general rank intact, serving in military intelligence while his case loses altitude on procedural grounds rather than merit.

This is what accountability looks like inside a procurement system too byzantine to convict anyone in real time: the money gets weighed, the suspect gets detained for a night, bail gets posted, and then the file simply outlives everyone’s attention span. Nobody is acquitted. Nobody is convicted. People just change desks until the scandal ages out of memory. Shchyhol didn’t beat his case. He’s outlasting it.

In other news, [Hegseth plans to screen all US troops, including women, for low Testosterone](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/07/15/us/politics/hegseth-troops-testosterone.html).

Every American service member over thirty will be screened annually for low testosterone. Women included, treatment optional. The branding, naturally, was not optional: a “High-T Department of War.” Add it to the pile. Transgender troops banned. Women’s promotions blocked after the selection boards had already approved them. Beards gone. The flu vaccine mandate gone too, until an outbreak at basic training killed a recruit and the mandate came quietly back. This is a defense secretary curating virility for a force that, at the moment, is not fighting anyone.

So: one military stages a toughness it does not need, screening healthy men for a deficiency in order to sell them a drug and a slogan. The other has toughness on constant, involuntary demand, and a rising share of its draft-age men are removing fingers, forging documents, or swimming rivers to get out from under it. Hegseth’s manosphere is theatre. Ukraine’s is a court filing nobody wanted to make, from a state that ran out of volunteers years ago and still hasn’t found an honest way to ask for the rest.
