By then, with any luck, neither man will still hold public office. And we won't be dead.

Listened to an unsatisfying podcast w/ Z stenographer Simon Shuster.

Sigh.

It occurred to me that postliteracy arrived in Ukraine before the Americans found a word for it. In 2019 the candidate did not debate, did not read, did not need to. He governed by clip, the way a comedian governs a room — not by argument but by rhythm. The résumé was retired; only the face remained, warm and unaccountable. Z!Team supplied the punchlines, Z supplied the smile, and the electorate, starved of Hegel, voted for the closest thing to a laugh track.

It is not that reasoning failed. It is that reasoning was never on the ballot.

Seven years on, Simon’s magazine [explains](https://archive.ph/hJRqc#selection-761.0-1805.227) that television made politics into entertainment, and entertainment cannot be cross-examined. Kyiv already knew. We had merely mistaken the diagnosis for a punchline of our own — pink unicorns, 47 seconds, the war reduced to a demo reel. The dialectic did not lose the argument. It was never invited.

I listened to Shuster’s podcast hoping for a plan. Four and a half years into the largest land war in Europe since 1945, someone who has spent more time in the presidential bunker than any other Western journalist ought to know whether the man running the country has a theory of how it ends. Shuster came close. He built forty minutes of scaffolding toward the question — Z’s endgame, the privacy of his own mind — and then, right at the threshold, the podcast asked its listeners to subscribe. The answer to “does Z have a plan” was itself paywalled.

What Shuster gives us for free is not a plan but stagecraft. Z’s team, we’re told, worked hard — deliberately, consciously — to preserve the Churchillian image manufactured in the invasion’s first days, particularly for audiences abroad too far away to see the seams. The nightly address is not governance; it’s a running production. The bunker gym, the ping-pong table, the disciplined diet — Z himself understands physical fitness as part of the image. Even his health is cast, not lived. The Trump courtship followed the same logic: not “how do we end the war” but “how do we avoid being blamed for its continuation.” A positioning exercise, not a peace plan. Z hoped his own outsider-comedian-turned-president arc would resonate with Trump’s — casting, again, not statecraft.

So it goes: a license to build interceptors, a couple of years off, timed to outlast whoever’s still in office to take credit or blame. A village vaporized, folded into the same feed as the summit handshake. Z is not waiting for victory or negotiating toward peace. He is performing continuity — competent, disciplined, camera-ready continuity — until some other force, battlefield or ballot box or mortality, writes the ending for him. So it goes[1](#footnote-1).
