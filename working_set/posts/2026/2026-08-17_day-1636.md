Hours earlier, Moscow demolished what remained of the Pochaina outdoor flea market in Obolonsky district. It is becoming increasingly difficult to argue that three ballistic-missile strikes — June 15, July 30 and August 16 — are simply coincidence. The BBC investigates, [without success](https://www.bbc.com/news/videos/c8dnn05e90lo).

The big news today is how Z intends to resolve the mess he created a month ago when he sacked the government, leaving the country without a defense minister or foreign minister.

The move upset the cardboard kids, who have spent the past month demonstrating outside the Franko Drama Theatre, across from the President’s Office.

A thousand or so people marched from the Philharmonic to the square yesterday evening. There will be many more this evening and tomorrow.

Z’s parade of disgraced defense ministers — Taran, Reznikov, Umerov, Shmyhal, Fedorov — springs to mind, along with the tidy sum of more than \$300 billion in military, economic and humanitarian largesse Ukraine has graciously accepted from its foreign benefactors since February 2022.

Here in the land of borshch-slurping never-surrender patriots, where Z’s flunkies have monetized the brand with official [Fire Point reels](https://www.instagram.com/firepoint.ua/), foreign digital creators such as Laura Loomer and Caolan Robertson are still peddling Chernobyl-kissed berries and mushrooms.

Robertson’s [sit-down](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eFZ3-nZPCu0&t=2s) with Fedorov over the weekend reminded me of a hostage video where the hostage has been in captivity long enough to start enjoying it.

Not the interrogation kind. The other kind, where the captor has discovered that comfort is a more efficient leash than fear, and the hostage has discovered that performing gratitude is easier than resisting.

Ask him, “Who is Vova?” and he goes all stiff.

“I’d rather not reveal all the details of our conversations.”

The sound of a man declining to name the hand on his shoulder.

Ask him about the missing defense ministry — the one he isn’t running, the one nobody is running — and he offers: “I hope one will be appointed in the near future.”

As though the appointment were weather rather than a decision Z has simply declined to make for a month.

This is the loyalty-under-erasure register in its purest form: total discretion about the man who fired him, total fluency about the war he no longer has a portfolio to fight.

Then the register shifts, and Fedorov becomes messianic.

Ask him what comes next and you get: “We will find gamechangers.” “People won’t be ashamed of what they saw in our team.” “We won’t lose our ability to generate ideas for our country’s development.”

A man promising redemption in the passive voice, naming no office, no plan, no date.

It is the Z house style exactly, stripped of even the pretense of specificity: hope as a product you keep manufacturing regardless of whether anyone ordered it.

And then, because Robertson apparently ran out of real questions with roughly a third of the interview still to go, several unbothered minutes on Elon Musk.

Four years of “communication.” Ideologically complicated but productive. Fedorov declining to specify what exactly Musk gets out of it beyond the two of them “finding understanding, even in difficult situations.”

Palantir’s [Karp got the same treatment](https://abdymok.substack.com/p/day-1542?utm_source=publication-search) in May — described in the tone reserved for a generous uncle rather than a vendor selling a 22-point manifesto about which cultures are “dysfunctional and regressive.”

Fedorov, [as Day 1542 noted](https://abdymok.substack.com/p/day-1542?utm_source=publication-search), is the delivery mechanism for that manifesto — the profile through which the ideology arrives as innovation rather than argument.

None of this illuminates anything about the missing ministries or the kids outside the Franko Drama Theatre.
