Yesterday morning we wrote about Valentyn, who knew nothing about physics but everything about how power operates in the shadow of empire[1](#footnote-1). His insight — that information lives in the correlations, not the parts — rhymes, as we noted, with John Preskill’s description of quantum systems: you cannot read them sequentially. Page by page, there is nothing. The signal is in the relations. ([Gerardus ‘t Hooft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerard_%27t_Hooft), it should be said, would find this entire framing irritating — his cellular automaton interpretation holds that quantum mechanics is *deterministic* at depth, that the probabilism is ours, not nature’s. The metaphor is contested even among the physicists. We are using it anyway.)

We meant it about memory and entanglement. But it applies, with grim directness, to the negotiations currently determining whether Ukraine survives as a state.

I thought about this after a cup of coffee at ZigZag with Ruslan, who told me that a printing house in Salerno, Italy had agreed to print a limited edition anthology of his works, a compilation, as it were, of five books, including Wartime Sketches[2](#footnote-2)[3](#footnote-3).

The picture is not readable part by part. Each element — a broken Rada vote, a stalled IMF benchmark, a deputy muttering that “there are no votes” in a presidential meeting — looks like noise in isolation. The correlation is the signal. And the correlation says: the system is closer to collapse than the probabilistic language of Ukraine experts is willing to admit. (This thought arrived, with some force, while walking past [Puncher Fight Club](https://puncher.kiev.ua/pechersk) on the way to [Dodo Socks](https://dodosocks.com/en/)’ flagship store in central Kyiv. The city has a way of concentrating the mind.)

The negotiations have calcified around a single question: will Ukraine withdraw from the portions of Donetsk oblast it still holds. Everything else — energy deals, humanitarian corridors, ceasefire geometry — exists primarily to generate the appearance of momentum. Trump’s golf partner returns to this point regardless of what else is raised. The Russians barely need to show up. The golf partner is doing their arguing for them.

Z is not passive in this. On Sunday he announced that Ukrainian military intelligence holds irrefutable evidence of Russia supplying Iran with signals data — effectively telling Washington: we are useful to you in the Iran adventure, too. It is a desperate move. Though one should note that neither man has distinguished himself as an astute consumer of military intelligence. Z spent six months in 2022 dismissing American warnings of an imminent invasion as destabilizing noise. Trump’s relationship with his own intelligence services has been, to put it generously, transactional. Two leaders who have each, in their own way, looked at the signal and seen static.

The fuzziness here is not fundamental. It is epistemic. The American model has too few variables: it excludes the military logic (commanders holding high ground in Donetsk cannot be told that open steppe is strategically equivalent), the political logic (a parliament that cannot pass IMF benchmarks will not ratify a territorial referendum), and the economic logic (European unity on EUR 90 billion in credit is, to be charitable, unverified).

Z is already planning for three more years of war. Not as ambition. As contingency. The parliament is being quietly restructured for what one insider recently called, with exhausted irony, “survival mode.”

The stalemate is not a stable equilibrium. It only looks like one read part by part. Read the correlations and the position is already determined.

Someone in Kyiv knows which way. They just can’t say it yet.
