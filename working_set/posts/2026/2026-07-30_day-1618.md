I was listening to Fedorov’s 96-minute exit interview for the second time at 2 in the morning, when no one yet knew the trajectory of the 50 or so cruise missiles crossing the border. *Vision. Plan. Structure.* Those words came up again and again. Antiballistics did not come up once — not even in passing — despite being the single word Z used most insistently [with Sky News the same week](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-HypRv9GHuI&t=23s), his one unambiguous ask of Washington and London. Yermak’s name surfaced, arms-lobby names surfaced — but 96 minutes in, nobody asked the one question that would have actually mattered:

Who is “Vova”?

[Video: https://youtu.be/WbjYc4-OuT0]

Fedorov, for his part, offers his own tidy account of why he was really sacked — procurement reform, wounded interests, a system that couldn’t tolerate transparency. [It is not the account of Z,](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-HypRv9GHuI&t=23s) who stayed entirely upstream of all that: two institutions failing to coordinate, a president forced to choose. Two men, two stories, and neither one touches the name that would make either version legible.

Which reminds me of the disbandment of Det A.

Substack subscribers know that I’ve been following the “Lighting up the Battlefield” saga since before 1984, when Detachment A was [disbanded](https://detachment-a.org/), and at least as doggedly for the now [deactivated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asymmetric_Warfare_Group) Asymmetric Warfare Group. It’s all been hush, hush, hush, but that’s why the story remains so interesting and relevant, now with crypto twists and beer from Lida. — [Day 41, Phase 2](https://abdymok.substack.com/publish/posts/detail/53500960/share-center?alreadyPublished=true). *Ruska Lozova op begins* (May 5, 2022)
