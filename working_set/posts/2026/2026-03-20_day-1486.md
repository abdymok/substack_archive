*Imminence in America’s underpants*

More than four years ago — exactly 20 days before Russia launched its campaign to decapitate Kyiv — we tried to draw a line between *imminent* and *inevitable*. It was a modest effort, and it failed. But thousands of people read that post, which the woman claiming to be [KonMari](https://konmari.com/?srsltid=AfmBOorz-J2dvxb0GuJZBtiSHAcmoXnooeMRHeZKxCczqJu_eevt2zcT) later placed behind a paywall, hoping to draw more founding members into this Substack experiment[1](#footnote-1).

Running early this morning down the *Alley of Friends* along Kyiv's western embankment of the Dnipro River, I thought: *We are back.* New country, new war, same two words. A leader ignoring his intelligence community. The result, again, is literal. A third word has now joined them.

A small observation. Words do not contain meaning; they trigger it. *Imminent* is especially excitable. It has now triggered, in sequence: the invasion of Ukraine, a war with Iran, and a certain unease in the crossed eyes of Kash, Tulsi, John and “Rainman,” DIA’s new [clueless](https://greydynamics.com/dia-director-james-adams-iii-new-era-for-us-intelligence/) boss.

In February 2022, the White House warned publicly that Russia’s invasion was *imminent*—that is, soon, very soon, possibly before you finish this sentence. Z, advised by men who treated translation as a decorative art, heard *inevitable*—which is to say, abstract, negotiable, not necessarily Tuesday. He waved off the warning as one waves off an elderly relative who has mistaken the weather for prophecy. The intelligence was good. The judgment was not. The invasion, when it came, declined to be metaphorical.

Now Iran. Donald Trump, who Anne Applebaum [suggests](https://archive.ph/wQqVh) does not think strategically, historically, or with sustained care, launched strikes on a country his own intelligence community assessed as not building a nuclear weapon. He explained this with a “good feeling.” One hesitates to file this under methodology.

On Wednesday, his Director of National Intelligence was asked whether Iran posed an imminent threat. She replied, with admirable economy, that defining *imminent* is not her job. It is the president’s. She supervises some 80,000 people who produce judgments. One must respect the division of labor.

Her written testimony said Iran had made “no efforts” to rebuild its nuclear infrastructure. Her oral testimony suggested it was “trying to recover” that same infrastructure. Mark Warner noted the discrepancy. She did not contest him with enthusiasm.

John, more practiced in Washington’s bilingualism, offered that Iran has been a *constant* threat, long watered and carefully tended by previous administrations. This is the analytical equivalent of explaining that the fire predated one’s arrival with gasoline.

Let us pause on *constant*. We now have three words: *imminent*, *inevitable*, *constant*. They are not synonyms. A constant threat is durable, familiar, almost comforting in its predictability. It was there last year, and the year before, and on [Day 1465](https://abdymok.substack.com/p/day-1465) of Russia’s war, when American envoys discussed Iran in the morning and Ukraine after lunch. The rooms were nearby. Efficiency has its moments.

A constant threat is neither new nor urgent. It does not, by itself, justify a war begun on a feeling. Ratcliffe’s statement, read slowly, argues against the war while appearing to support it. His analysts knew this. They were spared the inconvenience of saying so.

The Strait of Hormuz is now obstructed. Oil rises. The Navy recalculates. NATO allies—tariffed, mocked, and periodically threatened with annexation—decline to assist. In the Kremlin, Vladimir Putin observes events with professional curiosity and notes, correctly, that burning cities are transferable.

The symmetry is plain. Z discounted good intelligence because he preferred his judgment. Donald Trump discounted it because he prefers his feelings. One was overconfident, the other, less encumbered. Both paid. Others paid more.

In both cases, we are told only the leader can decide what is *imminent*. Not the analysts. Not the professionals. Not the 80,000 employees nominally employed for that purpose. Only the leader—then and now. This has been stated plainly, even under oath.

It is, in a modest, technical sense, insane.

[Fazil Iskander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fazil_Iskander) wrote that real humor requires approaching the edge of pessimism, peering into the abyss, convincing oneself there is nothing there, and then walking back. The footsteps on the return journey are the joke.

I have looked. The labeling is accurate.

Not imminent. Not constant. Inevitable. Stupid.
