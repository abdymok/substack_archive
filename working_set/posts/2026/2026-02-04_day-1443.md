Simon Shuster’s *Atlantic* piece opens with Kyiv freezing and closes with inevitability[1](#footnote-1). Putin is relentless, winter is weaponized, Ukrainians endure. It’s polished, moving—and useless. If you’re walking on ice blocks because the mayor clears streets while presidentially-appointed district heads ignore sidewalks, you don’t need atmosphere. You need to know why the system guaranteeing your misery was designed exactly this way.

[Image]

*Photo c/o abdymok (February 4, 2026)*

This is the dogfooding loop Kyiv perfected: municipal workers reinstall bicycle barriers monthly while infrastructure decays. Activity signals competence; repetition guarantees contracts. Nothing improves because improvement ends the game.

Scale that to your apartment building. Darnytsia district contracted insulation for 119 buildings at UAH 254 million in 2025. Investigators found 20% price inflation—UAH 46 million siphoned. Inflate contracts, pocket margins, leave you freezing, repeat. You’re not cold because Putin is relentless. You’re cold because someone made 46 million while you waited for insulation[2](#footnote-2).

The energy sector runs bigger numbers. Operation Midas audio from September 2025 captured participants discussing kickbacks on protective structures for facilities Russia would destroy months later. \$100 million gone while Russia prepared winter strikes. Investigation went public November 2025. Shuster filed February 2026. He mentions none of this.

Instead meet Maksym Bakhmatov, Darnytsia head, described as “former TV producer” from “the same comedy circuit as Z.” Omitted: Z’s Presidential Office appointed him May 2024. District heads serve presidential pleasure, not voters. When Bakhmatov admits “zero generators, zero food,” he’s describing his own chain of command’s performance. You got a comedian managing quarter-million people through winter war. Athletic prowess, comedic timing—essential qualifications for heating infrastructure, apparently.

Desniansky district relies on one thermal power plant. Fourth winter of war. Everyone knew Russia targets energy infrastructure. Instead of redundancy: CHP-6 and hope. Instead of protective structures: kickback negotiations. Instead of experienced administrators: loyalty appointments managing your frozen pipes.

Here’s your Squid Game: the elected mayor clears streets, Z’s appointed district heads clear sidewalks. Coordination fails, you walk on ice, nobody’s accountable because authority splits between democratic and appointed structures. When gaps freeze over literally, officials shrug. System works perfectly assuming the point was never heat but contract flow.

Key Operation Midas player: Timur Mindich, Kvartal 95 co-owner, Z’s close friend. Audio recordings caught him (call sign: “Karlsson”) discussing percentages with Energy Minister Halushchenko (”Professor”)—debating whether 10% or 15% was appropriate for protective structure contracts while Russia positioned for winter offensive. Infrastructure protection funds became commission structures.

You freeze, they debate margins[3](#footnote-3).

War explains the missile. It doesn’t explain why hardening funds became repair contracts, why protective structures weren’t built, why redundancy never materialized, why accountability froze before pipes did. None of this appears in Shuster, [NBC’s Kyiv coverage](https://www.nbcnews.com/world/ukraine/kyiv-freezing-dark-russian-strikes-leave-ukraines-capital-powerless-rcna254359), [Tim Mak’s humane reporting](https://www.counteroffensive.news/p/how-ukraines-homeless-are-faring), or Euronews’ [empathy quiz](https://www.euronews.com/my-europe/2026/02/02/watch-the-video-kyiv-is-freezing-would-you-survive-in-20c-without-heating) asking viewers whether they could survive −20°C without heating—governance failure repackaged as resilience porn.

You don’t need proof winter is cruel or civilians resilient. You need journalists explaining how known risks went unmitigated, how money moved, how appointments were made, why failure wasn’t just predictable but profitable.

Freezing isn’t fate.
