Z announced the influence operation against Putin on June 25 with a stated 40-day span. So today, running the count forward, we’re wrapping up the second week of the adventure.

The op itself, as we framed it back on [Day 1590](https://abdymok.substack.com/p/day-1590), is a number attached to no declared content — a countdown offered less to Moscow, which hasn’t dignified it with comment, than to Trump, who reportedly enjoys boldness. Forty days bought is forty days he stops asking whether the underlying problem is fixable at all.

[Image]

The munitions depot in Vyshneve, in Kyiv region, belonged to one of the enterprises of [Ukroboronprom](https://ukroboronprom.com.ua/). On 6 July, during a massive Russian strike, it caught fire, then began detonating, in that order, as such things do. Nine people died. This is a fact, and facts, in this country, tend to arrive well after the funerals they describe.

Z was asked about it on July 9. [He answered by voice message](https://interfax.com.ua/news/general/1183781.html), which is itself a small monument to the age. There is a criminal case, he said. Guilty parties will be held responsible. There will, without doubt, be dismissals at Ukroboronprom, since the depot belonged to one of its enterprises. The sentence has the shape of accountability without containing any.

The strike itself came the night before, on July 6, and the explosions that followed were not modest. The Kyiv Regional Military Administration counted nine dead. A spokeswoman for the State Emergency Service reported that rescuers were still working through the aftermath at thirty-five separate locations, which is the kind of number that tells you more than any statement does.

The Ministry for Communities and Territories Development counted 280 damaged residential buildings — 253 private houses, 27 apartment blocks. The government will send more than UAH 3 billion (about \$67 million) from the state budget’s reserve fund to deal with the consequences. Reserve funds, in Ukraine, are where consequences go to be paid for after the fact.

Andriy Lykhoviy, spokesman for the General Staff, told [Interfax-Ukraine](https://interfax.com.ua/news/general/1183781.html) this week that the site where the explosions occurred does not belong to the Armed Forces’ sphere of management and is not subordinate to them. A useful sentence, in its way — it draws a boundary around responsibility with great precision, right at the moment responsibility might otherwise have had somewhere to land.

**Ukrainian Words of the Week**

- **Вечорниці (vechornytsi)** — Not “party.” Youth gathered in the evenings to sing, embroider, joke, smoke pot, court each other, and dance — a single word doing the work of an entire social institution, one that happened to also produce a fair number of marriages.

- **Рушник (rushnyk)** — Not “towel,” or only barely. An embroidered ritual cloth that accompanies a person from birth through marriage to burial — the thing a couple stands on during a wedding, the thing used to bless children, the thing that greets honored guests bearing bread and salt. Every pattern and color carries meaning most people quietly know and never explain. Untranslated into other languages for the same reason a word for a specific bone in the body doesn’t translate as “bone.”

- **Щем (shchem)** — Maybe the hardest of the three. A single feeling that braids together a light ache, nostalgia, tenderness, longing, and warm memory all at once — the emotional equivalent of a chord rather than a note. Other languages can hit individual overtones. None has one word for the whole chord.
