*CNN , August 12, 2026*

Every civilization without a written account of its own beginnings invented one. Myths of origin are not lies. They are what grows where evidence should be. We still do this. We have historians now, and journalists, and podcast hosts with ring lights. The evidence is not scarce. The myth wins anyway.

Z has become an origin myth with unusually good video coverage.

Politician, wartime leader, hero, symbol. At each stage the man loses a little and the sentiment gains it. By the symbol stage you are not describing a person. You are maintaining an installation. The maintenance crew gets nervous when someone points at the plumbing.

Twenty-five narrators, five trades: witnesses, interpreters, celebrities, insiders, legitimizers. I saw him. I know what it means. Feel this. I knew him from inside. He belongs among statesmen. None of them need agree. None of them need know each other. The jobs converge on the same figure without coordination, the way ants build a structure none of them designed.

[Image]

Almost all of them are foreigners. Two exceptions: Yuliia Mendel, Oleksiy Arestovych. The Ukrainian journalists who cover Z tend to be less comfortable in his presence than that. Yuriy Butusov’s 2021 quarrel with him is the specimen to keep on the shelf: no heroic interpretation offered, just a politician being made to answer for himself.

The foreign narrator meets the symbol. The Ukrainian journalist meets the administration. Symbols have one advantage administrations don’t: they never have to explain themselves.

Here is the mechanism, caught mid-operation. An outside expert is asked about the latest defense minister fired amid corruption allegations. The answer arrives in two parts. Part one: Z inherited a system rotten with Soviet residue, didn’t build it, has spent the war managing it. Fine. Part two: Z protects the rot and removes whoever threatens it. These cannot both be true at full strength. If the rot explains the firing, Z is a man fighting the system. If Z’s choice explains the firing, the rot stopped being the subject three sentences ago. Nobody notices the furniture has moved.

That’s what myth is for. It absorbs contradictions that would break an ordinary argument. The payment comes in the oldest currency there is: absolve the founder, blame the rot. The rot is ancient, structural, Soviet, someone else’s fault by definition. The founder stays exceptional. What goes missing is the one specific Tuesday when one specific man made one specific decision.

Patterns are less trouble than myths. Watch what happens when a subordinate gets a source of power that doesn’t run through the Presidential Office. Zaluzhny. Fedorov. Whoever’s next. No curse required. Just attention.

The machinery that turns a wartime president into a symbol was never his alone. It belongs to everyone standing around him with a camera or a memoir contract, narrating him upward, one testimonial at a time, until the symbol is finished and they’ve become its unpaid staff.

Spend enough time maintaining a symbol and the plumbing starts to look like sabotage.
