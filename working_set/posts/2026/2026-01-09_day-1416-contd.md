For elderly Kyivites and families with young kids—roughly 3 million souls in a city that once held 3.7 million—this is catastrophe. Ukraine’s population has hemorrhaged to 37-38 million from pre-war estimates of 41-43 million. We’re getting smaller, colder, and darker.

Russia’s gift? Ballistic missiles, IRBMs, including their Oreshnik toy that hit Lviv last night, and Shahed UAVs raining down on our infrastructure. The Kremlin loves its reflexive control campaigns—threaten the West, terrorize Ukrainians, rinse, repeat.

President Z—the one who didn’t warn us about the imminent inevitable invasion, who’s overseen corruption in state financing for energy facility protection and corrupt backoffices across the economy—reports: 242 drones last night, 13 ballistic missiles targeting energy and civilian infrastructure, one Oreshnik intermediate-range ballistic missile, 22 cruise missiles. Four dead in Kyiv alone, including an ambulance worker. Dozens wounded. One residential building hit twice—the second strike landing while emergency services responded to the first. Twenty residential buildings damaged in Kyiv and the region. “The attack came precisely when significant cold weather arrived. Precisely against the ordinary lives of ordinary people.”

A Russian drone also damaged the Qatari Embassy—the very country that mediates with Russia to free Ukrainian POWs and civilians held in Russian prisons. Classy.

Z wants “a clear reaction from the world. First and foremost from the United States, which Russia actually pays attention to.” He’s calling the Energy Staff together for a meeting on restoration timelines, necessary equipment, and responsible parties. Maximum is being done to restore heat and electricity to people. Air defense support for Ukraine remains “a constant priority. Not a single day can be lost in deliveries, production, or agreements.”

Sound familiar? Welcome to Berlin, 1948. The Soviet blockade began June 24, 1948, cutting off all road, rail, and water access to West Berlin’s 2.5 million residents. The U.S. and UK organized a massive airlift—planes landing every few minutes at peak—supplying food and fuel for 15 months (462 days) until September 30, 1949. A defining Cold War moment. A major propaganda victory for the West.

Except this time there’s no airlift coming, just Britain dropping off a dozen air defense systems and two prototype Gravehawks. And even if planes were coming, Ukraine’s airports haven’t functioned since Day 1.

Mayor Klitschko: Half of Kyiv’s apartment buildings—nearly 6,000—are without heat. Water supply interrupted citywide. Utility workers have connected hospitals and maternity wards to mobile boilers. City services operating in emergency mode. The mayor urges residents who can temporarily leave to do so.

They’re draining the heating systems now. Without draining, pipes burst and the system is destroyed. The Kyiv City Military Administration helpfully notes this is “a standard technical measure at sub-zero temperatures and does not mean prolonged absence of heat.” They drain even for outages lasting hours—it’s safety protocol. So, nothing to worry about. Except everything.

Within days, hundreds of thousands won’t be able to stay in their apartments. Zeroing out Putin’s cold terror calculations is doable, but a gigantic pain in the ass.

Meanwhile, Foreign Minister Sybiha announces Z is ready to visit the U.S. next week to discuss security guarantees. If there’s a signal from the American side. The final decision depends on Washington’s position. Nothing says “allied solidarity” like waiting by the phone while your capital freezes.

Weather forecast: Snow through January 11, temperatures -6°C to 1°C. Mid-month “warming” to 7-12°C. Yellow warning for ice.

ISW reports Russia’s forming artillery brigades they can’t staff. The Kremlin rejected Western security guarantees—Zakharova called peacekeepers “legitimate targets.” Russia wants neutrality, demilitarization, puppet government. Their 2022 war aims, unchanged.

I didn’t shave this morning. We live on the 8th floor of a modern building in a swanky district. Doesn’t help.

Berlin held out for 462 days on planes landing every few minutes. We’re on day 1,416.

The difference? Truman, not Trump, was in charge and Berliners knew the world was watching.
