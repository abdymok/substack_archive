A chunk of Russian bomb fell outside our office door on Sunday. Nobody noticed it immediately. For hours it just lay on the pavement—fragment of a cruise missile or anti-aircraft ordnance, still hot from being fired at the city—until the garbage truck picked it up. Just another piece of garbage. Just another fact of Kyiv in May 2026.

We pondered this situation on [Day 1545](https://abdymok.substack.com/p/day-1545), where we argued that in wartime, deontological and utilitarian ethics converge. Truth-telling becomes both a duty and a practical necessity. And [the following day](https://abdymok.substack.com/p/day-1546-c5b) we watched as this convergence broke down entirely, as respectable people retreated into procedural evasion precisely when procedure was least trustworthy.

But the bomb fragment on the pavement is the better illustration.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

There is an argument that seems designed precisely for understanding what is happening in Ukraine right now. Supervenience does not imply reducibility. Yes, high-level facts supervene on low-level facts. Yes, everything about Ukrainian military adaptation and state capacity is necessarily determined by billions of low-level facts—individual decisions, resource flows, institutional choices. But supervenience doesn’t mean you can *explain* the high level by reducing it to the low level. The levels have “explanatory autonomy.”

[The Atlantic tells us](https://www.theatlantic.com/ideas/2026/05/putin-lost-control-russia/687269/) that Russia is losing the war. [The Institute for the Study of War documents](https://understandingwar.org/research/russia-ukraine/russian-offensive-campaign-assessment-may-25-2026/) Ukraine’s military adaptation at scale. [TIME Magazine celebrates](https://time.com/article/2026/05/23/how-ukraine-found-the-cards-to-win-without-help-from-the-u-s-/) Ukrainian ingenuity and drone capability. All of this is true at the macro level. The strategic facts are real. Ukraine is finding asymmetric advantages. The drones are working.

But can you explain what is happening to Ukraine by reducing it to these high-level strategic facts? Can you derive the actual conditions of actual soldiers from the macro narrative? Can you *reduce* the experience of people trying to evacuate from Kostiantynivka to the statement “Ukraine is winning”?

No.

And that’s where the problem begins.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

This is what it sounds like when a serious person operates entirely at the macro level.

Finland’s President Alexander Stubb recently [assessed](https://yle.fi/a/74-20227651/64-3-300219?utm_source=chatgpt.com) Ukraine’s military situation:

> “Nyt se on puhdasta matematiikkaa."

He noted that the casualty ratio is approximately one Ukrainian soldier for every eight Russian soldiers, and that support for the war is declining in Russia.

Pure mathematics, he said. One to eight. A ratio. This is not a lie. The casualty statistics are real. The trend is real. Ukraine is indeed in a stronger position than it has been.

But what is it *reducing*?

The mathematics supervenes on low-level facts. One Ukrainian soldier. Eight Russian soldiers. But what supervenes upward from those numbers? What does it mean to be that one Ukrainian soldier? What are they fighting for? Who is allocating their supplies? Who is enriching themselves through the management of their unit?

The macro-level assessment cannot answer these questions because it has evacuated the micro level. “Pure mathematics” is deliberately indifferent to the apartment dinner, to the procurement system, to what is actually happening at the level where soldiers live and die and where corruption advances at the same pace as adaptation.

This is not because Stubb is lying. It is because he is operating at a level of description where such things are invisible. Where you can celebrate a ratio while remaining entirely indifferent to whether the system producing that ratio is being hollowed from within.

And this is the problem with explanatory autonomy taken to its logical extreme: it permits you to tell a true story at one level while being completely oblivious to what is actually happening at another.

This matters for Ukraine because the country is being described at multiple levels simultaneously, and these descriptions are increasingly in tension.

**At the macro level:** Ukraine is adapting. Its drone program is devastating Russian infrastructure. The soldiers are surviving. The state is building capacity.

**At the micro level:** [A minister invites a procurement official to dinner in a private apartment.](https://abdymok.substack.com/p/day-1546-c5b) A businessman interested in defense contracts is present. Months later, that businessman is purchasing stakes in companies receiving state contracts worth billions. The soldiers on the Road of Life are being managed by people who are simultaneously enriching themselves through that management.

The temptation is to say: the micro level is just “the way things work” in a stressed state. The macro level is what matters—Ukraine is winning, strategically speaking.

But our framework suggests this is precisely backwards. You cannot explain what is actually happening at the macro level by reducing it to strategic statements alone. The low-level facts—the apartment dinner, the billion-dollar stock purchase, the procurement system being hollowed from within—these supervene upward. They shape what “military adaptation” actually means when it is being managed by people with corrupting incentives.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

The bomb fragment on the pavement is both.

At the macro level, it is nothing—one fragment among thousands. The war continues. Russia is losing. Ukraine adapts.

At the micro level, it is everything—it landed outside our door. It could have arrived through the window. It landed at a specific coordinate at a specific time and happened not to kill anyone this time.

You cannot derive one description from the other. The strategic truth does not make the tactical threat disappear. The tactical reality does not invalidate the strategic assessment. But they are incommensurable.

And the people making decisions about procurement, about resource allocation, about who gets what contract—they are choosing which level to inhabit. They are living at a level where strategic victory is compatible with personal enrichment. Where corruption can advance at the same pace as adaptation. Where an apartment dinner about body armor can coexist with celebrations of institutional coherence.

This is not because the low level reduces to the high level. It is precisely because it doesn’t. Because there is explanatory autonomy at each level. Because you can tell a true story at the macro level that completely elides what is happening at the micro level.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

We are troubled less by the fact of corruption (that is eternal) than by the fact that respectable analysts can celebrate military adaptation while remaining entirely indifferent to the procurement system being hollowed from within. They are not lying. They are simply operating at one level and refusing to see what supervenes from below.

This is the sophisticated moral evasion: the understanding that high-level facts and low-level facts are autonomous enough that you never have to explain how they fit together. You can celebrate the drones while ignoring the dinner. You can praise institutional capacity while the institutions are being systematically compromised.

Our framework allows for this. It permits explanatory autonomy. But that autonomy also permits complicity.

The bomb fragment lay on the pavement for hours. Nobody thought it remarkable. It was just debris. Just one more fact among millions.

But it was real. And so was the dinner. And so was the billion-dollar purchase. And so was the soldier saying to the foreign correspondent: “I didn’t expect anything like this.”

All true. All autonomous. All impossible to reconcile without asking what we actually believe about what is happening.
