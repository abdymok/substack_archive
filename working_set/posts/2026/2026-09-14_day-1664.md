Every war produces men who explain, with great confidence, why a price went up. Diesel is now such a price, and Ukraine — practiced at being blamed for weather it did not make — has again been given a starring role it does not deserve.

The honest split: Ukrainian strikes on Russian refining and exports account for perhaps five to fifteen percent of the current squeeze. The war in Iran, the Gulf refinery damage, the trouble at Hormuz, explain the rest — eighty-five to ninety-five percent. If one number is required, call it ten to Kyiv, ninety to the Gulf. A judgment, not an official breakdown.

[S&P Global](https://www.spglobal.com/energy/en/news-research/latest-news/crude-oil/071426-global-diesel-market-tightens-as-russian-export-ban-ukraine-strikes-bite) has Russian diesel and gasoil exports falling from about 24 million barrels a month in the spring to 14.8 million in June — a real loss, but not the loss of a refining system. Crude can be rerouted, stocks drawn down, lines restarted. A wound, not an amputation. The [IEA’s September assessment](https://www.iea.org/reports/oil-market-report-september-2026), [relayed by Reuters](https://www.globalbankingandfinance.com/iea-cuts-russian-oil-output-forecasts-ukrainian-attacks/), trims Russia’s 2026 output forecast by 125,000 barrels a day — confirmation of damage, not evidence of a global shortfall.

The pattern repeats in diplomacy, too. The son-in-law said over the weekend that if Ukraine agreed to give up territory, we would already have a deal. According to Jared, a peace agreement is prepared but stalled by Ukraine’s refusal to cede territory along lines demanded by Putin.

And on the anti-corruption front this morning: Ruslan Kravchenko, who has submitted his resignation as Prosecutor General, tells us he has signed a notice of suspicion against National Anti-corruption Bureau of Ukraine (NABU) head Semen Kryvonos in two cases.

Kravchenko suspects Kryvonos of forging official documents to obtain an unlawful benefit on an especially large scale, and of a fictitious adoption aimed at evading liability in court.

He also reports a notice of suspicion against a person close to the Specialized Anti-corruption Prosecutor’s Office (SAPO) head, who had influence over High Anti-Corruption Court judges and offered them unlawful benefits.
