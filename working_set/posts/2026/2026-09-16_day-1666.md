It is a beautiful morning in Kyiv, which is a sentence that means nothing and everything. The important thing is that it is morning, and that Kyiv is still here to have one.

The sky is the color it is supposed to be. The chestnuts have started falling from the trees. A woman in front of the Shevchenko memorial is teaching her Malinois not to bite me.

Not that I’m afraid of them. I grew up with a Dutch Shepherd.

That last part is not a metaphor.

Also, a twenty-two-year-old contestant from a television talent show named Valeriia [sang along with an air raid siren](https://www.instagram.com/valeriya_furman) — matched its pitch, held the note, let her voice braid itself into the sound until it was no longer clear which one was mourning and which one was performing.

[United24 picked it up](https://www.instagram.com/reels/Dc_vfWtuh-k/) and Valeriia has reposted the original on [TikTok](https://www.tiktok.com/@furmanlll/video/7684803251971050760?lang=en). The comments filled with the usual reflex: *this is the Ukrainian soul, they hurt her and she sings.*

She did not make the final twelve of the talent show.

The war, generous as always, gave her a better stage than the one she lost.

The siren itself has more reason to be feared than it used to.

Russia has increased its use of [jet-powered drones roughly sixfold this summer](https://www.reuters.com/business/aerospace-defense/ukraine-races-counter-russias-jet-powered-drone-attacks-2026-09-15/) — machines fast enough that Ukraine’s cheap interceptors, the ones that made the propeller-driven Shaheds of 2023 into something approaching a solved problem, mostly cannot catch them.

A senior commander put it rather more plainly on Telegram: *we completely screwed up the initiative again, that’s a fact.*

This is the honest version of events, which is usually the version delivered by men who do not have to explain themselves to a press office.

The men who do have press offices were busy elsewhere.

Yevhen Magda spent [an hour](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Gxvel3E8YnA) yesterday cataloguing the week’s rot: a fugitive prosecutor general’s disappearing suspicion notices, an anti-corruption chief who will not say whether he recognizes the woman in his own falsified paternity case, a tax-service head fired for loyalty to the wrong patron.

Feudalism, he called it, “on our money and not only ours.”

It is the sort of conversation Kyiv has been having with itself for 35 years now, in different rooms, about different names, arriving at the same diagnosis each time and prescribing nothing.

Meanwhile, the Third Army Corps announced [Operation Vivaldi](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ffadr-u2rbk) — the Lyman salient’s infiltration pockets cleared, seventy-five square kilometers swept, the promise of three more movements to come.

This is the part of the war that still works the way war is supposed to: men doing a hard thing competently, refusing to spoil their own surprise.

Four Seasons, they said.

We are only in the first.

And Z, asked by a Canadian interviewer whether he will be okay, answered with the joke he always makes when he does not want to answer honestly: *I’m okay if Ukraine is okay.*

Then, for one second, he dropped the bit and [admitted he doesn’t know](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HX8R0RhYILA).

Depends on many things.

Strong bodyguards, he said.

We’ll see.

*We’ll see* is also the operative philosophy of the diesel market, which [The Atlantic explains](https://archive.ph/2D32Q) has very little to do with Ukraine and everything to do with a war eight time zones away — a fact that has not stopped the jackass in the White House from blaming Kyiv’s refinery strikes for a six-dollar gallon in San Diego.

Ukraine gets credited with disasters it did not cause and denied credit for wars it is, on any given beautiful morning, still managing to survive.
