There is an old saying in the Kryvyi Rih — though perhaps I invented it just now — that that a man who perfects the art of kneeling will eventually forget how to stand..

Z is a practical man. He understands that dignity, like a good overcoat, must sometimes be pawned when winter is very cold. And the winter in the Dnipropetrovsk region has been very cold indeed, for many years now.

So Z’s people, being resourceful and not without humor, proposed naming a depopulated strip of adjacent coal country — fifty miles long, forty miles wide, populated mainly by soldiers and the shops that sell them flowers for their wives — after the most powerful man in the world. Donnyland, they called it[1](#footnote-1). A place that sounds like happiness, though it is not.

[Image]

One must admire the ingenuity. Trump, after all, has his name on towers, casinos, a university that was not quite a university, and now — if things go well — a small wounded piece of eastern Europe. Z’s people even prepared a flag. Green and gold. And a national anthem, composed with the assistance of a machine that has read everything and understood rather less.

The most poignant detail, to my mind, is this: it is not clear the Americans ever saw the flag.

This is the essence of the relationship. Z gestures grandly across a great distance. Trump looks the other way, occupied with other matters — Iran, perhaps, or a press conference.

Six years ago, a writer noticed this pattern and said: it will not end well[2](#footnote-2).

He was not wrong. He was simply early.
