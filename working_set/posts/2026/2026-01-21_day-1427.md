Running water returned this morning. Heating too. Electricity. I took a long shower—the kind you take when you’re not sure when the next one might be—then made the catastrophic decision to set up a Xiaomi Smart Electric Hot Water Dispenser via Wi-Fi at 5:00 a.m.

The app cheerfully informed me the water was “ready.” What followed was a half cup of triumph, a thoughtful hum, then a dramatic geyser of steam scalding my fingertips. I deleted the app while my hand was still burning.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Standing there, fingertips throbbing, I skimmed four texts on my phone with my other hand. In my headphones, [Stewart Brand told Sean Carroll](https://www.preposterousuniverse.com/podcast/2026/01/19/341-stewart-brand-on-maintenance-as-an-organizing-principle/): “There’s way more ways a thing can go wrong than can go right.” The Xiaomi demonstrated this on my skin.

**[Trump’s press conference](https://rollcall.com/factbase/trump/transcript/donald-trump-press-conference-briefing-room-january-20-2026/)** scrolling past: Nearly two hours of rambling. Mug shots of migrants. Minnesota won three times (lost three times). Greenland. “You’ll find out.” Markets down 800 points. “Markets no longer have a better sense of what’s real and what’s theater.”

Brand: “When the makers of things are paying close attention to the maintainers of those things, all goes well. And if not, not.”

**[Russian strikes on Kyiv](https://understandingwar.org/research/russia-ukraine/russian-offensive-campaign-assessment-january-20-2026/)**: 470 drones, 47 missiles. Minus 18°C. Half the city without power. Chornobyl substations hit—second time in a week. “Minor power outages.” “Zero nuclear threat.” Training missiles with live warheads bolted on. Russia running low on actual stockpiles.

Brand explaining Arab militaries losing wars because they lack NCOs—the sergeants who maintain things, who overlap, who take responsibility. “That is a recipe for losing a war.”

**[Pastukhov’s essay](https://t.me/v_pastukhov/1791)**: The “striking similarity” between MAGA and the Russian World. Trump on Greenland, Putin on Ukraine—identical justifications. Predicate offense. Artificial states. Natural right older than international law. Victim’s stubbornness “provokes” the great power.

Carroll asking about solo sailors. Knox-Johnston improvising repairs. Crowhurst hating maintenance, running out of rum, going mad. “He would reward himself with a drink after completing an unpleasant piece of maintenance. Pretty soon he ran out of rum and wine and then went crazy instead.”

The Xiaomi beeped cheerfully. Still ready. Still confident.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Then **[Lavrov’s press conference](https://mid.ru/en/foreign_policy/news/2073858/)** yesterday in Moscow, where he quoted Marco Rubio quoting *The Godfather*: “Women and children can be careless, but not men.” Nuclear powers must maintain dialogue.

Lavrov said Rubio told him in Riyadh that Trump’s foreign policy is “guided by national interests and common sense,” which “implies recognition of the national interests of the United States’ leading partners.” When interests align, pursue them. When they diverge, “it would be a crime to allow such differences to escalate into confrontation.”

“I responded that I fully share this philosophy and logic,” Lavrov said.

This while Russia strikes Chornobyl. While Trump threatens Greenland with military force. While Pastukhov writes about “the simultaneous dominance, in two former superpowers, of ideologies with such an obviously similar nature.”

Lavrov also noted that Trump, “responding to a related question just a few days ago, stated that he has no interest in international law and that norms of behavior in the international arena are determined by his own moral judgment.”

------------------------------------------------------------------------

5:20 a.m. Fingertips still tender. Four tabs open. Brand still talking about Pete Seeger at 85, watching hippies forget to maintain a Hudson River sloop: “You have to consider that one of the major arcs of civilization is maintenance.”

Systems designed without humility fail in predictable ways. A water dispenser announcing “ready” with aggressive LED confidence while having no idea what it’s doing. A two-hour press conference about Greenland seizures while markets crater. An ideology that treats maintenance—of alliances, of international law, of the distinction between truth and theater—as weakness. As *sailorizing*. Something to get through, reward yourself with a drink after, run out of rum, go crazy.

Brand: “We don’t have a backup. With a global civilization with no backup, does that mean it’s extra fragile or extra robust? I’m pretty sure it’s extra resilient, partly because so many parts are quite different from each other.”

But what if the parts start copying each other’s worst maintenance failures? What if “national interests and common sense” means two nuclear powers simultaneously deciding international law is for women and children?

Pastukhov: “This is too systemic, too deep, to dissolve on its own like a bad midnight dream. Rather, this is precisely the case when the sleep of reason produces monsters.”

The sleep of reason *wearing the mask of reason*. The algorithm. The “philosophy and logic.” The smart device confidently announcing it knows what you need while scalding your hand. Democracy as the dictatorship of one’s clan. Maintenance as something careless people do. Real men just take what they want.

I deleted the app. Turned off the dispenser. Ran my fingers under cold water.

I can’t delete the rest.

But at least the water’s running.

For now.
