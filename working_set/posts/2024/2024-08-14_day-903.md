If you haven’t been paying attention, “correctional facilities” in Russia are large torture chambers, [especially in Kursk region](https://mipl.org.ua/en/crime-scene-kursk-what-happens-to-ukrainians-in-the-most-notorious-russian-pow-facility/).

The little red blobs mark prisons in [Lgov](https://prisonlife.ru/mesta-lishenya-svobodi/322-ispravitelnaya-koloniya-3-g.lgov-kurskaya-oblast.html). A third correctional facility (far right) is located in [Malaya Loknya](https://www.google.com/maps/place/Malaya+Loknya,+Kursk+Oblast,+Russia,+307835/@51.3337055,35.2298679,2752m/data=!3m2!1e3!4b1!4m6!3m5!1s0x41292e9d9c4bf869:0xd9a3b4d467d80a5a!8m2!3d51.3301295!4d35.2234224!16s%2Fg%2F1hhh2yk18?entry=ttu), closer to Sudzha, which Ukrainian forces occupied last week.

Just ask [Oleksiy Anulia](https://texty.org.ua/articles/111246/nine-months-in-russian-captivity-oleksii-anulia-told-how-he-was-starving-eating-worms-and-a-live-mouse/). The plight of dozens of Ukrainian prisoners of war and civilians held in Kursk and other Russian regions has been [extensively documented](https://www.currenttime.tv/a/ukraintsy-v-sizo-rossii/31856366.html). One objective of the incursion is to rescue people like him, I hope.

Ukraine has never been great at OPSEC. Neither has Russia. Soldiers from both countries haven’t been able to resist posting videos and selfies from Russian towns in Kursk region, bragging about killing one another.

A dozen (12) journalists wrote a wordy frontpage article for [The New York Times](http://nyt.com) about the incursion[1](#footnote-1) . But they left out the part about Putin [putting Aleksiy Dyumin in charge](https://www.businessinsider.com/putin-appoints-aleksey-dyumin-bodyguard-russia-defense-kursk-ukraine-2024-8) of managing the counterterrorism op aimed to eject Ukrainian interlopers. That’s a shame, because we are in the eleventh (11) year of the war that Dyumin helped to start.

Dyumin oversaw Putin’s invasions of Crimea and parts of eastern Ukraine in 2014. He, along with Dmytro Utkin, created Wagner Group, a private state-funded military company that Evgeny Prigozhin managed until his plane dropped out of the sky last summer after an unsuccessful mutiny[2](#footnote-2)[3](#footnote-3).

Fun fact of the day: Dyumin was born August 28, 1972, in Kursk.

Team USA spends about $1 trillion a year on intelligence. The Wagner PMC and Dyumin folders are capacious, chock full of at least a decade’s worth of intercepted electronic communications.
