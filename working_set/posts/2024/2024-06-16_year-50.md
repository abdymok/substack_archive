This weekend I went to a barbecue out of town to celebrate the 50th birthday of Oleksiy, who is home again on leave from the front. We all remember his 49th birthday on [Day 472](https://abdymok.substack.com/p/year-49).

What a difference one year makes! Non-stop partying has not kept me from glancing at news reports, which today no longer portray Ukraine as the object of a Russian “special operation” but as a participant in an “ongoing war.”

The finalized version of the [joint communiqué](https://www.reuters.com/world/europe/draft-joint-communique-ukraine-conference-switzerland-2024-06-15/) from the [Ukraine Peace Summit](https://www.google.com/search?q=ukraine+peace+summit&oq=ukraine+peace&aqs=chrome.0.69i59j69i57j0i512l3j69i60l3.2456j0j7&client=ubuntu&sourceid=chrome&ie=UTF-8)  will be the basis for future negotiations with Russia to end the catastrophe, according to Andriy Yermak, head of Ukraine’s President’s Office.

A couple of observations about Putin’s ultimatum:

- Putin's [90-minute rant on Friday](https://edition.cnn.com/2024/06/14/europe/putin-conditions-peace-talks-ukraine-intl/index.html) was the final chord in a drama that has dragged on for almost six months. Russia's talks with Uncle Joe have obviously reached an impasse.
- Ukraine refuses to start negotiations with Putin on the terms of maintaining the status quo along the lines of the existing separation of forces.
- Putin has zero confidence in the current US administration and its ability to get Ukraine to adopt a position that suits both Moscow and Washington
- Moscow has concluded the conflict with the West is existential and cannot, in principle, be resolved peacefully.
- Russian leaders view the West is a colossus on clay feet - poke it hard enough and it will collapse.
- Further events will depend on how Ukraine manages to defend by force areas of Ukraine which Putin claims belong to Russia.
