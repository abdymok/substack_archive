A couple of weeks ago, I happened upon [something](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2023-12-21/companies-boost-ukraine-s-war-economy-with-us-eu-aid-in-doubt), which gave me pause:

> What is moving is the [Ukraine’s] economy[1](#footnote-1). Companies[2](#footnote-2) have pivoted to new markets[3](#footnote-3) and products[4](#footnote-4) — many in the defense industry[5](#footnote-5) — and consumers[6](#footnote-6) are spending money[7](#footnote-7) (while they have it). — ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2023-12-21/companies-boost-ukraine-s-war-economy-with-us-eu-aid-in-doubt))[8](#footnote-8)

[1](#footnote-anchor-1)

It’s large, this word: it holds much; it is flexible. Cash outside banks – held in crypto, glass jars in root cellars or under mattresses – means … more drones. Looking on the positive side, National Bank reserves are solid.

[2](#footnote-anchor-2)

I see this guy named Company in the shower one morning, the lightning bolt of epiphany striking as he rubs the suds from his eyes, *That’s It! I’ll redefine myself – they’ll have to notice me!*

[3](#footnote-anchor-3)

The question of what is useful never quite goes away.

[4](#footnote-anchor-4)

A cart filled with at least one of every single item in the cat food aisle: tinned, bagged, blister-packed, things for kittens, things for old cats; a mountain of the stuff, and nothing else.

[5](#footnote-anchor-5)

A great deal of imagery begins to run through my head of Russian invaders being atomized by FPV drones.

[6](#footnote-anchor-6)

Easy to think of Thorstein Veblen here: the [consumer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consumer) practice of buying and using goods of a higher quality, [price](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Price), or in greater quantity than practical.
