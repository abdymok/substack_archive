Sigh. The Kremlin has said it expects Kushner to be doing the main work on [drafting](https://www.reuters.com/world/kremlin-adviser-says-putin-us-envoy-witkoff-understand-each-other-2025-12-05/) a possible deal. And jackass Donald Jr. is spouting, again.

[Video: https://youtu.be/niarA8cZ7L4]

As for the the [2025 NSS](https://www.whitehouse.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/2025-National-Security-Strategy.pdf), its Europe section opens with concerns about European military spending, then immediately pivots to the real emergency: not enough babies and too many regulations. Europe faces “civilizational erasure” and will be “unrecognizable in 20 years,” the document warns, in prose that reads like a Substack panic attack achieved sentience and infiltrated the State Department.

The contradiction at the document’s core is exquisite. America champions “genuine democracy” while celebrating that “patriotic European parties” are gaining influence—parties that, inconveniently, keep losing elections. European governments “trample on basic principles of democracy to suppress opposition,” we’re told, but the solution is apparently to encourage the opposition doing the suppressing. When your democratic vision requires overriding election results because voters chose wrong, perhaps reconsider the “genuine” part.

The Ukraine bits deserve special recognition for their euphemistic brilliance. We need an “**expeditious cessation of hostilities**” to “**stabilize European economies**” and “**reestablish strategic stability with Russia**.” Translation: Let Putin keep his conquered territory so Germany can buy cheap gas again. The document actually complains that Europe has “unrealistic expectations for the war”—apparently expecting to not reward aggression with territorial concessions is now unrealistic. Bold stance from a strategy document theoretically opposed to “adversarial domination.”

Most remarkable is the fretting about European “external dependencies” while advocating policies that would make Ukraine permanently dependent on Russian imperial benevolence. German chemical companies building plants in China using Russian gas is presented as a crisis, but the proposed solution is... giving Russia what it wants in Ukraine? Twisted.

The “civilizational self-confidence” rhetoric would be touching if it weren’t so transparently about demographics and cultural anxiety dressed up as strategic analysis. Europe needs to “remain European,” whatever that means, while simultaneously opening markets to American goods and doing exactly what Washington demands. Sovereignty for thee, subordination for me.

The document’s vision of “aligned sovereign nations” operating independently but in concert sounds inspiring until you realize it’s describing the exact function of institutions like the EU and NATO—the very “transnational bodies” the text excoriates. The alternative framework appears to be vibes-based coordination where European countries spontaneously agree with American preferences, which is either magical thinking or imperialism with better marketing.

Europe doesn’t need American guidance to rediscover its “former greatness.”

The subtext isn’t even subtle: Europe’s problems aren’t military or economic, they’re existential and civilizational. And America’s role is to save Europe from itself by encouraging the political forces that would dismantle the postwar liberal order.

It’s a strategy built on nostalgia for a past that never existed, contempt for institutions that actually work, and a willingness to sacrifice Ukrainian sovereignty for cheap energy. Call it what it is: managed decline with a civilizational aesthetic.
