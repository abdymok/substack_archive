#### "Stabilizing" - Ukraine's New Word for "Still Fighting for Our Lives"

Our bloody little war is stuck on infinite respawn mode—and everyone's pretending the Putin-Trump beano might change something. It won't, because America's chief negotiator fired everyone who might explain why. So, we're left with making threats while admitting powerlessness, claiming toughness while showing reluctance to actually use available tools.

Donald, who decides foreign policy in "two minutes" based on "instinct," meets Vova, who's ruled Russia for 25 years and is "skilled at catching his interlocutors off guard." Donald's top Russia expert? Real estate developer [Steve Witkoff](https://www.yahoo.com/news/articles/putin-via-trumps-envoy-witkoff-024751411.html). Vova brings a quarter-century of power and unchanged demands for complete Ukrainian capitulation.

Donald now promises "very severe consequences" if Vova isn't "serious about ending the war"—without specifying what those might be. When asked if he could convince Vova to stop targeting civilians, Donald's answer was refreshingly honest: "I guess the answer to that is probably no."

The Financial Times [reveals](https://archive.ph/UnXEv#selection-1889.0-2555.152) stunning institutional vandalism: Donald fired 1,300 State Department officials last month, including Russia analysts. Twenty-five percent of foreign service officers quit since January. The NSC was "drastically pared back." As former Ambassador Eric Rubin notes: "Donald does not have a single policymaking person who knows Russia and Ukraine advising him."

Meanwhile, the actual war grinds on. ISW [reports](https://understandingwar.org/backgrounder/russian-offensive-campaign-assessment-august-13-2025) Russia suffered 60,000 casualties in July alone—Vova's response is moving 27,000 troops to priority sectors and manufacturing 79,000 strike drones this year. Russian forces infiltrate near Pokrovsk while systematically destroying Ukrainian evacuation routes. Ukraine's sole advantage—deep strikes—is precisely what any Donald "air truce" would eliminate.

Tom Cooper's Ukrainian military sources [describe](https://xxtomcooperxx.substack.com/p/Z-shield) systematic military degeneration under Z, with excellent tactical analysis becoming mere "what if" scenarios. Children walk through combat zones to reach evacuation points while 1,150 more need emergency evacuation from Dobropillia.

Here's where farce becomes terrifying: Donald openly plans recreating history's worst diplomatic disasters. Steven Erlanger's analysis [captures](https://www.nytimes.com/2025/08/13/world/europe/yalta-trump-putin-meeting-alaska.html) the horror—this is Munich 1938 meets Yalta 1945. Vova calls Anchorage the "new Yalta," positioning himself as Stalin's heir. Except unlike Stalin, who helped defeat fascism, Vova started this war.

Vova demands territories he's never conquered (like Hitler demanding the Sudetenland), while Donald approaches with less preparation than Chamberlain brought to Munich. European leaders extracted promises Donald won't negotiate without Ukraine present, and Donald claims he'll walk away if Vova isn't acting in "good faith"—but without experts, how will he recognize manipulation? Vova has been preparing for this moment for years; Donald is "lowering expectations."

According to ISW, Vova’s demands remain unchanged since June—four Ukrainian oblasts (including unoccupied areas), regime change in Kyiv, NATO rollback to 1997. These aren't negotiations; they're surrender terms dressed as diplomacy. Donald's "very severe consequences" threats ring particularly hollow given his reluctance to increase economic sanctions despite mounting frustration.

The beano in Alaska will produce either meaningless theater or catastrophically bad agreements, because Donald eliminated anyone who could distinguish between them.

Welcome to Groundhog Day, historical edition—where we repeat the past because we fired everyone who remembered it. Vova already won by getting unprepared, historically ignorant Donald to chat about concessions. The adults left the room, taking 80 years of hard-learned lessons with them.
