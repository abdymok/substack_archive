They meet. They talk. They draft documents. Two documents. Perhaps three. The documents say things. Specific things. Very concrete things, they say. But what things? They cannot say. The things remain unsaid.

Belgium waits. Belgium wants guarantees. A blank check, they say. For forever, they say. The others hesitate. Perhaps they will give the check. Perhaps not. 50:50, says Merz. Perhaps less. They will decide Thursday. Or perhaps not Thursday. Perhaps the weekend.

There are €210 billion. Most in Belgium. Some in France. Smaller amounts elsewhere. The billions wait. Frozen. Immobilized. But not seized. Not yet. Perhaps never. They want to use them. They cannot use them. Russia has filed suit. $230 billion they demand. The circle closes.

Eighteen countries have treaties. Bilateral treaties with Russia. They should rescind them. They do not rescind them. The treaties wait. Russia could use them. Will use them. Through investor-state mechanisms. The words pile up. Nothing happens.

France demands Ukraine buy only European weapons. Not American weapons. European weapons. The others consider this. They draft more documents. Operating documents. Mil-to-mil documents. Eight hours of talks in Berlin. About a dozen countries. Top officials. They sign off, mostly. Progress, they say. Real and concrete progress.

800,000 troops for Ukraine. A peacetime level. Sustained and significant support required. European forces on the ground. Not NATO forces. Just European forces. In western Ukraine. Away from the cease-fire line. Which line? There is no line. There is no cease-fire. Russia is not party to these talks. Russia demands the Donbas. Russia forbids NATO troops. Russia shows no flexibility at all.

Trump says no American troops. Just intelligence. Vast intelligence systems. To monitor. To verify. To detect false flags. The Americans will lead a mechanism. International participation. Early warning. Of what? Of future attacks. Which attacks? After which cease-fire? Which cease-fire exactly?

The Budapest Memorandum failed in 2014. Failed again in 2022. This time will be different, they say. Legally binding, they say. Subject to procedures. Trump will submit to Senate. Perhaps as a treaty. Perhaps not. They do not say.

Z expects finalization. In the coming days. Perhaps this weekend. Then convey to Russia. Then meet with negotiators. Then perhaps. Perhaps then.

The Germans speak. Pistorius says the plan is good in its core. Now we wait for Moscow’s reaction, he says. Laschet says we’ve never been this close to peace. Whether it leads to ceasefire remains open, he says. Depends on Moscow’s reaction, he says. Klingbeil says we shouldn’t take the fifth step before the first. We are already the greatest supporter, he says. We will always meet our responsibility, he says. When the debate really arises, he says.

The Kremlin speaks. No Christmas ceasefire, they say. No details received on guarantees, they say. Putin must say where the journey goes, the Germans say. Then we will see further, they say.

The documents wait. The billions wait. Thursday waits. Russia does not wait. Russia is not waiting. Russia was never waiting.

The siren goes. Again.
