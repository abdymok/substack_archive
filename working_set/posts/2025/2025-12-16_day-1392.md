Nothing is agreed except the pretense that it might be.

The Berlin summit’s [joint statement](https://www.consilium.europa.eu/en/press/press-releases/2025/12/15/leaders-statement-on-ukraine/) reads like a Trump golf resort prospectus—lavish promises of security guarantees, peacetime forces of 800,000, European-led multinational battalions, and ceasefire monitoring mechanisms. It’s all very impressive until you notice nobody’s closed on the land yet.

Everyone’s pounding water in a mortar about NATO while treating territorial concessions as existential symbols rather than negotiable assets.

US officials claim “90% consensus” after eight hours of talks. Western leaders “welcome significant progress” and praise “strong convergence” while carefully noting that “some issues would need to be resolved in the final stages.” German Chancellor Merz calls America’s commitments “truly remarkable.” Translation: we’ve agreed on everything except the one thing that matters. That pesky 10% happens to include whether Ukraine surrenders the the defensive line protecting the country since 2014—a “demilitarized economic zone” where Russia gets sovereignty but pinky-swears not to occupy it with troops.

[ISW](https://understandingwar.org/research/russia-ukraine/russian-offensive-campaign-assessment-december-15-2025/)’s toplines say officials agreed on “NATO-like” security guarantees—but the Kremlin has consistently rejected any deal offering *reliable* Ukrainian security.

Trump’s team promises Article 5-like guarantees, with Senate votes and everything. They’ll help Ukraine maintain 800,000 troops, secure its skies, provide intelligence and logistics. The only catch? No ground troops. And that demilitarized zone business. Oh, and Russia has to agree—which Moscow has already said it won’t, especially to those security guarantees.

The Berlin statement ends with a telling line: “It is now incumbent upon Russia to show willingness to work towards lasting peace.” But Russia’s already shown its willingness—by declining every negotiation, rejecting every framework, and continuing to shell Ukrainian cities.

Nothing is agreed until everything is agreed, the statement reminds us. Quite right. And right now, nothing is agreed except the pretense that it might be.
