Pokrovsk. Bakhmut. Avdiivka. Three years, three cities flattened, fetishized as “strategic milestones.” This is no longer attrition: it is stalemate. We live in a gigantic defense innovation hub. Ukrainian forces rotate units, build command posts for instant evacuation, and destroy buildings not to *win*, but to kill as many Russians as possible. The 400 km² Pokrovsk–Myrnohrad agglomeration has been diced into sectors on Palantir maps while start-ups test new warfighting tech. Meanwhile, Russian forces are massing to consolidate infiltration gains disguising themselves as civilians. Twenty months for thirty miles, hundreds of thousands killed or wounded, cities emptied. Western flaks say the battle for Pokrovsk could be a milestone. Reality calls it more ruin made manifest, a theater of perfidy, maps, drones and rotting corpses. One big fat grey kill zone.
