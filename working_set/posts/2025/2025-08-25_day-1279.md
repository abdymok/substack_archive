Russia’s giveaways:

> "I didn’t say they conceded on everything, but what they have conceded is the recognition that Ukraine will have territorial integrity after the war; they've recognised that they're not going to be able to install a puppet regime in Kyiv – that was of course a major demand at the beginning – and importantly, they've acknowledged that there is going to be some security guarantee." - JD ([Meet the Press](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YRwZE5MJ5co), August 24, 2025)

[Image]

Sigh.

*The Occidental[1](#footnote-1)* (*Мирні переговори*), formerly called *Переможемо разом* (*We Will Win Together*) and *Кримська платформа* (*Crimea Platform*), is the newly reopened diplomatic restaurant at the Willard Hotel on Pennsylvania Avenue. On Thursday nights before the midterm elections, they used to have Ukrainian folk music and Z would show up, fresh from awarding "National Legends" to opera singers and high-jumpers while his missiles sat unused in Pentagon-approved storage. It was a pretty cramped place, and it began to occur to defense officials, "Should we approve ATACMS strikes this Thursday or not?" They realized that regulars—mostly corrupt arms dealers, think tank fellows and money managers—would be asking themselves the same question.

The problem is not new. In physics, there's the spherical cow assumption: "Consider a spherical cow of uniform density." It's a joke about how theorists oversimplify complex problems to make them mathematically tractable. Political upheaval—like a war—just adds another layer of comedy.

The more things change, they stay the same. Today JD Vance meets you at the entrance to the negotiations, where little ATACMS missiles hang down from the ceiling, blocked by Pentagon approval mechanisms. This is where Belarusian bitumen wholesalers rub shoulders with members of Ukraine's defense ministry, investment bankers and peace process architects from Washington's impoverished think tanks. Photographs of magical territorial concessions are posted daily by analysts on Twitter. If you're lucky, Mykola the Pork Chop might be there, along with Elbridge Colby practicing his color-coded weapons taxonomy, while Z livestreams from Kyiv about "Ukrainian legends" and how "everything will be alright" and "we will embrace each other, so gently."

The spherical cow approach to ending-the-war negotiations assumes perfect information, rational actors, and frictionless diplomatic surfaces. In reality, Z can't fire his missiles without Pentagon approval while Trump tweets about the impossibility of winning without offense. Putin processes his abandonment issues in an empty chair that may or may not represent genuine territorial integrity commitments. The Donbas sits at the center like Schrödinger's province—simultaneously Ukrainian and Russian until someone observes the final settlement.

Vance's concessions revelation is classic spherical cow thinking: assume Russia recognizes Ukrainian territorial integrity, assume they've abandoned regime change, assume meaningful security guarantees. But spherical cows don't lactate, and spherical peace processes don't end wars. The real negotiations happen between Thursday night regulars who know that if everyone uses the same pure strategy—territorial compromise, security guarantees, frozen conflicts—it's guaranteed to fail, especially when one side is awarding medals to opera singers while the other side systematically destroys power grids.

Most predictable outcome? More theoretical frameworks, more spherical cow assumptions about rational actors and clean territorial divisions. The restaurant stays open, the geishas keep greeting diplomats at the door, and somewhere in the back room, people who actually understand the physics of war continue to block the weapons that might make the mathematics work.
