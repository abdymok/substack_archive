[The Treaty of Brest-Litovsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Brest-Litovsk) delivered Ukraine its first taste of 20th century great power "independence": Lenin agreed to stop fighting, the Germans moved in as liberators, then came rapid collapse and civil war chaos. Now we're watching the sequel, with Ukraine once more caught between great powers deciding its fate without meaningful Ukrainian input.

Donald's recent press conference announcing his Anchorage summit was a masterclass in amplifying Russian propaganda—all while insisting he's the toughest negotiator Vova has ever faced. Meanwhile, Z's latest pep talks sound like whistling past the graveyard: "No decisions will be made on Ukraine without Ukraine." The real question is whether Ukraine gets a voice in the negotiations or just gets to rubber-stamp whatever these two hash out in Anchorage.

**Team USA's Warm-Up Act**

The most revealing moment came when Donald channeled Viktor Orbán to explain Russian military doctrine. According to his Hungarian whisperer, Russia always wins because "they fight wars. That's what they do." This historical determinism conveniently ignores Russia's spectacular losses in 1905, 1917, 1979, and 2022. But why let facts interfere with a good invincivity narrative?

Donald's role as Vova's real estate agent was equally striking. He gushed over Russia's territorial acquisitions, describing occupied Ukrainian lands as "very prime territory" and coveted "oceanfront property." This wasn't analysis—it was a sales pitch reframing war crimes as savvy property investment.

The blame-shifting was pure Kremlin gold. Vova bears no responsibility for Europe's largest war since 1945; it's "Joe Biden's war." Vova was just an innocent bystander swept along by American incompetence—a narrative that strips him of agency while absolving moral responsibility.

Donald's advance team has already warmed up Vova's negotiating position. As JD declared on Fox News: "The President and I certainly think that America is done with the funding of the Ukraine war business." Nothing says tough negotiation like announcing your red lines before you even sit down.

**The Ukrainian Equation**

Whatever Donald and Vova decide, Ukraine will swallow. The only variable is how much Z will have to smile while doing it. He might manage to stomach dividing Ukraine along current battle lines, though explaining that to Ukrainians won't exactly be a career highlight. What he absolutely cannot do is voluntarily hand over territories Russia hasn't yet conquered.

Not yet, anyway.

Ukraine's surrender formula simply hasn't ripened. Three possible endings await: Z could solve his mobilization crisis and deliver a knockout punch, inspiring Europe to double down on a post-American war effort. Or the counterattack could fail spectacularly, forcing Z into his own Brest-Litovsk moment—signing away everything he'd previously died defending. Or Ukraine could collapse so completely that Vova's appetite grows beyond current territories.

The catch? To survive such capitulation, you need Lenin's revolutionary ruthlessness—the ability to sacrifice territory for survival, then spend decades plotting revenge. Z lacks this cold calculation. He appears too invested in self-aggrandizement to make the brutal pivot from hero to realist.

**The Anchorage Sideshow**

So Donald and Vova head to their Alaskan "showdown" not to settle anything, but to size each other up and sketch the rough outlines of Ukraine's eventual partition. This isn't diplomacy; it's reconnaissance with vodka.

Vova doesn't need Donald to be a conscious Russian agent—just a useful one. Donald's attraction to strongmen, transactional view of alliances,and desperate need for diplomatic "wins" make him the perfect mark. The Russian president gets American legitimization of his conquests wrapped in dealmaking language.

They won't reach any deals now (unless Russia's military suddenly discovers competence in the next 48 hours). But they'll absolutely draw dotted lines on all their Molotov-Ribbentrop fantasy maps, saving precious time when circumstances finally force Ukraine's hand.

The tragedy isn't that Donald might strike a bad deal—it's that he's already advertising Ukrainian surrender terms while calling it tough negotiation. It's geopolitical meal prep: chopping all the vegetables now so you can cook quickly when the moment's right.

History doesn't repeat, but it sure does rhyme. And right now, it's rhyming in Russian.
