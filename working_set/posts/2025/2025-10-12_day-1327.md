This residential building in Kyiv’s Pechersk District was hit on its eastern facade - the side where residents watch the sunrise each morning. Directly across the street is a cafe that was once called Trump Cafe, but the owners renamed it Nolan because they felt Donald was wasting Ukraine’s time. And he is.

Thomas Peter took a nice photo from inside after the attack, looking down the hill at the Dnipro River. You can make out Mat’ Rodina in the distance. She is looking in the direction of Moscow. She is made of titanium and it would be a lot of work, maybe impossible, to reposition the head. They fixed her shield a couple of years ago, but it’s already rusting.

If this map is correct, Kyiv got hit on October 10 by Shaheds coming from both sides of the Dnipro—because why attack from one direction when you can be annoying from two? The drones do their signature move: loitering in lazy circles before committing to the nosedive, which makes pinpointing their launch sites a fun guessing game. The little blue lines streaking down from the north? Ballistic missiles, probably the North Korean party favors Russia’s been showing off lately.

Here’s Z mastering the art of complaining while insisting he’s *totally not complaining* about Kyiv’s spectacular failure to protect its central heating plants from getting wrecked, which plunged the city into hours-long blackouts.

“Take CHP-5, CHP-6. This isn’t just about air defense—we can’t waste Patriots on drones. What questions should we ask the mayor? Kyiv’s municipal enterprises? I could tell you what I think about all this. But I won’t, because we all have one war,” Z said Friday, radiating restrained exasperation.

He stressed that priority one is standing firm and defeating the enemy.

“We’ll do everything. Where local authorities can’t handle it, where the private sector has relevant stations—we still have to protect it all. One country, one people, and nobody cares whose station or whose property it is when the lights go out,” he added. *— Interfax-Ukraine, October 10, 2025*

Sigh. Same guy who spent six months in 2021 getting told—repeatedly, insistently, with charts and maps—that Russia would definitely invade, and kept telling us that there was nothing to worry about.
