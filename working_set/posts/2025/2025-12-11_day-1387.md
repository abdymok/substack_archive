If anyone’s been wondering why Z appears to be bouncing around European capitals like a man possessed, here’s the situation: He’s not stuck between a rock and a hard place. He’s stuck between two rocks and a vacuum.

**Rock One: Russia.** Moscow systematically rejects every proposal—including its own previous demands. Elections? Russia demanded them for years. Z agrees to hold them in 60 days. Russia immediately rejects them and preemptively delegitimizes any Ukrainian government it doesn’t directly control. Energy ceasefire? Rejected. Russia’s too busy destroying the power grid every seven to ten days. Security guarantees? Threats against Europe for even discussing them. The Kremlin’s position is clear: surrender or die slowly.

**Rock Two: America.** For the first time since 1945, the United States has sided with the aggressor in a European war. Trump’s goal isn’t peace—it’s rapprochement with Putin. His officials encouraged Belgium to block access to €210 billion in frozen Russian assets because Washington wants to return them to Russia as part of a settlement. America is actively preventing Europe from funding Ukraine’s defense using Russian money.

**The Vacuum: Europe.** Europe has €245 billion in frozen Russian assets sitting in Belgium. Ukraine needs $200 billion for the next two years. The math is simple. The politics are impossible. Finance ministers worry about the euro’s credibility. Belgium fears Russian retaliation. NATO commanders say 2029 is the deadline for credible deterrence, but Europe can’t replace American capabilities and won’t access the money sitting right there.

So when critics call Z stubborn, what they’re observing is a man trying to play chess while his opponents play calvinball and his supposed allies actively help the other side. His “superhuman stubbornness” isn’t tactical incompetence—it’s the only rational response when every door is locked, every window barred, and the house is on fire.

The parallel universe isn’t Z’s diplomacy. The parallel universe is thinking this was ever going to end in negotiated settlement when one side demands surrender, another wants rapprochement with the aggressor, and the third has the money but won’t spend it.

And yet—somehow—Ukraine hasn’t collapsed. Which tells you everything about Ukrainian resolve and absolutely nothing about Western strategy, because there isn’t one.
