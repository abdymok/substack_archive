Our thoughts this morning were with Trey, standing bug-eyed on the balcony of the Intercontinental Hotel in Kyiv, blue helmet in hand, waiting…

*[@TreyYingst 6h: Air defense active on the outskirts of Kyiv.](https://x.com/TreyYingst/status/1965529767035457762)*  
*[@TreyYingst 7h: Air raids sirens sounding across Kyiv.](https://x.com/TreyYingst/status/1965512799209357535)*

Putin has put Trump in a very difficult position. All those drones — some launched from Belarus — flying into Poland. If this is not a deliberate provocation by Putin aimed at humiliating NATO, then it is a spectacular fiasco engineered by the recently decorated Chief of Russia's General Staff Valery Gerasimov. You decide which is more annoying.

Occam's Razor suggests the simpler explanation: this wasn't grand strategy but the predictable consequence of launching hundreds of missiles and drones in the general direction of targets near NATO's border. When you fire that much ordnance with Soviet-era accuracy systems, some of it will inevitably go astray. The question isn't whether Putin intended this specific outcome, but whether he cared enough about the consequences to prevent it.

When you systematically dismantle the rules that constrain behavior, you shouldn't be surprised when the chaos spreads in directions you didn't anticipate.

The latest air attack comes just hours after rioters burned down the parliament building in Kathmandu, Israel bombed Doha, and glide bombs wiped out 24 residents of Yarova collecting their pensions. Coincidence? Perhaps. Or perhaps we're witnessing what happens when the post-war international order — those boring, liberal constraints on state behavior — continues to crumble.

As Nikolayevsky Vanek [noted](https://t.me/vanek_nikolaev/35811) on Telegram this morning:

> Dear whoever just woke up,  
> You just slept through another combined attack. The main strike was on Zhytomyr, but they also hit Vinnytsia and Lviv. The attack, like the previous one, was very vague. A peculiarity: more than 10 Shahed UAVs left Ukraine's air space and flew into Poland.  
> **This has never happened before.**  
> I am sure that the Poles will record fewer. I would not expect any particular reaction.

But the Poles are now very much part of our House Dance. Defense Minister Władysław Kosiniak-Kamysz is cutting short his London meetings to rush home. President Karol Nawrocki is convening emergency security sessions. They've shortened military reporting times to six hours in the eastern provinces — those red zones [on the map](https://x.com/terytorialsi/status/1965616716177641629) where Russian debris now litters Polish soil.

*ALERT GOTOWOŚCI WOT W związku z naruszeniem polskiej przestrzeni powietrznej...*

[Image]

The Polish alert reads like a bureaucrat's fever dream: "Please remain calm while we figure out whether these are Russian missile fragments or just really aggressive litter."

What's darkly comic is how Putin's "just do things" philosophy creates problems he never intended. Those drones over Poland weren't testing NATO's resolve — they were simply the price of doing missile business without quality control. When you abandon all constraints, your chaos becomes everyone else's emergency meeting.

The Poles are now learning what Ukrainians have known for years: sovereignty is a subscription service, and Putin just canceled everyone's plan.

It's all anti-climactic, until one explodes in your living room. Or your neighbor's.
