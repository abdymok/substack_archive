My aging, perforated brain this weekend re-wrapped itself around (came to accept, perhaps) the fairly rudimentary and time-proven concept that *a variable created inside a function won’t necessarily be available the next time the function is called*.

[Image]

About two-thirds of the way through [this sanctimonious drivel](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2025/nov/16/Z-pledges-to-clean-up-ukraine-energy-sector-amid-corruption-scandal)—in which Harding and Rankin solemnly intone that Z is “under pressure to prove to European allies that he is serious about tackling corruption”—I recalled Harding’s [slavish interview](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZyHvrjHnVIg) with Z mere days before this $100m scandal exploded. That puff piece contained precisely zero questions about the industrial-scale graft that was apparently an open secret to everyone except Luke, intrepid Guardian correspondent[1](#footnote-1).

The whiplash is almost comical. Last week: heroic wartime leader, defender of democracy, no follow-ups necessary. This week: “worst scandal of his presidency,” though do note the impressive verbal gymnastics required to avoid saying anything that might puncture the narrative. Even now, even here, we get the usual therapeutic framing—it’s not about corruption, you see, it’s about Ukraine’s “struggle and fight against corruption.”

How inspiring. How completely useless.

One has to admire the sheer nerve required to practice journalism this way—to conduct [a major interview](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZyHvrjHnVIg) while studiously averting one’s eyes from the story, then to cover that same story later while exhibiting zero self-awareness about one’s own credulity. It’s performance art, really. Bad performance art, but still.

And speaking of performance art: a day later this [masterpiece of evasion](https://abcnews.go.com/Business/wireStory/mike-pompeo-adviser-scrutinized-ukraine-defense-company-boost-127579709) appeared in [The Guardian](http://theguardian.co.uk), the [AP](http://ap.com) reported that Mike Pompeo has joined the advisory board of [Fire Point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fire_Point_(Ukrainian_firm)), Ukraine’s premier defense contractor—currently under investigation for corruption and facing uncomfortable questions about its ties to Tymur Mindich, who happens to be Z’s old business partner and the star of this particular scandal. You’d think the spectacle of a former U.S. Secretary of State joining the advisory board of a company entangled in the very corruption investigation our Guardian heroes are so delicately chronicling might merit a mention, or at least a moment’s pause. But why let an inconvenient detail like that interrupt the elegantly hedged narrative?

There’s a war on, after all.

Which brings us to the silver lining in all this: if you’re an actual journalist in Ukraine—the kind who asks difficult questions—you don’t need to worry about having an Achilles’ heel. You’re not some tragic figure with a fatal flaw. You’re just an obstacle on the tracks, and eventually you’re going to get flattened. The only real choice is whether you try to derail the oncoming train or step aside and let the Lukes of the world tell everyone how smoothly the trains are running.
