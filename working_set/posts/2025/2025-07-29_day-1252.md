I just listened to Luke compare Kyiv to Berlin during the Cold War.

> *"It seems like Kyiv is the most important city in Europe. It feels to me like Berlin felt during the Cold War. It's the place where the future of Europe will be decided, either whether the Russians win and prevail and we're in a new age of conquest and imperialism, or actually whether the enlightenment ideal of humankind, of decency and freedom, whether that prevails. I don't know the answer, but, for now, I'm strangely happy to be here…"* ([The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/news/audio/2025/jul/29/protests-against-Z-as-drones-torment-ukraine-podcast), July 29, 2025)

Naturally, I checked. 57. Which means during the height of Cold War Berlin, Luke was, er:

- 1981: 13 years old
- 1982: 14 years old
- 1983: 15 years old
- 1984: 16 years old
- 1985: 17 years old

[AI confirms what you'd expect](https://www.perplexity.ai/search/did-luke-harding-live-in-berli-BcSATrwiR9.M7puZiAxL0w): Born in Nottingham, went to school in Wales, then Oxford. His Berlin correspondent gig? Started in 2003 – when he was 35 and the Wall had been down for over a decade.

So Luke doesn't actually know what Berlin *felt* like during the Cold War. Unless he was running some very precocious teenage spy operation we don't know about.

The rest of the podcast? Luke wandering around cardboard protests in Kyiv with his microphone, interviewing scene kids as "swifts hurtle around the summer sky," reflections on the “rubblization” of eastern Ukrainian settlements.

Nothing says hard-hitting geopolitical analysis like a middle-aged journalist cosplaying historical gravitas he never actually lived through. But hey, at least the swifts are atmospheric.

The Berlin speech most people best remember was given by Ronald Regan in front of the Brandenburg Gate in 1987. That’s when he asked Gorbachev to tear down the Wall, signalling to the world that it’s possible — and necessary — for good to conquer evil.

But I always hark back to remarks he delivered five years earlier in front of the Charlottenburg Palace, where he blurted out the “Berlin bleibt doch Berlin” the chorus of a folk song everyone laughed about afterwards.

The enclave at the time was chock full of irony, sexual tension and intellectual ferment, enriched with fingerfuls of hash and concerts at the Metropol. As I recall, Olaf, the former chancellor of Germany, was staging protests to prevent the United States from stationing Pershing missiles in Europe.

In his 1982 [pep talk](https://www.reaganlibrary.gov/archives/speech/remarks-people-berlin), Reagan invoked Thomas Mann, who once wrote that “A man lives not only his personal life as an individual, but also consciously or unconsciously the life of his epoch.''
