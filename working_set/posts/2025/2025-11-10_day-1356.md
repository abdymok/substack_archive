[Lights flicker in Kyiv.](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2025/nov/09/volodymyr-Z-why-should-i-be-afraid-donald-trump) Z grins. “It’s normal,” he says, framing blackouts as quaint quirks. Every word polished, every gesture rehearsed. He denies tension with Trump, praises King Charles, radiates calm moral authority. *“So why should we be afraid?”*

[Image]

It’s “normal” because the president and his top managers failed miserably to prepare. Same with fall-back lines of defense, fortifications, mandatory military conscription, selective justice (persecution of political rivals), etc., ad nauseum. This might explain why anti-corruption agencies this morning [are searching](https://nv.ua/ukr/ukraine/politics/nabu-priyshlo-z-obshukami-do-germana-galushchenka-ta-v-energoatom-novini-ukrajini-50559334.html) the residence of Ukraine’s Justice Minister Herman Halushchenko.

[The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/world/ukraine) isn’t writing about that. It’s all hush hush.

Meanwhile, pockets collapse in the Pokrovsk-Myrnohrad agglomeration. Russian and Ukrainian forces grind through access roads, probing and interdicting each other’s GLOCs. Drone swarms hum, mortars boom, positions shift by meters, weather frustrates operations. Russian advances have slowed; reinforcements trickle in. Uncertainty dominates, indifferent to heroics or cameras.

[Image]

Z’s show‑business polish conceals logistics, attrition, and the slow grind of reality. He has finally recognized this is a war of stalemate, not attrition, something we pointed out laboriously on Day 1323[1](#footnote-1).

The president is a comedian of slapstick idiocy: timing, gestures, moral punchlines, all carefully rehearsed. Standup itself, much like his set, pleads: *please like me, please like me.* Polished, heroic, consumable.

Maybe replace him with Eddie Izzard, who didn’t mask absurdity but exposed it. We don’t want to conceal the grind. It’s better to illuminate it without the theatrics.

[Image]

I direct your attention to the pledge page at **ukraine@war**. The backoffice is now run by someone representing herself as the the terribly sane Japanese organizer Marie Kondo, aka [KonMarie](https://konmari.com/?srsltid=AfmBOopueSUIqjASRZS3HDYhEaJc_znxxu8OeBXT4kMpCr9Dz2QzyUSB), who has hooked it up to [Stripe](http://stripe.com) in order to pay for portable charging devices like the one on the table below[2](#footnote-2). She will also use pledges to pay off debts from her MBA vacation in the United States.

[Image]

I have great hopes for the site becoming a reliable source of comment and information freed of the sentimental histrionics and pre‑emptive ridicule of any opposing view germane to warblogs, most of which have gone from licking each other’s wounds following the start of hostilities, to taking grim pleasure cataloguing every atrocity carried out in the name of preserving empire, to forming—with a sort of shuddering inevitability—into a lockstep pro Z mosh pit in which the most cynical predictions of last year—about those who would dare to advance a political agenda on the back of inconceivable human tragedy—have become not only true, but somehow above criticism.

Conversely, I expect [ukraine@war](https://abdymok.substack.com/) to be free of the handwringing that characterizes some Substack authors, among whom, now and again, a flurry of concern arises over What Must Be Done about whatever bile currently poisons the postings and their comment sections. It shouldn’t be in the least surprising that idiots driven by bloodlust and malformed patriotism are sharing their opinions on X every chance they get. Yes, there is unchecked bootlicking, great crimson swathes of it. Same as it ever was. Let them rant and screed and ‘fisk’ and bleat. As with any echo chamber—[Iuliia Mendel’s Weekly Updates](https://substack.com/@iuliiamendel?utm_source=global-search) included—no one outside it is listening.

What will be made evident, I hope, is that criticism of Ukraine’s leadership failures is not a reflexive ideological matter, born out of political allegiances, pro-Russian sympathies, or what colour underwear one prefers. It is rather awareness that we can condemn both: Putin’s criminal invasion—a genocidal war of aggression driven by his maniacal need to control resources and power, a bloodthirsty calculus in which Ukrainian lives are merely variables in his delusions of empire—and simultaneously Z’s catastrophic failure to prepare adequate defenses, protect critical infrastructure, or manage this war with anything approaching competence.

As opposed, let’s say, to the prevailing narrative that demands we choose between uncritical worship of Z’s wartime leadership or tacit support for Russian aggression. Both can be true. Both must be said. One does not cancel the other, no matter how much the cheerleaders and the defeatists wish it did.
