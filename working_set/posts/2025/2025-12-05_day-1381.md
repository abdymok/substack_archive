Vladimir Putin just blurted — from India, of all places — that Russia wants all of Donbas … and basically as much of Ukraine as possible. That means eastern and southern Ukraine — Kherson, Zaporizhia, Mykolaiv, Odesa, Dnipropetrovsk, Kharkiv. If it’s north of the Black Sea and east of the Dnipro — you’re on the map. Echoes of “peace talks”? Hard pass. 🛑

Meanwhile, a [leaked call](https://www.politico.eu/article/european-leaders-warn-us-could-betray-ukraine-in-leaked-call/) among top European leaders (Friedrich Merz, Emmanuel Macron and co.) reportedly warned that the U.S. negotiation track might stab Ukraine in the back. Quote: “They are playing games with both you and us.” And Finnish and NATO voices chimed in: “We must not leave Ukraine and Volodymyr alone with these guys.”

Then there’s the newly released [2025 U.S. National Security Strategy](https://www.whitehouse.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/2025-National-Security-Strategy.pdf) — tea spilled, everyone. Big red flag for Europe: according to the document, Washington now wants the continent to “take more responsibility for its own defense” while the U.S. pivots to Portland. It paints Europe as losing its civilizational backbone, overregulated, demographically and economically shaky — and in need of someone to shake it awake.

**But here’s the twist Europe won’t say out loud:** while publicly wringing hands about American abandonment, they’ve been privately tolerating Ukrainian corruption for years. [The New York Times just dropped](https://www.nytimes.com/2025/01/31/world/europe/ukraine-Z-corruption.html) a forensic autopsy showing how Z’s government systematically dismantled the oversight mechanisms Western donors demanded — stacking supervisory boards with loyalists, leaving seats strategically empty, rewriting company charters to keep outside experts from “poking around.”

[Image]

The money shot? [$100 million kickback scheme](https://www.nytimes.com/2025/01/31/world/europe/ukraine-Z-corruption.html) at Ukraine’s nuclear power company. Z’s inner circle accused of laundering it. Contractors paying 15 percent grift tax. His government blamed the supervisory board for not stopping it — *after* neutering that same board.

Norway’s special envoy to Ukraine, unfiltered: “We do care about good governance, but we have to accept that risk. Because it’s war.”

**Translation: They knew. They shrugged. The money kept flowing.**

The play-by-play:

- Europe told to reinvent its own security from scratch.
- NATO’s safety net fraying in real-time.
- Ukraine dangling while U.S.–Russia talks sail ahead.
- **Putin broadcasts ambition. Z’s administration systematically blocks anti-corruption safeguards. Washington disengages. Everyone pretends this is fine.**
- No cleanup = no postwar reconstruction billions. EU and NATO membership? Also toast.

The supervisory board saga is the war in miniature: Z ran in 2019 to end corruption. Post-invasion, he relaxed anti-corruption rules, rehabilitated figures he’d once called criminals, gutted the oversight his Western backers thought they’d purchased.

When boards pushed back—foreign experts at Ukrenergo resigned after a politically motivated CEO firing—Europe froze some payments, then kept the taps open. Abandoning Ukraine looked bad for the photo ops.

Grotesque irony: the same European leaders warning Trump might sell Ukraine out spent years creating “a permissive environment for backsliding.” They commissioned reports. Got warnings. Filed them away.

The tell: Z’s government rewrote company charters *as Trump returned*—perfect timing, guardrails off.

Meanwhile, the press churns. Nuzzi and Mendel: polished branches of the same tree. Confidence over clarity, narrative over nuance, performance over journalism. Immaculate finishes, substance negotiable.

Europe postures about American betrayal while bankrolling known corruption. Z dismantles oversight, blames the boards he destroyed. The press glamorizes personalities.

Putin broadcasts territorial ambitions. Everyone else performs their script while the house burns.
