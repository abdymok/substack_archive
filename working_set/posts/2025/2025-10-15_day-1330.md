Historically, the U.S. had **small europium stocks** — for example, from the Mountain Pass operation before the 1990s, when it was still under [Molycorp](https://www.molycorp.com/) and produced some europium oxide for red phosphors in color TVs. But that production ended decades ago.

[Traxys](https://www.traxys.com/) was a financial/inventory partner and early investor in the reboot of [Molycorp](https://www.molycorp.com/) (Mountain Pass). They helped buy the Mountain Pass assets in 2008, provided inventory-financing and offtake arrangements, took an equity stake, helped sponsor the 2010 IPO — and then largely exited/sold their interests by about 2013.

Considar Inc. was one of the legacy firms that merged into and formed the core of the Traxys trading platform in 2003. Joe (Joseph) Filner founded and operated Considar — it was one of several metals-trading companies he ran (alongside Newmet Corp. / Noblemet and Project Development Inc.). He retired in the 1990s and died in 2000. We all remember his son [Bob](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_Filner), who in 2013 *self-destructed* as a misogynist US Congressman from San Diego. Practicing philosopher Volodymyr Pastukhov now has an English-language [Telegram account](https://t.me/pastukhov_eng), although he hasn’t posted this, yet:

> The era of the “Holy Alliance,” when Russia was, as it is commonly written, “Europe’s gendarme” (though I prefer the term “overseer of Europe” in the name and at the behest of pan-European reaction), is undoubtedly the “gold standard” of relations between Russia and Europe for Putin and his clan. In short, there was a time when it wasn’t Europe that “bent” Russia to suit itself, but Russia that “bent” Europe to suit its own vision of “political happiness,” and many would like to see that happen again.
>
> However, in legal theory, there is a term: “attempt with unsuitable means.” It is used when the criminal’s chosen method and weapon are clearly insufficient to carry out their criminal intent. For example, an attack on a bank with a cardboard pistol would clearly be classified as such a situation. Similarly, if Putin really wanted to become the new “Europe’s gendarme” (there’s no harm in wanting), he’s clearly chosen the wrong way.
>
> I’ll leave aside the moral and legal aspects of the matter – after all, we live, if not in an age of the “end of history,” then certainly in an age of the “end of morality” – but from a purely practical standpoint, the war with Ukraine, in Putin’s own words, has become Russia’s second-largest geopolitical catastrophe in the last half-century since the collapse of the USSR. And no matter how many stacked the deck in the Kremlin, this verdict of history will remain. Almost everyone in Putin’s elite understands this. They can’t do anything about it yet, but everyone understands.
>
> Wars are started after at least more or less calculating the immediate consequences. Russia has repeated in Ukraine the same mistake that Germany made in Russia (then the USSR) last century: counting on a brilliant blitzkrieg, it plunged into a resource war with an enemy far superior in resources.
>
> Let’s recall Ribbentrop’s testimony at the Nuremberg Trials about the reasons for Germany’s defeat: they underestimated the ferocity of the Red Army’s resistance, the extent of American and British aid, and the effectiveness of Allied air power. Couldn’t all this, adjusted for the time, be applied to the Russian invasion of Ukraine?
>
> Putin’s ammunition clearly doesn’t match his ambitions. A man who has been unable to conquer two regions of Ukraine for three years can hardly hope to conquer all of Europe. What is he counting on now? How comically and tragically history repeats itself: on miracle weapons and on Roosevelt (Trump) falling out with Stalin (Z), and the coalition disintegrating. It makes me want to rewatch “Seventeen Moments of Spring,” but from a different perspective.
>
> Yes, with China’s help, he can hold on to this war for a long time, but even with China’s help, he can’t win it. Which means time is against him. It’s no surprise that he opened another front and declared war on time itself, intending to live forever.
>
> For What? – Wrong question. The right question is – Why? Because he knows that with his departure, ANY successor will wind down this war. Which means he has no successors and cannot have any. This is a terrible impasse for the country, for the state, and for Putin personally.
