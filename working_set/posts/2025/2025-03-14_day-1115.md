In other news, practicing philosopher Volodymyr Pastukhov [writes](https://t.me/v_pastukhov/1433):

> The Ukrainian revolution, which took place in two stages (in 2004-2005 and in 2013-2014), had three sources (three components, as theorists of revolutions of the last century would say). These sources were the general democratic movement (also anti-corruption), which gave the revolution its face and name (Revolution of Dignity), the national liberation movement (which Moscow stubbornly did not want to see, but which Washington saw well) and **the consumerist movement**, fueled by those who do not care what empire to live under, as long as they live well (which Moscow understood perfectly well, but Washington did not understand well). The third component, although the most massive, was also the most passive, but between the general democratic and national liberation components there was a continuous struggle for dominance from the very beginning. The success of the revolution was largely due to the fact that in 2013-2014 the vectors of these driving forces coincided.
>
> At the first stage of the war, this unity of goals and objectives of the general democratic and national movements was once again confirmed, which is what ensured a high level of resistance to aggression. A promising trend emerged for the accelerated formation of a Ukrainian nation in wartime conditions (that is, a community based on values, and not on language, blood and religion). But the most important thing was that this combination of general democratic and national liberation goals ensured the international support for the Ukrainian revolution, without which the miracle of 2022 could never have happened.
>
> It was the prevalence of general democratic (as Gorbachev would say - universal) values ​​at the first stage of the war that allowed Western public opinion to assess this war as “its own,” as a war for the principles and ideals of the free world. And only these public sentiments pushed the Western elites (always skeptical) to take concrete actions.
>
> Unfortunately, the turn in public sentiment in the West that we are witnessing and of which Trump is the spokesman, did not happen without reason. Ultimately, it was a reaction to the change in the vector of the Ukrainian revolution itself. **These changes became noticeable by the fall of 2022 and obvious by the summer of 2023. The essence of these changes was that the national liberation component completely suppressed and dissolved the general democratic component.**
>
> This had far-reaching consequences, and to a greater extent outside Ukraine than in Ukraine itself. As general democratic values ​​faded into the background (or rather, remained in the foreground exclusively in the form of slogans and decorations), and national goals and objectives came to the fore, the attitude towards the war in Ukraine began to change. It's not that Europeans and Americans have become sympathetic to Putin and Russia, but they have stopped perceiving this war as "theirs" and have started treating it as "Ukrainians' war." There is, after all, a stylistic difference in what is written on the banners of the revolutionary army: "for our and your freedom" or "may you all die." Ultimately, the shift in perception has become so noticeable that it has allowed the so-called "Trumps" and "Vances" to reformat policy towards Ukraine. **From today's perspective, the fact that Z, in conditions of the most brutal aggression, took the line of least resistance, that is, betting on national sentiment to the detriment of the original general democratic goals and objectives of the Ukrainian revolution, seems like a mistake that has had grave consequences.** And now it's too late to change anything - he is trapped in history...

The parts in bold are worth thinking about, but today is Friday and I haven’t had breakfast yet.
