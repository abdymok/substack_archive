### Peace talks

Here, finally, is the framework of the peace plan, as per Z:

> **Draft Peace Plan: 20-Point Overview**
>
> **Sovereignty & Security (Points 1-6)**
>
> - Reaffirms Ukraine’s sovereignty
> - Full non-aggression agreement with Russia, monitored via satellite surveillance
> - Strong security guarantees for Ukraine
> - Ukrainian Armed Forces maintained at 800,000 troops in peacetime
> - U.S., NATO, and EU provide Article 5-style security guarantees
> - Russia must enshrine non-aggression policy toward Europe and Ukraine in law
>
> **Economic & European Integration (Points 7-10)**
>
> - Ukraine joins EU on specific timeline with short-term privileged market access
> - Major development package covering tech, AI, data centers, and gas infrastructure
> - Multiple funds created for economic restoration and humanitarian issues
> - Free trade agreement with the United States
>
> **Nuclear & Energy (Points 11-12)**
>
> - Ukraine remains non-nuclear per NPT
> - Zaporizhia nuclear plant jointly operated by Ukraine, U.S., and Russia (no compromise reached yet)
>
> **Social & Territorial (Points 13-16)**
>
> - Both sides implement tolerance education programs; Ukraine adopts EU rules on religious tolerance and minority language protection
> - **Most difficult point:** Current troop deployment lines in Donetsk, Luhansk, Zaporizhia, and Kherson become de facto contact line; Russia must withdraw from Dnipropetrovsk, Mykolaiv, Sumy, and Kharkiv regions for agreement to take effect
> - No future territorial changes by force
> - Russia allows Ukraine commercial use of Dnipro River and Black Sea; Kinburn Spit demilitarized
>
> **Implementation (Points 17-20)**
>
> - All-for-all POW exchange (including those convicted since 2014); return of all civilian detainees, hostages, children, and political prisoners
> - Ukraine holds elections as soon as possible after signing
> - Peace Council chaired by Trump monitors implementation; includes Ukraine, Europe, NATO, Russia, and U.S.; sanctions for violations
> - Ceasefire takes effect immediately upon agreement

How charmingly both delegations describe their Florida soirée—”productive,” “constructive,” all those reassuring adjectives deployed like diplomatic sandbags. Witkoff’s [memo](https://x.com/SEPeaceMissions/status/2002826320615444873?s=20) reads like a corporate retreat agenda: four key documents, timelines discussed, boxes ticked. One almost expects a PowerPoint on synergistic peace frameworks.

Z, meanwhile, delivers his Christmas address amid 635 Russian drones and 38 missiles targeting energy infrastructure—the ninth such large-scale strike since January. He insists Ukraine “will never be an obstacle to peace” while noting Russia must take ending the war “100% seriously.” This, as Russian mechanized columns of 24 armored vehicles get obliterated near Pokrovsk in daylight assaults so incompetent even Russian milbloggers are complaining.

The gap between Mar-a-Lago’s strategic planning sessions and Ukraine’s smoldering power stations represents not merely a diplomatic challenge but a category error. One side drafts 20-point plans; the other launches Christmas strikes. Guess which approach Putin finds more persuasive?
