It is unsettling at best to see someone once considered a hero descend into madness, yet curiously satisfying to see the product of this madness cleanly and elegantly blown to smithereens.

Yesterday I promised myself I wouldn't write about Ukraine this week. That lasted exactly 24 hours. But the connections between yesterday's post about military careerists and war profiteers and what I'm seeing in today's Ukraine discourse are too obvious to ignore[1](#footnote-1).

I had great hopes for becoming a reliable source of comment and information, freed from the sentimental histrionics and pre-emptive ridicule of opposing views that characterize war bloggers, who have evolved predictably: first licking their wounds after arguing all-out invasion was unlikely, then taking grim pleasure in cataloguing every mistake made by those resisting occupation, and finally coalescing—with shuddering inevitability—into a lockstep neoauthoritarian echo chamber. [The most cynical observations we made last year](https://abdymok.substack.com/p/day-1013) about those who would exploit inconceivable human tragedy for political gain have not only proven true, but have somehow become immune to criticism.

[Yekaterina](https://www.instagram.com/p/C59Esv4tahI/) waking up a last year.

Conversely, I expect to be free of the gloomy handwringing that characterizes some in opposition, among whom, now and again, a flurry of concern arises over What Must Be Done. It shouldn't be in the least surprising that xenophobic idiots driven by bloodlust and malformed MAGAism are sharing their opinions about how Ukraine should surrender every chance they get.

What will be made evident, I hope, is that annexation of any part of Ukraine is not a reflexive ideological matter, born out of party allegiances, 'anti-Americanism,' or what colour underwear one prefers. It is rather that the world is being sold a bill of goods: one that is apparently having some success convincing people the invasion has anything to do with the restoration of Russia's empire, or revenge for the implosion of the USSR, or weapons of mass destruction, or the protection of security interests, or—most gallingly of all—'regime change' for the good of the fraternal relations of Slavic brothers.

As opposed, let's say, to it being about salvaging at any cost power grabs that have been, from every imaginable perspective, unmitigated disasters.

It's amusing to note that the most lurid Cold War fantasies of covert propaganda spread on behalf of our own President's Office by chatbots have been rendered irrelevant by a chattering horde of useful idiots [now serving it up for free](https://x.com/Sassovivente/status/1957804491991839053) every day: who needs black ops when you've got an account on X.

[Image]

Speaking of useful idiots, yesterday's exploration of how military experience gets packaged and sold brought me back to those two big breaks on the stroll downtown that happened in between Meaghan [luxuriating on Potemkin’s steps](https://www.instagram.com/p/DNsp1A83Mx0/), standing [in a field of ripe sunflowers](https://www.instagram.com/p/DNYd7gGi60r/) or keening [at GUR's memorial on Rybalsky Island](https://www.instagram.com/p/DNffTNSubXJ/?img_index=1).

Raise your hand if you enlisted in the US military because of the 2003 invasion of Iraq.

[Video: https://youtu.be/fKGc\_xaQkUU]

Which brings me directly to John, again, who today [opines](https://www.washingtonexaminer.com/opinion/beltway-confidential/3780298/trump-utterly-incoherent-ukraine-strategy-john-bolton/) that Donald's interference in our tragic bloody war has been incoherent.

[Image]

*Incoherent?* More like psychotic.

And back to [award-winning Keith](https://x.com/ZUa/status/1960023126336500058), who, we recall, re-raised his hand in 2003 and played a critical role in the disastrous US occupation of Iraq as the director of operations of the Coalition Provisional Authority.

That didn't end well.

The pattern here isn't subtle: the same voices that cheered us into Iraq are now positioning themselves as Ukraine experts, their previous disasters somehow qualifying them as strategic geniuses rather than disqualifying them entirely. It's a remarkable feat of rehabilitation—one that would be impressive if it weren't so predictably, er, unhelpful.

But hey, at least they're consistent in their inconsistency.
