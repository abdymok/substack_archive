I had intended to write a long uninspiring post about the defense of Kupyansk in Kharkiv region.

[Image]

Specifically about the enemy bridgehead on the western bank of the Oskil, stretching for almost 40 kilometers, varying between 3-5 kilometers wide, because its dismemberment is near and dear to my heart.

[Image]

But I’ve been distracted by pundits bloviating about what politicians have been saying at the UNGA.

Trump was **locked in** all day yesterday. Now we’ll spend the next month dragging him back to earth. Let’s start with Ukraine.

He didn’t actually say anything new—though try explaining that to people who treat his words like gospel at a tent revival. The key is metaphor. Luckily, late-night comics have it covered. Jimmy Kimmel nailed it: Trump changes positions so fast he should be sponsored by IKEA—”Some assembly required, instructions in Swedish, and it still collapses.”

That’s where we are. Z and Lavrov both show up, each promising victory. The EU waves €50 billion around. Trump does what any seasoned con artist would: nods at everyone, pockets the cash, and plays the waiting game. Eventually someone blinks, someone else gets the call, and Umerov buys another house in Boca Raton.

Anne [gushes](https://archive.fo/XoqsB#selection-615.0-922.0) over Ukraine’s manicure-to-missile pivot—nail techs building drones that torch Russian oil. Nick dutifully [tracks](https://edition.cnn.com/2025/09/24/europe/trump-ukraine-putin-land-analysis-intl) Trump’s flip-flops like they’re foreign policy instead of performance art. David gravely [explains](https://www.nytimes.com/2025/09/24/us/politics/trump-ukraine.html) how the man ping-pongs between “Ukraine’s doomed” and “Ukraine’s unstoppable” with all the authority of a Magic 8-Ball.

The Kremlin plays animal kingdom—Russia’s a paper tiger, then a bear, but never a paper bear. Z sticks to his greatest hits: friends and weapons, because treaties don’t stop Kalibrs and his English-language vocab is limited.

Meanwhile, the pundit class nods sagely, quotes extensively, and pats itself on the back for moral clarity—all while eyeing gas prices.

Which brings us back to Kupyansk and “the large air compressor with a variety of accessories.” It’s for boring (#2 on the list).

This gadget provides sustained high-pressure, high-volume air delivery that can power multiple heavy-duty tools continuously—the perfect donation to defenders of Kupyansk, who can use it for numerous heavy-duty tasks, such as tunnel excavation and surface mining operations.
