**What happened:** There were drones over Poland yesterday. Some time ago, NATO promised Article 5 would guarantee Poland's safety. The smirking chimp in the Kremlin sent the drones anyway. The alliance, inside its Brussels headquarters, was very excited to issue a strongly-worded statement.

Ukraine's former Commander-in-Chief Valeriy Zaluzhny [has for months](https://eutoday.net/zaluzhny-nato-is-not-ready-for-the-war/) been warning that NATO might "go the way of the United Nations—paralysed by its own structure and outdated assumptions."

But Western officials, including US Ambassador to NATO Matthew Whitaker, keep insisting the alliance has never been stronger.

[Video: https://youtu.be/voNwoRuCW5g]

These are the inevitable posturings from those who daily recite Cold War catechisms via PowerPoint, from those who glibly claim readiness for wars they fundamentally misunderstand, from those so fully infused with bureaucratic self-preservation that it no longer matters what the threat is.

Someone just asked what I thought of this.

Zaluzhny was right, obviously.

Apropos of Putin's message to NATO:

> "Let your allies walk past my drones without air defense, their Article 5 guarantees left at headquarters, while you stay three time zones away from any real response. I pledge complete safety."

Which brings me to the recipe for institutional collapse:

Take one military alliance, aged 75 years. Stuff it with procedures designed for fighting the last war. Season with strategic complacency and American indifference. Should the alliance not be prepared for modern warfare, pretend it is anyway. If you can't be bothered updating doctrine for drone swarms, issue another communiqué and let's get on with it.

Roast slowly until the contradictions are exposed and the whole thing falls apart. Serves nobody.

The drones over Poland are a pop quiz NATO hasn't studied for. While Zaluzhny explained that modern warfare runs on "drones, algorithms, and high-precision systems," NATO was shopping for expensive ways to intercept $50,000 drones with million-dollar missiles. It's bringing cavalry to cyber-war.

Putin chose his moment exquisitely—Trump back in office, European allies thoroughly debased by their diplomatic genuflections, the transatlantic façade worn thin. Those drones didn't just violate Polish airspace; they violated the premise that NATO's enemies still play by NATO's rules.

Zaluzhny warned this war would "last as long as it takes for a new technological equilibrium to emerge." Putin is building that equilibrium while NATO tries to remember where they put the old one.

The alliance will now discover whether Article 5 is a security guarantee or just expensive letterhead.

My money's on letterhead.
