# Flying Blind and Loving It

Czesław Miłosz had a useful term for people who mistake their intellectual submission for wisdom: *ketman*—the art of paying lip service to official doctrine while privately maintaining doubts. Today's Trump administration has managed something even more impressive: they've eliminated the doubts entirely by firing everyone who might have them.

The New York Times [reports](https://www.nytimes.com/2025/08/21/us/politics/trump-government-expertise.html) that Trump has gutted Russian expertise across the intelligence community. "They're flying blind without the expertise," warns one former official. But this misses the point—they *want* to fly blind. Expertise is the enemy of certainty, and certainty is Trump's brand.

Remember the old Galician Jew's wisdom: anyone claiming to be 100% right is "a fanatic, a thug, and the worst kind of rascal." Trump's approach to Putin embodies precisely this fanaticism. "I think he wants to make a deal for me," he told Macron, captured on a hot mic—the voice of a man who has already decided what reality must be.

This is *[ukrainesplaining](https://abdymok.substack.com/p/ukrainesplaining?utm_source=publication-search)*in reverse. Where the ukrainesplainers we skewered exaggerated Ukrainian agency and mystery (Snyder's breathless take on Z staying in Kyiv: "It was a mystery, in the profound sense of the word"—oh, puhleaze), Trump's team has decided Russian expertise itself is the problem. Why puzzle over Putin's actual intentions when you can simply declare yourself uniquely gifted at reading him?

Miłosz understood how this works. In *The Captive Mind*, he showed that intellectual captivity requires not just accepting official narratives, but actively rejecting inconvenient knowledge. The captive mind doesn't merely ignore expertise—it purges experts as threats to preferred reality.

"Let's just hope we aren't irradiated and freeze to death during the process"—now carries extra bite. The Biden team at least maintained the pretense of balancing competing risks while trying to avoid "direct conflict" (whatever that meant). Trump's crew has taken the cleaner approach: eliminate the risk assessors entirely.

The fired officials include a senior CIA Russia analyst, the NSA's top data scientist on Chinese cyber threats, and three people who worked on Russian influence operations. Their crime? Having institutional memory that contradicts the narrative of Trump as Putin whisperer extraordinaire.

Miłosz packed his intellectual baggage with writers who understood imperial power and its seductions. Trump's team travels lighter—they've simply discarded the baggage handlers who might point out inconvenient destinations.
