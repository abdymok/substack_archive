# An Annotated Manifesto Against Tidying Up Joy

Marie, or KonMarie as she insists we call her (because one name wasn’t quite guru enough), has graced Google again this year with wisdom about finding joy at work. Her previous appearance concerned the radical notion of cleaning up after yourself—riveting stuff for a company worth billions.

In a TIME interview, we recall, she revealed keeping a musical tuner at home. When it rings, she claims, it purifies the air and calms her mind. One might as well keep a toaster. It makes toast. Same principle.

Here’s what she won’t tell you about that tuner: Musical scales themselves expose the fraud of perfect order. Between any two notes lies infinite possibility, yet humans choose discrete pitches—the five-note pentatonic, the seven-note diatonic, the twelve-tone chromatic. These emerge not from tidiness but from physical resonance: strings vibrating at 3/2 ratios, creating the pleasure we call consonance.

Our elaborate system was cultural choice, not cosmic law. We learned that in Solfeggio.

The tuner rings at 440 Hz—A above middle C—but only because we agreed in 1939. It’s arbitrary. Organized. Tidy.

Real music—real joy—lives in embodied knowledge, not imposed order. You can’t spark-joy your way to Oklou. On the occasion of yet another genuflection before the tidy person (that sound you hear is techno-fascist knees hitting floor), remember: the tuner only knows one note. Music requires all of them, messy and glorious and true.

This is something to think about when you are walking up eight flights of stairs.
