Imagine, for a moment, the future of negotiations to end Russia’s invasion of Ukraine.

You're minding your own business, scrolling through Telegram channels during an air alert[1](#footnote-1), checking White House [briefings](https://www.whitehouse.gov/articles/2025/08/sunday-shows-president-trumps-pursuit-of-peace/) or something, and you plug a wire into the back of your head so you can physically experience the diplomatic side of bullshit.

[Image]

Now imagine surfing to the latest peace proposal. Quickly, you click on a random framework, only to find yourself drowning in "security guarantees" that guarantee nothing. You follow another thread that brings you to Marco Rubio's transformation—an experience that reminds you of the shower scene from Alfred Hitchcock's *Psycho*.

There's no escape from this theater of the absurd, where Vova is now just a misunderstood negotiating partner, and Little Marco—once Vova's harshest critic—has undergone what he calls [a "transformation" on foreign policy](https://www.nytimes.com/2025/08/20/us/politics/marco-rubio-ukraine.html). Translation: he learned to read the room, and the room belongs to Donald.

[Image]

Donald has delegated the impossible task of crafting "security guarantees" to Little Marco, who must now square the circle: reassure Ukraine while appeasing Vova, satisfy Europeans while avoiding NATO. It's diplomatic origami with a hand grenade.

The irony is delicious. Meaghan [warns](https://www.msn.com/en-us/news/politics/trump-stands-at-a-churchill-chamberlain-crossroads-over-ukraines-sovereignty-says-kelloggs-daughter/ar-AA1KdWfn?apiversion=v2&noservercache=1&domshim=1&renderwebcomponents=1&wcseo=1&batchservertelemetry=1&noservertelemetry=1) that Donald faces a "Churchill-Chamberlain crossroads" where "you cannot trade another nation's sovereignty for the illusion of stability." Yet that's precisely what's happening, with everyone pretending otherwise.

There's something about the sense of isolation and open space in these negotiations, which evokes the Eternal Struggle—man against man against nature. Sovereignty claims all in the end, but it is the struggle over territory that defines diplomacy.

[Image]

The Kremlin continues its masterclass in gaslighting. Lavrov [suggests](https://www.reuters.com/world/europe/russia-says-talks-ukraines-security-without-moscow-are-road-nowhere-2025-08-20/) Russia wants "truly reliable" security guarantees—meaning the 2022 Istanbul framework that would neuter Ukraine permanently, ban NATO membership, and require Russian permission for Western military aid. It's like offering to guard the henhouse while sharpening your claws.

Also, there is a kick to the balls, strong enough to lift a diplomat off the ground, yet not strong enough to produce a blood-curdling shriek. It's Vova’s negotiating strategy: demand everything, concede nothing, let time do the work. He's supposedly agreed to meet Z, except Lavrov clarifies this means more working groups and preliminary discussions. It's diplomatic "check's in the mail."

Europeans keep begging for "Article 5-like" protections—NATO guarantees without NATO—while Trump rules out American troops but suggests Europeans are ready for "boots on the ground." European leaders must now sell their publics on dying for Ukrainian sovereignty while Washington washes its hands. Automatically the funniest thing about this whole circus.

The most revealing detail: analysts discuss the "gray areas" including territorial divisions, Crimea's status, and—delightfully—"Z's fate." Despite "mutual desire to remove Z," there's disagreement on methodology. Should they undermine him politically or wait for resignation-by-exhaustion?

Ukraine sits impossibly caught between Donald's impatience and Vova's patience, between European sympathy and American indifference. Z is "caught between Donald and the degradation of the front"—diplomatic speak for running out of time and options.

The proposed solution involves elaborate kabuki theater where Putin magnanimously "exchanges" Ukrainian territory he doesn't control for territory he desperately wants, while everyone pretends this constitutes negotiation. It's "yours for yours," except one side's "yours" was never theirs. Okay, it's a bit weird, but I suppose this sort of thing does happen from time to time in international relations. Whoever designed this peace process neglected to put in any actual peace.

Most remarkably, this charade sells as American diplomatic success. Rubio now soothes that "we can't end the war without talking to Vova"—conveniently ignoring whether the conversation ends the war or just pauses it until Putin wants round two.

The tragedy isn't that this might fail—it's that it might succeed, creating Meaghan's ["illusion of stability."](https://www.msn.com/en-us/news/politics/trump-stands-at-a-churchill-chamberlain-crossroads-over-ukraines-sovereignty-says-kelloggs-daughter/ar-AA1KdWfn?apiversion=v2&noservercache=1&domshim=1&renderwebcomponents=1&wcseo=1&batchservertelemetry=1&noservertelemetry=1) Europe returns to "hybrid war," Ukraine loses territory and sovereignty, Putin claims vindication.

And your pain is irrelevant.

Principles are negotiable, sovereignty is a suggestion, and peace means whatever the strongest party says it means. Churchill would be mortified; Chamberlain would feel vindicated.
