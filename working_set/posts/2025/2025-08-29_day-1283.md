*A cloudless late summer morning in Kyiv.*

Peeked at what Team Russia blew up yesterday morning on the right bank of the Dnipro. Not pretty.

Two 9M727 Iskander-K cruise missiles decided to redecorate most of a city block, blowing out windows like some deranged urban renewal project. Didn't have time to hunt for craters—too busy dodging glass. Across the river in Darnytsky district, missiles turned a 5-story residential building into rubble. Police bodycam footage captured the first responders stumbling through that particular horror show. Libkos was there, too.

[Image]

Meanwhile, somewhere in the rarefied air of international diplomacy, European officials are having what can only be described as an existential crisis. They're still "mystified," apparently, by what exactly Putin promised Trump's envoy Steve Witkoff during their cozy August chat in Moscow.

Here's the thing about Putin's promises: they tend to explode on contact with reality.

While diplomats parsed the fine print of "de jure recognition" and territorial concessions, Putin was busy demonstrating his negotiating style with high explosives. The Trump administration rolled out an actual red carpet for Vlad in Alaska—complete with B-2 flyover and presidential applause—while Kyiv residents got a different kind of morning show: incoming cruise missiles.

Team USA's **pathetic attempts** at both-sidesing this conflict is almost performance art at this point. At the White House briefing, they managed to equate Ukraine hitting military targets with Russia vaporizing apartment buildings. It's like comparing a surgeon's scalpel to a chainsaw and calling them both "cutting tools."

> Q: Two questions on two separate issues. First of all, the drone and missile strikes that took place overnight in Ukraine, General Keith Kellogg, the president's special envoy, has described these attacks as egregious. Do you agree with that description? And do you view President Putin as an impediment to getting peace between these two countries?
>
> A: Well, it's not about if I agree, it's about what the president thinks and I know what the president thinks because I talked to him about this. He was not happy about this news, but he was also not surprised. These are two countries that have been at war for a very long time. Russia launched this attack on Kyiv.
>
> And likewise, Ukraine recently dealt a blow to Russia's oil refineries. They have taken out, as a matter of fact, 20 percent of Russia's oil refinery capacity over the course of their attacks throughout the month of August. So the president is continuing to watch this intently and this killing, unfortunately, will continue as long as the war continues, which is why the president wants it to end.
>
> And that's why he's worked harder than anyone to end this war, which would have never started if he were president. But perhaps, both sides of this war are not ready to end it themselves. The president wants it to end, but the leaders of these two countries need it to end and must want it to end as well.
>
> And I think the president will make some additional statements on this later. ([RollCall](https://rollcall.com/factbase/trump/transcript/donald-trump-press-conference-briefing-karoline-leavitt-august-28-2025/), August 28, White House briefing)

The diplomatic dance continues with all the grace of a three-legged waltz. Trump's peace envoy—a real estate guy with zero diplomatic experience—apparently thought he'd cracked the code when Putin offered to give up "legal claims" to territories while conveniently forgetting to mention the thousands of Russian soldiers who'd be staying put. It's like agreeing to sell your house but keeping the keys and half your furniture.

European officials warned their American counterparts that Putin's word games usually end with more Ukrainian cities looking like today's crime scene. But hey, who needs details when you're making history?

Trump promised to end this war in 24 hours 53 times on the campaign trail. We're seven months in, and his latest diplomatic breakthrough coincided perfectly with this morning's missile barrage. 23 dead, including children—but at least we're "watching this intently."
