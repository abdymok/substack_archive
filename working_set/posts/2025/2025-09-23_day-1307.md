A "democratic autocrat" is about as much of an oxymoron as a "scientific feeling." But you can't choose your contradictions—an artifact remains an artifact, and it now needs explaining.

Jamie does a horrible job[1](#footnote-1).

Personally, the situation most closely resembles those "party purges" that early Bolsheviks practiced religiously. A movement that seizes power inevitably attracts hordes of "fellow travelers" who see it primarily as career advancement opportunities.

The essence doesn't change even if movements like Z's were originally conceived for genuine transformation. First, that was ages ago!—now everything's deadly serious, subordinated to lining pockets and preserving power. Second, initially, these opportunities were meant "for the people."

In Ukraine, the ruling party doesn't need "democratic suckers"—it will fleece them, retaining only distilled loyal cores that oscillate with the line instinctively.

Meanwhile, Putin performs his own purge theater. His defense industry can't afford new factories while he waves nuclear treaties around like party invitations. The man who's violated every international agreement since Yalta now lectures about arms control—a real "circus with bears.[2](#footnote-2)"

And Trump? He's discovered autism's cause: Tylenol. "This is based on what I feel," the president declared, dismissing decades of research for his gut instincts. Don't take Tylenol while pregnant, he commanded. Space out vaccines. The hepatitis B shot for infants? Disgraceful. His health team—Kennedy, Makary, Bhattacharya, Oz—stood awkwardly behind him as he went rogue, endorsing fringe theories they wouldn't touch. "They are waiting for certain studies. I don't—I just want to say it like it is.[3](#footnote-3)"

The pattern's identical across continents: paranoid leaders have reached the same conclusion. In besieged fortresses, internal criticism becomes externally weaponized. Ukrainian lawmakers questioning corruption become Russian stooges. Russian oligarchs become Western agents. American scientists become deep-state conspirators.

Ukraine fights for European values while adopting Russian methods. Russia claims superpower status while its economy resembles a Ponzi scheme. America preaches evidence-based policy while its president prescribes medical advice based on "feelings."

Here's where [Aumann's theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aumann%27s_agreement_theorem#:~:text=The%20theorem%20was%20proved%20by,theoretic%20definition%20of%20common%20knowledge.) becomes darkly relevant: rational actors with shared priors who make their posteriors common knowledge cannot disagree. Yet Putin, Z, and Trump—all facing similar evidence about democratic governance, expertise, and legitimacy—have reached identical conclusions through supposedly different reasoning. Their convergence on authoritarian solutions suggests either they're sharing priors we can't see, or they've stopped being rational actors altogether.

The drones still fly. The purges accelerate. And somewhere in Odesa, people wait out the latest air alert in a bomb shelter decorated by the Feldman sisters.
