Убей президента!

Убей эту сволочь, которая

так гнусно топорщит усы

над нашими всё же просторами,

над ликом родимой красы.

Чем можешь убей, не раздумывай,

возьми автомат иль топор

и вскрой эту голову “умную”,

и выбрось в клоаку, как сор.

Убей эту мразь авторучкою

иль зонтиком, или “пером”,

иль вовсе ма—аленькой штучкою,

подброшенной в терпкий ром.

Убей же врага, чтобы молодость

твоя не осталась в дерьме,

чтоб Край наш

воспрянул с гордостью

во славе, а не в ярме!

Убей аккуратно, дерзостно,

как может убить лишь Бог,

покончи геройски с мерзостью

и выкинь её за порог!

Очищенный дом беларуский наш,

проветренный,

солнцу представленный,

он будет закрыт для усатых саш,

но миру свободному явленный.

*November 30, 1995 ([Славамір Адамовіч](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavamir_Adamovich))*

Misha was yucking it up in Kyiv with Trey and Boris for the Pinchuk [whatever](https://yes-ukraine.org/) last week.

He’s Austrialia’s answer to Meaghan Mobbs, the daughter of Trump’s special envoy to Ukraine, Keith Kellogg, who had the gall to say [this recently on X](https://x.com/mobbs_mentality/status/1968628135982776491):

> As always many things can be true at once:
>
> 1. This veiled criticism of the US seems to be about Kimmel. Freedom of speech in America doesn’t mean freedom from consequence. He purposefully misled his audience.
>
> 2. Cancel culture in the US was predominantly about dragging past comments into the light and forcing unrestrained action in the present - sometimes decades later. Being held responsible in the moment for poor choice isn’t a crack down on freedom, it’s reestablishing accountability.
>
> 3. A course correction of the hyper-politicization of our media is long overdue. Only 1/3 of Americans trust it. That’s very bad.
>
> 4. I have concerns about free press in the US, but not for the reasons being insinuated here. Bari Weiss resigned from the NYT in 2021, stating, “that truth isn’t a process of collective discovery, but an orthodoxy already known to an enlightened few whose job is to inform everyone else.” Her is trying to undo so much damage driven by the elite media class.
>
> 5. But there is always risk of an overcorrection, and this occasionally worries me. However, there are endless stories critical of the admin and no one has yet been fired for telling the truth. Those who lie because of their own ideological persuasion are no longer journalists, they are propagandists. They should be removed, or acknowledged as such.
>
> 6. I have found foreign perceptions of Trump to be heavily unbalanced and at times completely out of step with the feelings of average Americans. This is because their beliefs are driven by media that skews left of center.
>
> 7. Ukraine is many wonderful things, but it is undergoing its own difficulties with free speech. One, in particular, is self-censorship. Journalists have acknowledged withholding critical reporting on government missteps to preserve morale or to avoid exposing corruption that might cause foreign partners to hesitate on approving aid. Opposition media has also been subjected to government pressure.
>
> 8. There has also been no time in Ukrainian history where the Presidential office has held and concentrated so much power. And democracy doesn’t mean a lack of discord or strife. America’s system was built to be resilient because of the belief in her people. A belief I hold. Nations fall, empires collapse, tyrants rise and fade, yet the idea of self-government persists. This is because it speaks to something eternal in the human spirit: the longing to be free.

Ah, how perfectly martial: a West Point graduate lecturing us on the delicate balance of democracy while apparently confusing a television studio with a battlefield. One might think that four years at the Academy would have taught Meaghan the difference between enemy propaganda and Jimmy Kimmel making jokes, but evidently the curriculum has gaps.

And what delicious timing: to return from the actual trenches of an actual war against actual tyranny, only to declare that America's greatest threat to liberty is... late-night television accountability. One imagines the Ukrainian journalists—dodging actual missiles while our author tends to actual wounds—might have a rather different perspective on what constitutes press suppression.

But then again, when your training teaches you that complex problems require decisive action and clear chain of command, perhaps it's only natural to see messy democratic discourse as something that needs a good, crisp correction. How wonderfully American: exporting freedom abroad while demanding discipline at home.
