So here's the story according to Bloomberg's unnamed "[people close to the Kremlin](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-09-20/putin-decides-russia-can-step-up-ukraine-attacks-and-trump-won-t-act)" (who are definitely real and not just Putin's press secretary with a fake mustache): Vlad met Trump in Alaska, decided the orange man won't lift a finger for Ukraine, and is now bombing the shit out of everything because *strategy*.

Meanwhile, the Institute for the Study of War—those same brilliant minds who once declared Putin a "rational actor"—has helpfully explained that this Bloomberg leak is actually Putin's 4D chess move to... wait for it... *[psychologically manipulate Western audiences through strategic media placement](https://understandingwar.org/research/russia-ukraine/russian-offensive-campaign-assessment-september-20-2025/)*.

Let's pause here. We're supposed to believe Putin—a man who invaded his neighbor because he was afraid of NATO expansion—is now playing subtle information warfare games through financial news outlets? The same Putin whose propaganda apparatus includes gems like "Ukrainian Nazi biothrowing mosquitoes"?

ISW breathlessly informs us that Putin "may have allowed these Kremlin sources" to leak, because apparently the FSB now runs on an honor system where officials casually chat with Bloomberg reporters. They've cracked the code: it's all about "exploiting friction between European and US officials."

Both reports share the delightful assumption that Putin's escalation represents some sort of calculated master plan rather than, say, the flailing of an autocrat whose "special military operation" has turned into a three-year meat grinder. Bloomberg frames it as Putin forcing Ukraine into talks "on his terms"—which is odd, considering his terms seem to involve Ukraine not existing.

We called this out back on Day 150ish when we noted how ISW had mistakenly assessed Putin as a "rational actor" in December 2021. Apparently some lessons take longer to learn than others[1](#footnote-1).

The real comedy gold? ISW warning that this Bloomberg article might "create fear in Ukrainian society ahead of Winter 2025-2026." Because apparently Ukrainians, who've been getting bombed for three years straight, were just waiting for a Bloomberg scoop to realize things might get cold and explodey.

Here's a radical thought: maybe Putin is bombing Ukraine's infrastructure not because of some elaborate post-Alaska revelation, but because bombing civilian infrastructure is what war criminals do. Maybe the timing has less to do with Trump's Alaska body language and more to do with Russia's desperate need to show *something* resembling progress before another winter of international isolation.

But sure, let's pretend this is all part of Putin's brilliant psychological operation rather than the predictable tantrum of a dictator whose invasion went sideways. After all, nothing says "strategic genius" like being surprised that invading your neighbor might take longer, er, than 1,306 days.

Watching these unfolding events, aspiring philosopher Volodymyr Pastukhov [is starting to think](https://t.me/v_pastukhov/1659) that every country's political trajectory needs its own "Kirov assassination moment." We still don't know for certain why Nikolaev killed Kirov in 1934, or whether he even did it. Was it an impulsive act by a cuckolded husband, or was the infidelity merely cover for deeper political motives? Perhaps it was simply years of accumulated rage against perceived injustice. The specifics, ultimately, don't matter.

What matters is that Stalin seized this assassination to crush all opposition and cement his personal dictatorship. The parallels write themselves: the precise motivations behind political violence become irrelevant once power decides to exploit the moment. History suggests we should be less concerned with parsing Putin's Alaska revelations and more focused on watching who benefits from the narrative that emerges.
