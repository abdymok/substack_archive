> My very brief and sketchy impressions of yet another Z-Trump meeting.
>
> I can’t shake the feeling that world leaders are, how shall I put it, not entirely honest with Volodymyr Z. I reject the idea that he’s simply naïve and invents unattainable plans out of thin air. I think he’s being offered advances - political IOUs, promissory gestures he clings to like straws. And those straws keep breaking, one after another. Yet he never gives up and keeps reaching for the next one.
>
> To be fair, that’s exactly how he’s managed to stay afloat - from straw to straw, for three years now. By any expert’s calculation, he should have drowned long ago. And yet he’s still treading water. The trouble is, you can’t rise on that - you can only keep floundering, waiting for the sun to evaporate the water so you can finally push off the bottom. Unless, of course, something knocks from below again.
>
> The Tomahawk story is just the latest straw. The core of the problem is simple: from the very beginning, it was made clear to Z that Europe (and, back then, America as well) was ready to help him fight - but not to fight for him, and certainly not with him.
>
> That formula needs to stay in mind whenever we discuss the realism or wishfulness of any new promise. The West - and by that, I mean both sides of the Atlantic - will do absolutely nothing that could even remotely risk being drawn into this war alongside or instead of Ukraine.
>
> And they won’t do it for one plain reason: not a single Western leader, not even the most hawkish Baltics or Poles, has a public mandate for war. What they have, at best, is a mandate to support Ukraine. And even that is increasingly questioned - both from the right and the left.
>
> What can actually be expected are two things.
>
> First, dancing on the edge - cautiously probing where exactly the line lies beyond which Moscow would strike not at Ukraine, but elsewhere. (A dangerous but exhilarating game, great for adrenaline junkies.)
>
> Second, attempts to provoke some third party into doing what you don’t want to do yourself. Europe still dreams this could become “Trump’s war.” Trump, on the other hand, wishes the Europeans all possible success in making it theirs.
>
> The Tomahawk case is even simpler.
>
> Russia already has weapons roughly in the same class - Kalibr, for example, and the U.S. already has plenty of enemies besides Moscow. So the obvious counterstep would be to deliver “symmetrical” weapons to some freedom-loving seafarers in Yemen or defenders of democracy in Venezuela. Biden has, in fact, already been warned about this scenario.
>
> But that’s just an illustration. Moscow still has plenty of ways to make a mess - that’s an objective reality, palpable and undeniable, yet one the West is still unwilling to internalise. Unfortunately, that doesn’t make it any less real. Which is why the outcome of Z’s Washington visit was entirely predictable: there will be no Tomahawks. There will be nothing that poses a real threat to Putin’s regime, because if that regime ever faces the prospect of losing power, it’s capable of anything. And every Western leader, including Trump, knows this, or at least pretends to.
>
> The bottom line:
>
> Ukraine will not be allowed a swift end to this war - no knockout, not even a knockdown. She’s being steered toward a long match, where the best possible outcome is a win on points, if she lasts until the final round.
>
