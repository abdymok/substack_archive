“I don’t relish the idea of taking life, but I’m doing what I feel is right,” says Derek Huffman, formerly an Arizona welder, now a Russian conscript[1](#footnote-1). What he’s “doing” is helping reduce Ukraine’s power grid to rubble while his wife livestreams their “escape from LGBT indoctrination.” The reward: expedited Russian citizenship and a [YouTube following](https://www.youtube.com/@HuffmanTime). The extraordinary life he advertises is complicity in war crimes, branded and monetized.

Two weeks ago, Putin and Trump convened in Alaska to “end the war.” It produced exactly what one would expect from three hours of cameras and handshakes: nothing. Since then, Moscow has treated Ukraine to another missile deluge while Xi, Putin, and a gout-ridden Lukashenko waddled across Beijing red carpets. Xi spoke of a “more just international order.” Translation: fuck the current one. Lukashenko, barely able to shuffle a hundred feet, has been demoted from Europe’s last dictator to geopolitical slapstick.

The contrast is instructive. The West confuses diplomacy with stagecraft—[Macron with his summits](https://www.lemonde.fr/en/international/article/2025/09/01/macron-and-starmer-will-co-chair-paris-summit-on-ukraine-security-guarantees-with-Z_6744932_4.html), Starmer with his [“security guarantees.](https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/cx2qr08l1yko)” Russia answers with missile diplomacy and the strategic flick of the thermostat. CNN, in its infinite solemnity, warns that “[winter is coming](https://edition.cnn.com/2025/09/02/politics/winter-is-coming-for-ukraine-what-it-will-take-to-keep-the-lights-on),” as though blackouts were meteorology rather than deliberate war crimes measured in degrees Celsius.

[Image]

Meanwhile, Ukraine produces lifestyle content. [The Kyiv Independent](http://kyivindependent.com)’s new pitch: “[Dare to Ukraine Village](https://kyivindependent.com/dare-to-ukraine/),” in which a host abandons Kyiv for a $500 rural budget and a dog named Letti. Raw, real, occasionally ridiculous—so long as the power stays on. Chess nights in Kyiv, erotic calendars, influencer vlogs: the show goes on, commodified survival with sponsors attached.

[Image]

Moscow, too, has joined the content economy. Huffman is the poster child: an American who fled pronouns only to embrace genocide. Sholem Aleichem might have said: you cannot spit out your citizenship. But you can apparently exchange it for complicity, provided you upload faithfully.

The grotesque does not end there. After MP Andriy Parubiy’s confessed killer made headlines, his ex-wife took to Facebook to reclaim her son’s memory: divorced since 1998, she raised him alone, watched him die as a junior sergeant with the 93rd Brigade in Bakhmut. “My son, a hero, died for Ukraine. Heroes do not die!” she wrote[2](#footnote-2). While parents bury their sons, Western readers are invited to marvel at budget survival blogs.

And still the clicks accumulate. Martyrdom-by-choice in Moscow, Kyiv’s lifestyle experiments, CNN’s seasonal dispatches. The algorithm is impartial to tragedy. It rewards the packaged story—whether genocide, stupidity or death.

The fiddler plays while the roof burns. Xi smirks, Lukashenko limps, Putin plots, and the West applauds its own handshakes. Winter approaches, armed and deliberate. And somewhere, inevitably, someone will turn it into content—raw, real, occasionally ridiculous—streaming live, assuming the lights stay on.
