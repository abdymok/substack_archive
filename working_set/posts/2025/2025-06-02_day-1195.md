Our months-long experiment to turn lactic acid into running fuel is yielding results. During high-intensity exercise, when oxygen is limited, muscles convert glucose into energy via anaerobic glycolysis, producing lactic acid as a by-product. The lactate produced is transported from muscles to the liver, where it can be converted back into glucose through the Cori cycle, which can then be recycled and used as fuel for slow-twitch muscles[1](#footnote-1).
