A friend from Dnipro gave me a gigantic piece of cake. A small piece is on the plate. Almonds on top, some carrots and dried fruits. A little heavier than I expected, but 9 on a cake scale of 1 to 10.

Other than a brief stretch in Hrodna and a few months in the center of Warsaw (we didn’t quite get along), I lived in Minsk for almost four years. I liked it there.

[Image]

Late one night about 30 years ago I was coming home in Minsk on the bridge over the Svislach River. I was feeling, oh, *moderately* misanthropic, and eager to get home to bed. After swinging around into the park from the long curve by Victory Square, traffic suddenly came to a dead halt. I was boxed in on four sides by police with truncheons and shields. After several minutes of bewilderment, I saw that cars were held up not because of something terrible like an accident, or merely inconvenient like an opposition march: it was a bunch of teenagers, who had emptied bags of glass marbles at the top of the hill.

And thus began my long and progressively more hostile relationship with law-enforcers in general.
