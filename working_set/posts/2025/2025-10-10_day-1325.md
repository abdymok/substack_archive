Our *turning off the lights post* on [Day 1323](https://abdymok.substack.com/p/day-1323) was prophetic, because, er, Russia did just that early this morning at around 2 a.m..

A Russian drone smashed into the 17-storey residential building across from Zvyrinetska Metro in Pechersk district.

[Image]

The Left Bank looks dark.

The exact moment most of the city lost power was captured for posterity. I was outside witnessing the chaos. Thank god I took the stairs—elevators and blackouts mix about as well as my cooking and smoke detectors.

Back at the flat, our house guest, maybe a relative, a young woman whose fastidiousness could shame Marie Kondo, had taken refuge in the corridor—perfectly centered between two walls—deeply absorbed in whatever she was tapping on her phone. Even during a power outage, she’d apparently found the most structurally sound spot to swap notes about incoming missiles.

Eyes glazed over approximately 20 seconds into this fucking *reportage*:

[Image]

Liberalism, last time I checked: a political tradition centered on individual rights, liberties, and pluralism, governed by the rule of law rather than personalized power, that makes space for diverse conceptions of the good life while respecting human dignity and agency. You know, the boring stuff.

Back in March, [I noted](https://abdymok.substack.com/p/day-1109) we were neck-deep in the mature phase of our era of negativity—The Golden Age of Ameri[ꓘ](https://discussions.apple.com/thread/8205179)a—that whole trajectory from Minsk (1997) to Moscow (2000) to Vova’s first Ukrainian adventure (2014). I wrote then that the current wave of illiberalism was destructive, not constructive, and that when coercion runs out of steam, the negative phase ends. Simple physics, really.

What I didn’t fully game out was how quickly the bulldozer would wake the dragons.

The administration risks rousing Popperian rabble-rousers: enemies of patrimonialism who led the charge after the so-called end of history, who’ve since retired or stayed abroad. Push them too far, and they may reunite like ex-revolutionaries in *One Battle After Another*, dusting off the destabilization playbooks that once rocked Belarus, Georgia, Ukraine, Yugoslavia, Romania, and beyond when the USSR imploded. They’ll all be dragons again—assuming they haven’t already dropped dead.

This changes things. I had figured twenty to forty years of negativity. But maybe there are “subjective factors” that could “flatten the wave before it crests.” These weathered practitioners of open society building *are* that breakwater: experienced, networked, deeply allergic to the resurrection of the old normal they spent their youth dismantling.

The question is whether their toolkit still works in an era vaccinated by social media algorithms against progress. The ‘90s revolutions succeeded partly because information asymmetries favored the insurgents. Today’s digital landscape—more surveilled, more manipulated, more fractured—is different. But these operators cut their teeth navigating the KGB, Securitate, and Stasi. Perhaps algorithms are just the next iteration of the surveillance state they’ve already beaten once.

Whether they succeed or fail, their re-emergence confirms something: this isn’t just another pendulum swing. It’s a genuine contest between incompatible visions, fought by people who remember when the stakes were existential and are old enough not to give a shit about consequences.

Which, if nothing else, should make for decent entertainment.
