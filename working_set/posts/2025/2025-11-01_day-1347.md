This is Kyiv’s Botanical Gardens early Saturday morning. The park sits above Vydubychi Monastery, which dates to the 11th century when Vsevolod I was in charge. This was during the height of Kievan Rus’ power, though the state was beginning to experience the internal conflicts and succession disputes that would eventually lead to its fragmentation

[Image]

The political structure was complex, with various princes controlling different cities and territories, but Kyiv remained the most prestigious seat and its ruler held the title of Grand Prince. The peak of Kyivan Rus empire started crumbling in the middle of the 12th century.

> **The War of Exhaustion**
>
> Gone are the dreams of a victory parade on Khreshchatyk or a cup of coffee on Yalta’s embankment. The war is now about something else entirely – about who will collapse first from sheer exhaustion.
>
> [@ZUa](https://x.com/ZUa) believes in long-range missiles; [#Putin](https://x.com/hashtag/Putin?src=hashtag_click) still believes in Gerasimov and in the submission of the Russian people, ready to die without complaint in endless “meat assaults.” Europe believes in money, and that it will have enough to grind down, with Ukrainian hands, as many Russians as Putin can herd to the front. The real resource war is only beginning now. Welcome to its new phase – one run not by parade-ground generals, but by rear-echelon draft officers (in [#Ukraine](https://x.com/hashtag/Ukraine?src=hashtag_click), the TCC chiefs), a world of blackouts and petrol queues, drug shortages, and endless nighttime sirens on both sides of the front line. In fact, this is now the central front line of the war. We don’t know precisely what Z and the Europeans told Trump, but it seems they convinced him that this “second front” could be broken through. I can find no other explanation for Washington’s return to the concept of “peace after a ceasefire.”
>
> Let’s set aside, for now, the question of what exactly triggered [@realDonaldTrump](https://x.com/realDonaldTrump), and look instead at what underlies the confidence of Z and the Europeans that they can win this war:
>
> 1. The assumption that Europe can afford, economically and politically, to finance the war in Ukraine for several more years.
>
> 2. The assumption that Z will be able, over those same years, to keep replenishing the Armed Forces with manpower through mobilisation.
>
> 3. The assumption that Trump, at Europe’s request, will be able to sustain sanctions long enough to keep constraining Russia’s oil and gas revenues, leading to a slow but inevitable degradation of the Russian economy.
>
> 4. Europe has entirely reoriented itself toward a long-term resource war – and feels comfortable enough on its fronts to have shaken Trump’s conviction that Russia cannot be defeated.
>
> And what about [#Russia](https://x.com/hashtag/Russia?src=hashtag_click)? The [#Kremlin](https://x.com/hashtag/Kremlin?src=hashtag_click), it seems, has also adapted. It clearly understands the seriousness of the threat and, having revised its earlier optimistic assumption on the fly, is coming up with a new one – the only thing better than an ongoing war is the next war. The one not yet fought. Either way, a prolonged resource war with the collective West is not the kind of war the Kremlin dreamed of. As was [@MedvedevRussiaE](https://x.com/MedvedevRussiaE) prophetic words: “They’ll fuss for a while and then get used to it – everything will go back to normal.” Reaching the point of no return in relations with the West was never part of the plan. So, when asked whether Putin is ready to fight forever, I can now answer with some certainty: no.
>
> Right now, Putin genuinely wants to wind down the war in Ukraine as soon as possible – to wriggle out of an open-ended resource war with the West. But how? Two paths are obvious:
>
> 1. To win the resource war with Ukraine before he begins to lose the resource war with Europe. This means that military pressure on Ukraine will soon triple, regardless of human, financial, or reputational losses. In the coming months, the fighting will reach unprecedented brutality – an actual Doomsday war. But if Ukraine holds out under this onslaught (through spring 2026), I won’t venture to predict what happens next.
>
> 2. Moscow will focus its maximum effort on making Europe feel the weight of war and rethink its approach to funding Ukraine. There will be no invasion – simply because, with the army bogged down in Ukraine, there’s no one left to invade with – but every known form of hybrid warfare will be unleashed in the coming months.
>
> So I wouldn’t dismiss Putin’s promise of an “earth-shaking response.” The Kremlin knows full well that the war has reached a decisive moment – and it has no desire to let it drag on in a sluggish, low-intensity form.
