```
Have you forgotten what we were like then
when we were still first rate
and the day came fat with an apple in its mouth

it’s no use worrying about Time
but we did have a few tricks up our sleeves
and turned some sharp corners

the whole pasture looked like our meal
we didn’t need speedometers
we could manage cocktails out of ice and water

I wouldn’t want to be faster
or greener than now if you were with me O you
were the best of all my days
```

[1950[1](#footnote-1)]

---

So here we are: cocktails made from ice and water, my DD Form 214 lost to history, a former National Guardsman crying on camera, and 758 people left in Kupyansk waiting for the math to run out. The poem says we didn’t need speedometers. The Shahed drones disagree. Forty years since ETS and the US military still can’t figure out drone warfare but wants to practice on American cities—maybe start with the ones that need liberating from potholes.

[Image]

*We should use some of these dangerous cities as training grounds for our military — Trump (September 30, 2025)*

Meanwhile Russia pounds Kharkiv and Dnipro, the Zaporizhia generator stays dark, and someone somewhere is still teaching transcription to people with bad ears.

O’Hara got it right: “you were the best of all my days.” The trick is living long enough to say it, and having cities left standing where you can.
