Raise your hand if you were at Tushino Airfield in September 1991.

I drove a borrowed Kopeika—a VAZ-2101, the “one kopeck car”—about thirty minutes north from the Hotel Moskva, where Soviet deputies still kept rooms. The Berezovzhskaya Accords were months away. The Baltics had already bolted. Ukraine too. The USSR was coming apart in real time.

We picked up two girls from Pokrovskaya Gora along the Klyazma and headed for the airfield. What we found there was half a million Russians losing their minds.

[Video: https://youtu.be/\_W7wqQwa-TU]

I was wondering this morning why the Metallica concert came back to me. Then I realized: Thanksgiving 1992. That’s when I moved to Moscow for good. By then, the chaos had settled. By late 1993, the experiment was over.

Putin’s paying in blood for Ukrainian soil. They’re paying in cash for golden parachutes. The difference is Putin knows what he’s buying.
