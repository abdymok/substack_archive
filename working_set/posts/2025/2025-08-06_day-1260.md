One thousand two hundred and thirty days ago (Day 30[1](#footnote-1)), we warned we had reached "stage five of a four-stage disaster management response." Politicians were deluding themselves that there would be "plenty of time to adapt, course correct and eventually grow accustomed whatever the result is."

Today's [18-page Senate minority report](https://www.banking.senate.gov/imo/media/doc/final_dropping_the_baton_-_how_america_is_failing_to_use_russia_sanctions_and_export_controls_to_help_achieve_a_just_peace_in_ukraine_-_august_2025.pdf) proves that warning was prophecy, not commentary.

Trump promised to end Ukraine's war within 24 hours—53 times. By June, he'd retreated to "maybe we're better off letting them fight more." The man who campaigned on ending wars discovered that Instagram-level foreign policy doesn't deliver Instagram-speed results. He gave up without implementing a single sanction against Russia.

The administration's "appeasement strategy to end the war has only made it more deadly." Steven jets to Moscow for more talks while bodies pile up. They've innovated beyond traditional appeasement—creating a version that actively amplifies the violence it claims to stop. It's diplomatic malpractice disguised as peacemaking.

Day 30 warned of "a catastrophe of such magnitude that there is no long-term development scenario." The evidence: Russia now floats "air-truce offers" that explicitly don't end the war while Visa carves payment systems into monuments to national poets. We've transcended crisis management entirely.

This isn't foreign policy—it's performance art with a body count.

Trump's failure isn't just political embarrassment. It confirms American foreign policy has entered a post-strategic phase where promises substitute for planning and talking points replace actual policy. "The reputations of both Z and Donald are mud," we note below. When your signature promise collapses in six months, "mud" seems generous.

As Steven boards another Moscow flight and the Senate documents another leadership failure, one thing is clear: America's allies are running out of reasons to pretend otherwise.

Unidentifiable people, meanwhile, say Keith may visit Kyiv.
