This is so far beyond horror.

[Image]

The pre-dawn shriek of missiles overhead—our weekly Friday alarm clock, right on schedule[1](#footnote-1). You’re out on the balcony overlooking Chorna Hora, high enough that jumping is definitely not an option, watching the sky light up like the world’s worst fireworks show. The missiles scream past, all urgency and drama. Then come the Shahed UAVs with their distinctive lawnmower-from-hell whine, cheaper and slower but just as committed. We’ve learned to tell them apart now, which is either a useful life skill or the most depressing party trick imaginable. Another night in central Kyiv, the middle of Europe, watching Russian, Iranian and North Korean projectiles. You take a deep breath and hope Rheinmetal’s [Skynex](https://www.rheinmetall.com/en/products/air-defence/air-defence-systems/networked-air-defence-skynex) doesn’t run out of ammo.

[Image]

Kyiv has thousands of faces. And some of them we don’t even suspect exist. Behind the facades of 1,000-year old landmarks, the sound of roosters—yes, roosters—and the usual must-sees, there are hidden places you won’t find in guidebooks or read about in [The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/europe) or [Kyiv Independent](https://kyivindependent.com/).

Chorna Hora (Black Mountain) is the very center of the mystical-hilly Pechersk Triangle, which includes Lysa and Busova mountains.

The secrecy of this historic area is fully explained by its name. Lysa Hora (Bald Mountain) is shrouded in legends of people being bricked up alive, which were supported by the preserved pagan shrine with a statue of Perun, that is, until the Wickians burned it down in 2013. Busova Hora (Bus’s Mountain) is in the Hryshko Botanical Garden[2](#footnote-2). No one likes to talk about about Chorna Hora because its history is said to be much scarier. Anyway, that’s where I live, right on the blue x.

Yesterday evening I regaled the gentlemen at [Tootsie Club](https://uk.tootsie.kiev.ua/) with tales of 1,000 hours of surreptitiously recorded conversations documenting Z’s back office kickback emporium in the energy and defense sectors. Ukraine’s [National Anti-corruption Bureau](https://nabu.gov.ua/en/) (NABU) and Specialized Prosecutor’s Office began their Monday morning drip-feed of audio snippets, implicating ministers and deputy prime ministers in the sort of illicit activities that would make a Chicago alderman blush.

As search and detain warrants pinged into the computer system over the weekend, our two stars—Tymur Mindich and Oleksandr Tsukerman—received what one can only assume was a very courteous heads-up and promptly absconded. Tymur, being Z’s old Kvartal-95 comedy studio partner and bosom buddy, was yesterday subjected to the crushing penalty of a three-year sanction decree. I’m sure he’s weeping into his offshore accounts as we speak.

Sensing the walls closing in, Presidential Office Chief Andriy Yermak unveiled a Friday afternoon masterpiece: [a cock-and-bull testimonial](https://t.me/ermaka2022/7145) about a gang of scoundrels *impersonating* his brother Denys, roaming Kyiv offering plum government posts in exchange for bribes.

[Video: https://youtu.be/PUQL1OBFOE4]

Hours prior, the National Police helpfully arrested said “gang members” in what can only be described as dinner theater without the dinner. The production values of the skit above are undergraduate at best.

Meanwhile, former Defense Minister Rustem Umerov—currently cosplaying as National Security and Defense Council Secretary—discovered urgent, previously unknown business in Turkey and the Middle East. How fortuitous.

Nothing says “I’m definitely not fleeing potential arrest” quite like an impromptu Middle Eastern diplomatic safari.

> I have just arrived in Istanbul. Over the next few days, I’m working in Türkiye and the Middle East to unblock the exchange process. There was an agreement — and it must be implemented. The President of Ukraine set a clear task: Ukrainians must return home from captivity. In the coming days, there will be meetings, as well as in Türkiye. The focus is precisely on this — to resume the exchanges. — Rustem Umerov ([X,](https://x.com/rustem_umerov) November 11, 2025)

Prime Minister Yuliia Svyrydenko contributed her own bit of performance art yesterday: a video solemnly pledging to audit all state-owned enterprises—hundreds of them, mind you—for improprieties.

Roman Chervinsky’s observation that Kremlin rats have taken up residence on Bankova seems, shall we say, understated.
