Kim Barker has a lovely article w/ pictures about how miserable life is in Odesa this winter, especially for the elderly and disabled[1](#footnote-1).

Z in his Christmas Day pep talk said he and his team talked for an hour with Steven Witkoff and Jared Kushner about next steps and the timing of peace framework plans. Putin will def not agree to any framework that might restore Ukraine’s viability after the fighting stops. Talking continues irregardless of this fact.

Parties to the conflict are now discussing DMZs, zones of influence, ADBZs, free trade zones, etc., presidential elections, referendums, etc. For this there are at least two reasons: desperation and a complete absence of options. Some try to counteract these by crusting proposals in security guarantees, or by cramming them with prosperity roadmaps, or by floating them in demilitarized zones, but these solutions—like reckless bling bling or a bald man’s rug—do little more than shine light on the problem.

The latest 20-point plan differs from November’s 28-pointer in that it’s marginally less suicidal: 800,000 peacetime troops instead of 600,000, a freeze along current lines rather than withdrawal from unoccupied Donbas. The Kremlin wants complete withdrawal from all four oblasts, denazification, demilitarization that ensures Ukraine cannot defend itself, and international recognition of annexations. Unplug the smoke detector, because this gap isn’t closing.

Ukrainian society treats any compromise as absolute capitulation—the difference between Versailles and unconditional surrender becomes meaningless when you’re convinced the next bite comes regardless. However, there is a gap between “first stage” and “second stage” where things could go completely off the rails. But total disintegration requires time, which requires a deal, which requires Putin to accept something less than total subjugation. Check for logic: there isn’t any.

Meanwhile Ukrainian forces have liberated 182 square kilometers around Kupyansk since December 11, with Russian milbloggers now admitting their command lied about controlling eleven settlements when they actually held three. The military reality keeps not cooperating with anyone’s peace frameworks.
