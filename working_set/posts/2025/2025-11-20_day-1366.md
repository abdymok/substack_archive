> in connection with the anti-corruption investigation of the National Anti-corruption Bureau of Ukraine (NABU) and Specialized Anti-corruption Prosecutor’s Office (SAPO).
>
> Aware of the significant increase in the threat to the existence of the Ukrainian state as a result of the exposure of large-scale corruption transactions at the highest levels of state administration and the subsequent crisis of trust in key institutions of power, we, the people’s deputies of Ukraine - representatives of the faction of the political party Servant of the People, declare our political position:
>
> 1. There should be no “untouchables.”
>
> All, without exception, those involved in the anti-corruption investigation of the NABU and SAPO into the activities of the criminal group led by Tymur Mindich must be released immediately. If further investigation reveals new persons with power involved in the activities of this group, the strictest sanctions provided for by the legislation of Ukraine must also be applied to them.
>
> 2. Full support for the NABU and SAPO.
>
> All state authorities, without exception, must assist the NABU and the SAPO in completing the investigation and submitting the materials to the court.
>
> Any attempts to put pressure on anti-corruption bodies, discredit their work, or artificially limit their powers are unacceptable and contradict the national interests of Ukraine.
>
> 3. Coalition of National Resilience.
>
> In order to restore the trust of Ukrainian citizens and our international partners in the state, we call for the immediate start of negotiations between all pro-Ukrainian factions and groups in the Verkhovna Rada of Ukraine on the creation of a coalition of national resilience.
>
> We call on all parliamentary pro-Ukrainian forces to abandon narrow-party and personal priorities that may hinder such a union.
>
> 4. Government of National Resilience – without party quotas and backroom agreements.
>
> After the formation of the coalition of national resilience, it is necessary to begin negotiations between all factions and groups that will be part of it on the formation of the Government of National Resilience.
>
> Such a Government should be:
>
> • formed without party quotas, backroom agreements and “own people”,
>
> • composed of specialists with an impeccable reputation and proven management experience in relevant areas,
>
> • focused on fulfilling a clearly defined anti-crisis mandate: ensuring defense capability, economic stability, energy security and an uncompromising fight against high-ranking corruption.
>
> Members of such a Government should defend national, not narrow political or party interests, and not use their positions as a springboard for future political campaigns.
>
> 5. Constitutional balance of power and strengthening trust in the institution of the President.
>
> The power triangle: the President of Ukraine — the Verkhovna Rada of Ukraine — the Cabinet of Ministers of Ukraine — should return to the framework of the Constitution of Ukraine and be built on the principles of a parliamentary-presidential republic.
>
> It is important that the activities of the Office of the President of Ukraine be as transparent as possible, institutionally regulated and free from any informal influence of persons involved in corrupt practices.
>
> We are convinced that the President of Ukraine will use the results of this investigation and the clear request of society as an opportunity to cleanse and renew his inner circle, to strengthen trust in the institution of the Head of State both within the country and among our international partners.
>
> We appeal to Ukrainian civil society, Ukrainian media, as well as to our international friends and partners with an appeal to support this initiative as a necessary condition for continuing the joint struggle to preserve the independence and sovereignty of Ukraine.
>
> In discord - defeat.
>
> In unity, cleansing and justice - victory.
>
> Glory to Ukraine!

**Five Blind Mice and the Peace Plan Elephant**

Five Financial Times journalists—*five*—teamed up to produce 746 words on Russia and America’s “sweeping new proposal” to end the war in Ukraine[1](#footnote-1). Christopher Miller, Anastasia Stognei, Amy Mackinnon, Phil Georgiadis, and Jamie Smyth pooled their considerable talents to explain how Kirill Dmitriev, Putin’s sovereign wealth fund guy, helped draft a 28-point framework that would have Ukraine cede the Donbas, slash its military in half, ban foreign troops, abandon long-range weapons, recognize Russian as an official language, and presumably install a golden toilet in the Kremlin as a thank-you gift.

What these intrepid correspondents somehow failed to mention—perhaps it slipped through the cracks between paragraphs about oil price fluctuations and framework stages—is that while Steve Witkoff was hand-delivering this magnificent document, Russian cruise missiles were simultaneously pulverizing apartment blocks in Ternopil, killing dozens of civilians.

But why burden readers with such tedious details? After all, the FT team was clearly too busy explaining why this plan is “very comfortable for Putin” and “heavily tilted towards Russia” to waste column inches on the inconvenient reality that comfort, in Putin’s case, typically involves Russia’s genocidal war in Ukraine.

The article does note that “oil prices fell sharply on reports of the proposal”—because nothing says serious journalism quite like prioritizing Brent crude futures over the bodies being pulled from rubble. Kevin Book from ClearView Energy Partners gets quoted discussing “oversupply concerns.” One imagines the residents of those obliterated apartments would be thrilled to know their deaths coincided with such promising market movements.

Perhaps the FT is pioneering a new genre: peace process reporting where the actual war is treated as atmospheric background noise, like traffic outside a conference room window. The missiles? The civilians? Just speed bumps on the road to that sweet, sweet diplomatic “framework.”

Numerous attempts to contact the five-person FT dream team and ask why mass civilian casualties didn’t merit a single sentence have, so far, been unsuccessful.
