The above *analysis* by [Sam Kiley](https://www.cnn.com/profiles/sam-kiley), CNN Senior International Correspondent, is loopy for a number of reasons. For starters, he hasn’t dropped the “dissident” label. Secondly, most Russians could give a shit about what happens to their compatriots in Belgorod oblast. The same goes for Kursk, Bryansk and maybe Voronezh and Krasnodar (**not Krasnodor**).

While cross-border raids may boost morale for some in Kyiv, they are probably counterproductive because they fuel enmity towards Ukraine among Russians. They might have some military value, but no one is sure how much.

We gave Sam a score of minus 15 points on a scale of 1 to 10 for his first reportage on May 25[1](#footnote-1). This second effort is just as meh.

Alya Shandra at Euromaidan Press talks up Russian Volunteer Corps (RDK) leader Aleksandr Fortuna [here](https://euromaidanpress.com/2023/05/30/the-russian-volunteer-corps-fights-for-ukraine-and-to-dismantle-putins-regime/?fbclid=IwAR1zwV8l07lJuHqh79B1sXKrM9sI9XzIs_qsYxdjFEfQ6tcdU9p1t-lo2l8). The interview exposes what Kirill Rogov [describes](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bPzipHMZv-s&t=2150&ab_channel=%D0%9E%D1%81%D1%82%D1%80%D0%B0%D0%BD%D0%B5%D0%B8%D0%BC%D0%B8%D1%80%D0%B5) as the “ideological wishy-washiness” behind the group’s message.
