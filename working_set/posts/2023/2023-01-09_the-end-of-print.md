My position on those early web page prototypes called books is clear: I love books, books are good, books decorate a room nicely. The codex is a robust plug-and-play interface. Hell, I’ve been reading the fucking things for years. Girls who wonder *ou est la bibliotheque* make me go all googly.

[Image]

The above is a rare [cover](https://web.archive.org/web/20010305083541/http://www.cardigan.com/coverage/humour/vlp2.html) for a book that did not as yet exist, at least in English until the early 1980s and my first trip to Cologne. Version one – everybody seemed to think version one was rather good – was drubbed and kicked and held, noses pinched, at arm’s length by the sales force of [Kiepenheuer & Witsch](https://www.kiwi-verlag.de/) for being too mopey. Improved upon in later years.

I recently came in contact with this dreadful jacket for [Demian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demian). Almost made me not want to re-read it.

[Image]

I find myself veering away from the usual disjointed bushwa about visible language and maps of the mind and centers that cannot hold toward a reiteration of thoughts from an [article](https://web.archive.org/web/20010305083541/http://www.adobe.com/web/columns/zeldman/20000821) I read awhile ago, by that bright fellow [Zeldman](https://web.archive.org/web/20010305083541/http://www.zeldman.com/).

It would be interesting to establish the point in history – no doubt somewhere in the ad-soaked marketing-mad 1960s – when function lost its lustre, when writing became secondary to its presentation, when commercial artists became graphic designers and the cult of haughty ego took hold.
