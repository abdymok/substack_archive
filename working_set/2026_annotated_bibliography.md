*Working draft · v0.1 · compiled from the 2026 linked-reference dataset through 28 October 2026*

## Scope and an important archive limitation

The 2026 linked-reference CSV contains 1,132 link occurrences across 264 URL hostnames, dated 1 January–28 October 2026, spanning 218 distinct post-day labels. The corresponding `working_set/26_redact.md` file is currently empty (zero bytes). This draft is therefore based on the linked-reference dataset, not a complete reading of the 2026 post texts.

That distinction matters. The CSV records the links and their surrounding link labels, but does not reliably preserve the argument made in the post, whether a source is being endorsed or criticized, or the full context in which it appears. This is consequently a provisional source guide, not yet the equivalent of the 2023–25 annotated bibliographies. It should be reconciled against the recovered 2026 Markdown archive before being treated as final.

Selection is based on documentary value, recurrence, and relevance to major themes visible in the link record: Russia's war and attacks on Ukrainian cities and energy infrastructure; air defence and drone warfare; diplomacy and U.S. policy; corruption and defence procurement; and the changing security relationship between Ukraine and its partners. Raw host counts are not publisher counts or quality rankings. Publisher aliases and archive.ph copies require normalization.

## Editorial verification checklist

Before treating this bibliography as final:

- Restore or regenerate `working_set/26_redact.md`. The current file is empty, so the annotations cannot yet be checked against the full post text or the author's treatment of each source.
- Confirm the precise headline, byline, and publication date for every press item. The link label in the CSV is often the author's own phrase, not the source's title.
- Separate source publication dates from archive link dates, especially for 2025 sources revisited in 2026.
- For NABU/SAPO cases, distinguish the initial announcement, notices of suspicion, detention, indictment, trial, and judgment. Do not state allegations as established facts.
- For intelligence reporting, compare the New York Times and Guardian retrospective accounts and identify where their sourcing and conclusions differ.
- For the winter energy crisis, pair international reporting with Kyiv municipal, utility, and emergency-service records. A human-interest report is not a citywide outage dataset.
- For drone and missile developments, distinguish prototypes, tests, announced targets, demonstrated interceptions, and operational deployment.
- For the reported diesel arrangement, verify the legal and transactional details before treating it as an accomplished policy change.
- Normalize publisher aliases and archive.ph URLs before producing frequency comparisons.
- Once the full Markdown archive is restored, revisit the selection to identify sources that the posts use as objects of criticism rather than evidence.
