# Annotated bibliography: Ukraine and the war, 2025

*Working draft · v0.1 · compiled from the 2025 Substack archive and linked-reference dataset*

## Scope and method

The 2025 Markdown archive contains 280 posts. Its linked-reference CSV contains 1,341 link occurrences across 303 URL hostnames, before publisher normalization. This is a selective bibliography of sources that illuminate the war, Ukraine's military and political position, international support, diplomacy, accountability, and the information environment.

Selection considers recurrence, documentary value, relevance to major events, and the archive's engagement with the source. Link frequency is not a measure of quality or reliability. A source may be included because the post disputes it, examines its framing, or uses it as evidence of a public position. Publisher aliases such as nyt.com and nytimes.com are counted separately in the raw data and must be consolidated before comparative statistics are produced.

Publication date and archive link date are not interchangeable. Some entries are primary documents, some are reporting, and some are analysis or opinion. Where the source is a reported allegation or a contested reconstruction, the annotation says so.

## Primary documents, official positions, and accountability

### 1. President of Ukraine. “Joint Statement on the Outcomes of the Meeting of the Delegations of Ukraine and the United States.” 11 March 2025.
[President's Office](https://www.president.gov.ua/en/news/spilna-zayava-za-pidsumkami-zustrichi-delegacij-ukrayini-ta-96553)

The official record of the Jeddah meeting, including Ukraine's stated readiness to accept an immediate 30-day interim ceasefire, subject to Russian acceptance and implementation. This is a primary source for Kyiv's public position, not evidence that a ceasefire had been agreed by both sides. Linked on 14 March.

### 2. U.S. Department of State. “U.S. Security Cooperation with Ukraine.” January 2025.
[State Department fact sheet](https://www.state.gov/bureau-of-political-military-affairs/releases/2025/01/u-s-security-cooperation-with-ukraine)

An official summary of U.S. security assistance and cooperation. Useful as a baseline for the administration's stated programme and categories of support. It should be read against appropriations, delivery records, subsequent policy changes, and independent oversight rather than treated as a measure of equipment reaching Ukrainian units. Linked on 4 March.

### 3. U.S. Army Security Assistance Command. “Executing Sustained Logistics Support for the Defense of Ukraine.” 2024.
[U.S. Army article](https://www.army.mil/article/270898/executing_sustained_logistics_support_for_the_defense_of_ukraine)

A background document on the logistics architecture supporting Ukraine. Although published before 2025, it is useful when considering the practical systems behind military aid and the consequences of interruptions. Linked on 4 March 2025.

### 4. Security Service of Ukraine. Announcement and material on Operation Spiderweb. 1 June 2025.
[SBU Telegram announcement](https://t.me/SBUkr/14947)

A primary Ukrainian security-service source for the operation against Russian strategic aviation. The announcement is essential for establishing what the SBU claimed and how it presented the operation. Operational details and damage assessments should be corroborated independently; official claims are not automatically a complete battle-damage assessment. Linked on 2 June.

### 5. National Anti-Corruption Bureau of Ukraine (NABU). Public materials on investigations into corruption in Ukraine's energy sector. November 2025.
[NABU](https://nabu.gov.ua/en/)

The bureau's official publications are the primary record for the investigation later widely referred to as Operation Midas. Use the specific notices of suspicion, procedural announcements, and court records rather than relying on a general homepage. Allegations must remain allegations unless established by a court. The archive links NABU during the November investigation.

### 6. Ministry of Defence of Ukraine. Estimated Russian combat losses, daily report. 5 November 2025.
[Ministry of Defence](https://mod.gov.ua/en/news/the-estimated-combat-losses-of-russians-over-the-last-day-900-persons-398-ua-vs-and-24-artillery-systems)

An example of the ministry's daily estimated-loss reporting. It is a primary source for the figures Ukraine's military authorities publish, not independent verification of each loss. Any use of the figures should identify them as Ukrainian estimates and avoid presenting them as independently confirmed counts. Linked on 5 November.

## Operational analysis, military technology, and specialist research

### 7. Institute for the Study of War. “Russian Offensive Campaign Assessments.” 2025.
[ISW assessment archive](https://understandingwar.org/research/russia-ukraine/)

A recurring operational reference across the archive, including dated assessments during the fighting around Pokrovsk, Kupiansk, and other sectors. ISW provides explicit analytical judgments and a useful daily record, but readers should distinguish confirmed events, claims attributed to combatants or officials, and the institute's own interpretation. The archive links dated assessments throughout the year, including October, November, and December.

### 8. Center for Defence Strategies. “Combat Immunity.” 2025.
[Report (PDF)](https://defence.org.ua/wp-content/uploads/doslidzhennya/CDS-Combat-Immunity-eng.pdf)

A Ukrainian policy and legal analysis of combat immunity and the protection of servicemen. It addresses the legal and institutional environment in which commanders make decisions under wartime conditions. Useful as a policy proposal and legal argument, not as a statement of settled law in every circumstance. Linked on 27 January.

### 9. TWZ. “Electronic Warfare Lessons From Ukraine Informing Air Force Special Operations Future.” May 2025.
[Analysis](https://www.twz.com/news-features/electronic-warfare-lessons-from-war-in-ukraine-informing-air-force-special-operations-commands-future)

A specialist account of how the war is informing U.S. military thinking about electronic warfare and special operations. Useful for tracing the transfer of battlefield lessons beyond Ukraine. The article's claims about institutional adaptation should be distinguished from actual procurement, doctrine, and fielded capability. Linked on 6 May.

### 10. The Wall Street Journal. “A Never-Ending Supply of Drones Has Frozen the Front Lines in Ukraine.” July 2025.
[Report](https://www.wsj.com/world/europe/a-never-ending-supply-of-drones-has-frozen-the-front-lines-in-ukraine-ae29c581)

A report on the effect of pervasive drone coverage on movement and battlefield geometry. It is useful for the argument that tactical transparency and attrition can constrain manoeuvre, but the headline's broad claim should be tested against variation between sectors and changes in tactics. Linked on 14 July.

### 11. The New York Times. “As Drone Warfare Evolves, Pentagon Sees Its Own Vulnerabilities.” 10 July 2025.
[Report](https://www.nytimes.com/2025/07/10/us/politics/us-military-drone-technology.html)

A report on U.S. military concern that lessons from Ukraine expose weaknesses in American doctrine and equipment. It connects the war to debates over force design and procurement. Read as a report on institutional concerns, not as proof that the U.S. military has already resolved those vulnerabilities. Linked on 14 July.

### 12. The New York Times. “3 Years Ago It Was a Casting Agency. Now It Has $1 Billion in Drone Contracts.” 26 October 2025.
[Investigation](https://www.nytimes.com/2025/10/26/world/europe/ukraine-fire-point-drones-flamingo.html)

An investigation into Fire Point, its rapid growth, and the scale of its defence contracts. Important for the relationship between Ukraine's expanding defence industry, procurement, political access, and accountability. Treat reported claims and financial figures according to the evidence presented, and distinguish questions raised by the investigation from findings established by regulators or courts. Linked on 27 October.

### 13. The New York Times. “Inside the Ukrainian Drone Operation That Devastated Russia’s Bomber Fleet.” June 2025.
[Archived copy](https://archive.ph/0ISlM)

A detailed account of the planning and execution of Operation Spiderweb. It is a key secondary narrative to read alongside the SBU's own announcement and subsequent reporting. The operational story, claimed aircraft losses, and strategic consequences are separate questions; do not collapse them into a single unqualified success metric. Linked on 4 June.

## Reporting, investigations, and battlefield accounts

### 14. Le Monde. “‘Dozens’ of Ukraine soldiers deserted while training in France, says French army official.” 6 January 2025.
[Report](https://www.lemonde.fr/en/international/article/2025/01/06/dozens-of-ukraine-soldiers-deserted-while-training-in-france-says-french-army-official_6736765_4.html)

A report on desertions among Ukrainian personnel training in France. It belongs in the bibliography because manpower, training, and unauthorised absence recur in the archive. The reported figure concerns a particular training context and should not be generalized to the Ukrainian armed forces as a whole. Linked on 7 January.

### 15. Reuters. “Top Ukrainian defence official sacked amid infighting over procurement.” 28 January 2025.
[Report](https://www.reuters.com/world/europe/top-ukrainian-defence-official-sacked-amid-infighting-over-procurement-2025-01-28/)

A contemporaneous account of the dispute around the Defence Procurement Agency and the removal of its leadership. Important for understanding the institutional conflict over weapons procurement, transparency, and ministerial control. Read alongside statements from the agency, the Ministry of Defence, and Ukrainian civil-society monitors. Linked on 29 January.

### 16. The New York Times. “Standoff at Ukrainian Procurement Agency Threatens to Disrupt Weapons Supply.” 27 January 2025.
[Report](https://www.nytimes.com/2025/01/27/world/europe/ukraine-weapons-agency-standoff.html)

A detailed report on the procurement dispute and its potential effect on weapons supply. It provides an external account of a consequential institutional conflict, but its description of the agency and its governance should be checked against primary Ukrainian documents and the subsequent outcome. Linked on 28 January.

### 17. Associated Press. “Ukraine's poorly managed and controversial mobilization drive.” March 2025.
[Report](https://apnews.com/article/russia-ukraine-war-conscription-law-a8ccc48e6c15276c5b5035288ed016ee)

A report on the difficulties and controversies surrounding mobilisation. Useful alongside reporting on desertion, recruitment enforcement, and the shortage of personnel. Mobilisation policy, individual abuses, unauthorised absence, and the size of the manpower pool are related but distinct subjects. Linked on 3 March.

### 18. Reuters. Reporting on Russian attacks on Odesa and civilian targets. 20 March 2025.
[Report](https://www.reuters.com/world/europe/russian-drones-hit-civilian-targets-odesa-cause-injuries-officials-say-2025-03-20/)

A contemporaneous account of Russian drone attacks in Odesa and reported civilian harm. Useful as a dated incident record. For casualty figures and damage, retain attribution to the named local authorities and update against later official information. Linked on 21 March.

### 19. The New York Times. “Ukraine's Air Defenses Struggle as Russian Strikes Intensify.” May 2025.
[Le Monde report on the same pressure](https://www.lemonde.fr/international/article/2025/05/26/en-ukraine-la-defense-antiaerienne-peine-a-faire-face-a-l-intensification-des-frappes-russes_6608414_3210.html)

The archive links reporting on the growing strain on Ukrainian air defence as Russian attacks intensify. The Le Monde article is a useful European account of the problem. It should be read with attention to interceptor availability, the mix of incoming weapons, geographic coverage, and the difference between a system's technical capability and the resources available to operate it. Linked on 26 May.

### 20. The Guardian. “‘There is no life’: Kupiansk’s slow demise reflects the fate of cities on Ukraine’s frontline.” 26 October 2025.
[Report](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2025/oct/26/kupiansk-ukraine-war-frontline)

A field report from Kupiansk on the civilian consequences of the changing front line. Its value is in documenting lived conditions in a city under sustained threat. It is a field account, not a complete operational assessment of the Kupiansk sector. Linked on 27 October.

### 21. The New York Times. “Supporters of Ukraine Anti-Corruption Investigator Call His Jailing Absurd.” 12 September 2025.
[Report](https://www.nytimes.com/2025/09/12/world/europe/ukraine-corruption.html)

A report on the detention of an anti-corruption investigator and the public response. Important context for the July crisis over the independence of Ukraine's anti-corruption institutions and the later Operation Midas investigation. The identity, procedural status, and exact allegations should be checked against court and agency records. Linked again on 4 December.

### 22. The Guardian. “Ukraine’s energy sector faces wide-scale investigation over ‘kickback’ allegations.” 10 November 2025.
[Report](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2025/nov/10/ukraine-anti-corruption-agency-investigates-energy-sector)

A major report on the anti-corruption investigation in the energy sector, later associated with Operation Midas. It is useful as an account of the scandal's public emergence. The word “allegations” matters: distinguish investigative claims, notices of suspicion, charges, and judicial findings. Linked on 11 November.

### 23. The New York Times. Reporting on the fall of Pokrovsk and the fighting in the area. 13 November 2025.
[Report](https://www.nytimes.com/2025/11/13/world/europe/ukraine-russia-pokrovsk.html)

A field report on the battle for Pokrovsk, an important logistical and operational centre. Useful for documenting conditions and the pressure on Ukrainian forces in the sector. Keep the distinction between control of the city, positions on its approaches, and the wider operational consequences. Linked on 14 November.

### 24. The New York Times. “No Power, No Heat, No Water: Odesa’s Days of Hell Under Russian Fire.” 25 December 2025.
[Report](https://www.nytimes.com/2025/12/25/world/europe/ukraine-odesa.html)

A report on the civilian consequences of Russian attacks on Odesa's energy and utility infrastructure. Particularly relevant to the archive's attention to the interaction between attacks, electricity, heating, water, and everyday survival. Linked on 26 December.

## Diplomacy, negotiations, and the international response

### 25. The Associated Press. “Trump says he can end the Russia-Ukraine war in one day.” 8 January 2025.
[Report](https://apnews.com/article/trump-russia-ukraine-war-un-election-a78ecb843af452b8dda1d52d137ca893)

A record of Trump's repeated promise to end the war quickly. Useful as a baseline against which to measure the subsequent policy, negotiations, and public statements. It records a political claim, not a realistic timetable or a negotiated proposal. Linked on 8 January.

### 26. The Financial Times. “Donald Trump pushes back Ukraine war deadline in sign of support for Kyiv.” January 2025.
[Report](https://www.ft.com/content/989dc02e-2a13-4c21-8ad0-0b32e098c410)

A contemporaneous account of the incoming administration's changing public timetable. Useful for following the gap between campaign rhetoric and the practical diplomacy that followed. Linked on 10 January.

### 27. The New York Times. “Ukraine agrees minerals deal with US.” April 2025.
[Archive copy](https://archive.ph/N9QHB)

A report on the negotiations over a U.S.-Ukraine minerals agreement. The deal became a major symbol of the new administration's transactional approach to support for Ukraine. The report should be read alongside the signed agreement itself and official explanations from both governments. Linked on 26 February; the archive revisited the agreement in May.

### 28. The Kyiv Independent. “The full text of the US-Ukraine minerals agreement.” May 2025.
[Agreement text](https://kyivindependent.com/the-full-text-of-the-us-ukraine-minerals-agreement/)

A published text of the agreement, useful as a documentary source rather than relying on political summaries or headlines. The outlet is included here for access to the primary document; its editorial reporting should be assessed separately. Linked on 2 May.

### 29. The BBC. Reporting on the proposed ceasefire and the subsequent dispute over its terms. March 2025.
[BBC report](https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/cwyg28wd1k3o)

A contemporaneous account of the competing interpretations and implementation disputes surrounding the proposed limited ceasefire. Useful for reconstructing the sequence of announcements and claims. A proposed pause, an agreed framework, and an observed cessation of attacks are not the same thing. Linked on 20 March.

### 30. The Associated Press. “Things to know about the limited ceasefire between Russia and Ukraine brokered by the US.” March 2025.
[AP explainer](https://apnews.com/article/ukraine-russia-us-limited-ceasefire-4f1d4a835c52e8a37716ea21b32ccb0b)

A concise explainer of the limited ceasefire discussions and their unresolved terms. Useful as a contemporaneous summary, but the claims of each party should be compared with the actual text of any agreement and subsequent events. Linked on 27 March.

### 31. The New York Times. “Trump Cut Europe Out of Ukraine Talks. Here’s How Europe Pushed Back.” 27 November 2025.
[Report](https://www.nytimes.com/2025/11/27/world/europe/europe-ukraine-trump.html)

A report on European efforts to influence the emerging U.S.-Russia peace initiative and prevent decisions about Ukraine being made without Ukraine and its European partners. Useful for the diplomatic sequence and the competing positions. It is a report on negotiations in motion, not a final account of the proposed settlement. Linked on 28 November.

### 32. The Financial Times. “US and Russian officials draft new peace plan for Ukraine.” November 2025.
[Report](https://www.ft.com/content/23536bf6-c550-4896-931f-a33a1c2ad91a)

A consequential report on the emergence of a U.S.-Russia peace proposal. It should be read as reporting about a developing draft, not as an agreed text or a settled diplomatic outcome. Linked on 20 November.

### 33. Reuters. “Full text: Europe's counter-proposal to US-Ukraine peace plan.” 23 November 2025.
[Text of counter-proposal](https://www.reuters.com/business/finance/full-text-european-counter-proposal-us-ukraine-peace-plan-2025-11-23/)

A documentary source for the European counter-proposal. Its value is that readers can compare the text with the U.S.-Russia draft and Ukraine's stated position rather than relying solely on summaries. A counter-proposal is not an agreed settlement. Linked on 24 November.

### 34. Reuters. “EU leaders agree Ukraine financing for 2026–27, Belgium's approval key.” 18 December 2025.
[Report](https://www.reuters.com/world/europe/eu-leaders-agree-ukraine-financing-2026-27-belgiums-approval-key-2025-12-18/)

A report on the European Union's decision over financing for Ukraine in 2026–27. Important for the material basis of continued Ukrainian resistance and the politics of European support. The final legal and financial arrangements should be distinguished from the political announcement. Linked on 19 December.

## Editorial verification checklist

Before treating this bibliography as final:

- Verify exact titles, bylines, and publication dates against the original pages. Some archive link labels are commentary or paraphrases rather than headlines.
- Add direct links to signed agreements and official texts wherever the archive currently points to a news report or an archived copy.
- For Operation Spiderweb, keep the SBU's claims, independent reporting, verified aircraft losses, and strategic consequences in separate columns.
- For mobilisation and desertion, preserve the distinction between reported individual cases, unauthorised absence, criminal desertion, and national-level estimates.
- For the Defence Procurement Agency dispute, compare the reporting with agency and Ministry of Defence statements and subsequent institutional changes.
- For the July 2025 anti-corruption crisis, add the actual law, the presidential signing record, and the subsequent reversal or amendments. Do not reduce the episode to a single headline.
- For Operation Midas, distinguish NABU/SAPO procedural records, media reconstructions, notices of suspicion, indictments, and court findings.
- For diplomacy, preserve the sequence and versions of proposals. Do not treat a draft, a counter-proposal, a public statement, and an agreement as equivalent.
- Normalize publisher aliases (including nyt.com/nytimes.com) before generating source-frequency comparisons.
- Consider a companion bibliography for the year's non-war reading, cultural references, and personal sources.

*Working selection, not exhaustive. Inclusion indicates relevance to the archive, not agreement with a source's claims.*
